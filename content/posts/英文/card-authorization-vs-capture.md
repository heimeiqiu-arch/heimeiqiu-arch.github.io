# Card Authorization vs. Capture: What Is the Difference?

When you pay by card online, the payment may be approved immediately, but the merchant might not collect the funds at that exact moment. This happens because card payments can involve two separate stages: **authorization** and **capture**.

Understanding these stages helps explain why a transaction can appear as pending, why a merchant may charge a card later, and why an approved payment does not always mean the transaction is fully completed.

## What Is Card Authorization?

Card authorization is the process in which a merchant or payment processor requests approval for a transaction.

The request typically passes through the payment processor, acquiring bank, card network, and card issuer. The issuer evaluates the transaction using factors such as available funds or credit, card status, security checks, and risk controls.

If approved, the issuer generally reserves the requested amount or reduces the available balance or credit limit temporarily. The exact treatment depends on the issuer and card product.

**Authorization is not always the same as final collection.** It indicates that the transaction has been approved at that stage, subject to the payment process continuing successfully.

## What Is Card Capture?

Capture is the step in which the merchant submits an authorized transaction for settlement.

After capture, the transaction can proceed through clearing and settlement. The timing of these steps varies by payment provider, merchant, card network, and issuer.

Some payment systems combine authorization and capture into a single flow. Others let merchants authorize first and capture later.

For example, a hotel may authorize an estimated amount when you check in and capture the final amount when you check out. An online retailer may authorize a purchase when you place an order and capture the payment when the item ships.

## Authorization vs. Capture: Key Differences

| Feature | Authorization | Capture |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Requests approval for a transaction | Submits an approved transaction for collection |
| Typical timing | Before capture | After authorization |
| Effect on available balance | May reserve funds or credit | Moves the transaction toward settlement |
| Can it be delayed? | Yes, within applicable limits | Yes, if the payment flow permits it |
| Does it guarantee final settlement? | No | Capture still does not guarantee every later settlement step will succeed |

The precise sequence depends on the merchant's payment setup and the provider's rules.

## Why Would a Merchant Authorize a Payment First?

There are several practical reasons for separating authorization from capture.

### 1. The final amount is not yet known

Hotels, car rental companies, restaurants, and some service providers may need to verify that a card can support a payment before the final bill is available.

### 2. Goods have not shipped

A retailer may want to check that the card is valid and the transaction is approved before preparing an order. The merchant may capture the payment when the goods are ready to ship.

### 3. The service will be delivered later

Some bookings and service transactions involve a gap between the initial payment check and the actual delivery.

### 4. The merchant needs to confirm availability

An authorization can help a merchant assess whether a payment is likely to proceed before completing the order. However, authorization does not guarantee that inventory, a booking, or a service will ultimately be available.

## Why Is an Authorized Payment Still Pending?

A pending transaction often means the issuer has approved an authorization but has not yet received or completed the later processing steps.

Common reasons include:

- The merchant has not captured the authorization yet.
- The merchant captures transactions in batches.
- The transaction is a hotel, rental, fuel, or other preauthorization.
- The merchant is still processing an order.
- The issuer has not yet updated the transaction's final status.

A pending transaction does not automatically mean you have been charged twice or that the payment has failed.

If the transaction remains pending longer than expected, contact the merchant first to ask whether it has captured the payment or released the authorization. Your card issuer can explain how the pending amount affects your available balance.

## What Happens If the Merchant Never Captures the Payment?

If an authorization is not captured, it may expire or be reversed according to the issuer's and payment provider's rules.

The reserved amount may then become available again. The timing varies by transaction type, issuer, merchant, and card program, so there is no single release period that applies to every card payment.

A pending authorization disappearing from your transaction list does not necessarily mean the merchant has issued a refund. It may simply mean that the original authorization was released.

If the merchant later submits a valid transaction under the applicable rules, the final posting can depend on the payment arrangement and issuer processing.

## Can a Merchant Capture Less or More Than the Authorized Amount?

Some payment systems support partial capture, where the merchant collects less than the authorized amount. This can happen when the final bill is lower than the initial estimate or when an order is shipped in separate parts.

Whether multiple captures or an amount different from the authorization are permitted depends on the payment setup, card network rules, and merchant category.

If the final amount is higher than expected, the merchant may need an additional authorization or another permitted payment flow. Do not assume every merchant can freely capture any amount against an earlier authorization.

## What Should You Do If the Payment Status Looks Wrong?

If you see an approved or pending payment but the order status is unclear, follow these steps:

1. Check whether the transaction is marked as pending, posted, reversed, or completed.
2. Ask the merchant whether the payment has been captured.
3. Check whether the merchant's order confirmation reflects the same amount.
4. Avoid repeating the payment immediately if the first transaction may still be processing.
5. Contact your card issuer if the amount remains unavailable or the transaction appears inconsistent with the merchant's explanation.

Keep the transaction date, amount, merchant name, and any reference number available when contacting support.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does authorization mean the merchant has received the money?

No. Authorization generally means the issuer has approved the transaction at that stage. Capture, clearing, and settlement are separate processing steps.

### Why was my available balance reduced before the transaction was completed?

The issuer may have placed a hold on the authorized amount. The hold can affect your available balance even while the transaction is pending.

### Is a pending authorization the same as a refund?

No. A pending authorization may be released without a refund ever being issued. A refund normally relates to a transaction that has already been captured or posted, although processing details vary.

### Can I cancel a payment after authorization?

You can ask the merchant to cancel the order or void the authorization. Whether this is possible depends on the transaction's current status and the payment provider's process.

## Final Thoughts

Authorization and capture are related but different stages of card payment processing. Authorization checks whether a transaction can proceed, while capture submits an approved transaction for collection.

Knowing the difference makes it easier to understand pending payments, delayed charges, and temporary holds. If a transaction appears unusual, confirm its status with the merchant and card issuer before making another payment.

Unexpected equipment failures, urgent supplier orders, and last-minute business travel can make the normal purchasing process too slow. Cards can help a company respond, but unrestricted emergency spending creates financial and security risks.

## Define an emergency and approve it

A genuine emergency is an unexpected situation where delay could cause significant operational, financial, safety, or customer impact. Essential equipment repairs, urgent supplies, or a service needed to restore a business system may qualify. A routine purchase that was simply forgotten should not automatically bypass controls.

Establish rules in advance: eligible requesters, authorized approvers, maximum amounts, accepted payment methods, required documents, and how temporary permissions are removed. The requester should provide supplier, estimated cost, reason, and expected business impact. Record approval through a designated channel; if a phone call is unavoidable, document approver, time, amount, and reason afterward.

## Prepare payment access and verify suppliers

Identify employees who may need emergency access. Options may include a dedicated operational card, a controlled manager card, or a temporary limit adjustment, but confirm the provider supports the required controls. Shared cards need a log showing who used them and why.

Urgency should not remove supplier checks. Confirm the supplier is genuine, the amount is reasonable, and the goods or services are required. Use verified contact details rather than relying on an unexpected message with new payment instructions. Pause if the payment page or requested method differs from normal practice.

## Document and review

After payment, submit merchant, amount, date, currency, receipt, emergency reason, why normal approval was not possible, approver, and order reference. Restore temporary settings after the event. Review whether the purchase was genuinely urgent, whether the process was fast enough, and whether better maintenance or inventory planning could prevent recurrence.

When evaluating virtual or physical cards, compare funding speed, limits, supported merchants, replacement procedures, security controls, and dispute support. A virtual card may suit an online supplier; a physical card may be more practical in person. Neither guarantees acceptance. Verify provider terms before relying on a card for critical purchases.

Website accessibility scanners and monitoring tools may charge monthly or annually based on the number of pages, domains, or team members. A virtual card can help organize these software expenses when the vendor accepts it.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Estimate the right plan
Review how the provider counts pages, scans, domains, and monitored properties. Confirm whether overage charges apply and whether reports are retained after a subscription ends. If you manage several client sites, compare the account structure with your client contracts and reporting obligations.

## Keep recurring costs visible
Use a reusable card if the service renews and the card is supported. Record the website or client, account owner, renewal date, and expected spend. Check whether a plan change will create a prorated invoice or add-on charge. Keep accessibility reports and remediation work tracked separately from payment records.

## When payment fails
Check the card’s status, available balance or limit, billing details, and the vendor’s accepted payment methods. Confirm whether the invoice includes more domains or scans than expected before adjusting the limit. Use official support to resolve account access or billing problems.

## FAQ
**Can a virtual card pay for accessibility software?** Some vendors accept virtual cards; verify at checkout.

**Does paying for a scanner guarantee accessibility compliance?** No. Automated scans have limits and do not replace appropriate evaluation and remediation.

**Where can I explore card services?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and confirm the card’s permitted uses.

## Final thoughts
Track the software expense, but judge accessibility progress by testing and remediation—not the subscription alone.

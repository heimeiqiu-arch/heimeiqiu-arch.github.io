A merchant may record $10,000 in customer payments but receive a smaller bank deposit. This does not automatically mean money is missing. Providers may deduct processing fees, refunds, chargebacks, reserve contributions, and other adjustments before paying the merchant.

A payout is the amount transferred to the merchant under the provider's payout arrangement. Understanding its calculation helps businesses reconcile sales records and manage cash flow.

## What Is a Merchant Payout?

A merchant payout is a transfer from a payment provider, acquirer, or other intermediary to the merchant's nominated bank account or eligible destination.

It usually represents the net amount payable for a group of transactions after relevant fees, refunds, adjustments, reserves, and contractual deductions. It is not necessarily equal to the total value of customer purchases made that day.

## How Is a Payout Calculated?

A simplified formula is:

**Net payout = eligible captured payments − refunds − processing fees − chargebacks and adjustments − reserve deductions**

The actual calculation depends on the provider. Some items may be deducted in another period, reported separately, or handled through a separate debit.

For example, assume a provider includes these items in one payout calculation:

- Captured customer payments: $10,000
- Refunds: $300
- Processing fees: $250
- Reserve contribution: $500
- Other adjustments: $50
- Illustrative payout: $8,900

This assumes all listed items are included in the same calculation. Actual statements may use different reporting periods and accounting methods.

## Why Does a Payout Differ from Sales?

A sales report may show orders placed. A payment report may show authorized or captured transactions. A payout report shows the amount transferred after relevant adjustments.

Differences can arise because some orders were authorized but not captured, refunds occurred after the original sale, fees were deducted, disputes affected the balance, reserves were retained, transactions fell into different payout periods, or currency conversion changed the final amount.

Compare reports using transaction and payout references, not totals alone.

## Payout vs. Settlement

Settlement generally describes the process through which obligations between payment-system participants are fulfilled. Merchant payout describes the transfer to the merchant under its provider arrangement.

A transaction may pass through network settlement and provider accounting before the merchant receives funds. The timing and structure depend on the payment arrangement. See [What Is Card Payment Settlement?](https://heimeiqiu-arch.github.io/posts/what-is-card-payment-settlement/) for the broader process.

## What Affects Payout Timing?

Common factors include the provider's schedule, transaction type, business days, holidays, bank processing, risk reviews, reserves, and verification status.

A displayed payout date is not always the date funds become available in the merchant's bank account.

## How Should a Business Reconcile Payouts?

Collect transaction, fee, payout, and bank reports for compatible periods. Match each payout using its reference, amount, and date. Identify the transactions, refunds, fees, reserves, and adjustments included.

If the bank deposit differs from the expected amount, check whether the provider combined batches, split a payout, or recorded a separate adjustment. Document unresolved differences and contact the provider with the payout reference.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are payouts always daily?** No. Schedules depend on provider, agreement, country, and account status.

**Can a merchant receive multiple payouts in one day?** Some providers support this, but it depends on the service and agreement.

**Are fees always deducted before payout?** No. Some providers invoice certain charges separately.

**Why is a payout marked paid but not in the bank?** Bank processing, holidays, account details, or intermediary processing can affect arrival. Check the transfer reference and contact the provider if the expected timeframe has passed.

A merchant payout schedule defines when a payment provider transfers eligible funds from processed customer transactions to a merchant's designated bank account.

Rather than transferring every customer payment individually, providers commonly calculate eligible balances and send them according to a predefined schedule. The schedule may be daily, weekly, monthly, or based on a custom arrangement.

## Common Types of Merchant Payout Schedules

**Daily payouts** transfer eligible funds on each applicable processing day. This can help businesses with frequent expenses, but daily payouts may still be affected by cutoff times, holidays, minimum thresholds, and settlement requirements.

**Weekly payouts** group eligible funds into a regular weekly transfer. This can simplify accounting for some businesses, but merchants need to understand which day the provider calculates and initiates the transfer.

**Monthly payouts** use a longer funding cycle. This may suit contractual arrangements or platforms that calculate balances over a reporting period, but it can create a longer gap between sales and access to funds.

**Custom schedules** may allow eligible merchants to select a frequency or request transfers manually. Availability depends on the account agreement and provider.

## What Determines the Actual Payout Date?

A payout schedule is not always a simple calendar rule. Transaction eligibility, cutoff time, bank operating days, currency, minimum thresholds, and account status can all affect the final date.

Some funds may still be processing or subject to review when the scheduled payout date arrives.

## Payout Schedule vs. Settlement Period

These concepts are related but different. The settlement period concerns how long it takes transaction proceeds to progress through the payment system. The payout schedule describes when the provider transfers eligible funds to the merchant.

For example, funds may become eligible after a settlement period and then be transferred according to a weekly payout schedule. A merchant should not confuse the settlement period with the time required for the bank to credit the transfer.

## How Should Businesses Choose a Schedule?

Consider operating expenses, transaction volume, refund exposure, and available working capital. A business that pays suppliers every few days may prefer more frequent payouts if the provider offers them at a reasonable cost. A business with predictable monthly expenses may be comfortable with a less frequent schedule.

The goal is to match the schedule to actual financial obligations, not automatically choose the fastest option.

## What Should You Check in the Provider Agreement?

Confirm payout frequency, time zone, cutoff times, weekend and holiday rules, supported currencies, transfer fees, minimum payout amounts, and conditions that can delay or suspend payouts. Keep copies of the payout policy and account notifications.

## Final Thoughts

A merchant payout schedule helps businesses understand when eligible payment proceeds are expected to reach their bank accounts. The right schedule depends on operational needs, provider conditions, and cash-flow requirements.

After submitting a payment, customers need to know whether the transaction succeeded, what they purchased, and what happens next. A clear confirmation page reduces uncertainty, duplicate attempts, and unnecessary support requests. Its most important job is to display an accurate transaction status.

## Show the actual payment status
Distinguish successful, pending, unsuccessful, and uncertain transactions. Do not display “Payment successful” until the provider has returned a confirmed result. If payment is still being checked, say so and explain when the customer should check again.

## Provide an order reference
Display a unique order number customers can quote when contacting support. Use the same reference in the confirmation email or receipt where appropriate. Keep the relationship between the order and payment records reliable, but do not expose internal credentials or sensitive system details.

## Show amount and currency
Display the transaction amount and currency clearly, along with the purchase date where useful. If the transaction currency differs from the merchant's reporting currency or cardholder's account currency, retain conversion details for reconciliation. Do not imply every card statement will show the same converted amount.

## Summarize the purchase
Show the product or service, quantity, selected plan, discounts, and final total. For digital purchases, explain how to access the content. For physical orders, provide fulfillment information or a tracking link when available.

## Explain what happens next
Tell customers whether a receipt will arrive by email, a download is ready, an order is being prepared, or booking details will follow. Instructions must match the actual fulfillment process.

## Provide receipt and invoice information
Where supported, offer a receipt or invoice containing the merchant name, order reference, purchase date, items, amount, currency, and applicable tax details. A payment receipt and a tax invoice are not necessarily the same document; follow requirements in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Make support accessible
Provide a support email, help center, or order-specific contact route. Explain what reference information customers should include. Never ask them to send full card numbers, security codes, or one-time passwords through chat or email.

## Handle pending and uncertain transactions safely
If payment is still processing, tell customers not to submit another payment until the status is checked. A timeout does not necessarily mean the transaction failed. Verify the provider's status before allowing a duplicate attempt or marking the order unpaid.

## Protect sensitive information and test mobile display
Show only appropriate payment details, such as card brand and masked last digits where supported. Never display full card credentials. Make the order reference easy to copy and ensure the status, amount, and next steps are readable on a phone.

## Final thoughts
A good confirmation page provides an accurate status, clear order details, useful receipts, and accessible support. It also helps connect customer orders with payment records.

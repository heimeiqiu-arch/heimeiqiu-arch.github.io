A merchant may challenge a chargeback by submitting transaction records and supporting evidence. In some card-network dispute frameworks, this response stage is referred to as **second presentment**. In the dispute context, it describes a response to a previously raised chargeback, not an ordinary transaction-clearing step.

## What is second presentment?

Second presentment is a dispute-processing stage in which the acquiring side submits a response to a chargeback, typically with supporting information from the merchant. It is commonly associated with the merchant's representment process.

The purpose is to challenge the chargeback under applicable network rules and explain why the disputed transaction should be reconsidered. Terminology and procedures vary across networks and may change; merchants should follow the instructions used by their acquirer.

## How does it work?

1. **A chargeback is raised.** The issuer submits a chargeback under the relevant dispute category and the acquiring provider notifies the merchant.
2. **The merchant reviews the dispute.** The merchant examines the reason, transaction history, customer communications, and supporting records.
3. **Evidence is prepared.** Documents may include fulfilment records, receipts, authentication data, customer correspondence, and refund records.
4. **The acquirer submits the response.** The acquiring side sends the response through the network process. This may be called second presentment in certain frameworks.
5. **The issuer reviews the response.** The issuer evaluates the evidence under applicable rules. The dispute may be resolved or proceed to another stage.

## What evidence should be included?

Evidence should match the dispute category. A non-delivery claim may require delivery confirmation or digital service-access records. A duplicate-processing claim may require transaction records showing the separate charges and explaining why they are not duplicates. An unrecognised-transaction claim may call for relevant authentication and customer-interaction records, subject to applicable rules.

A clear written explanation should connect the documents to the allegation.

## Why deadlines matter

Dispute processes operate within defined timeframes. The merchant may have a limited period to provide information to its acquirer, and the acquirer may have an earlier operational deadline for submitting the response.

The relevant deadline depends on the network, dispute category, notification date, and provider procedures. Use the deadline displayed by the payment provider rather than relying on a generic number of days.

## Second presentment vs. first presentment

First presentment is the initial submission of a transaction into clearing. Second presentment, in the chargeback context, refers to a response to a chargeback. Despite similar names, they describe different activities.

## What happens next?

The dispute may be resolved, remain unresolved, or progress to another available stage. Depending on the framework, further steps may include pre-arbitration or arbitration. Not every dispute is eligible for every stage, and escalation should be considered carefully.

## Common mistakes

Merchants may submit evidence that does not address the dispute reason, miss a deadline, omit important transaction context, or assume the same response procedure applies to every network. A good response is accurate, focused, and submitted through the correct channel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is second presentment the same as representment?

The terms are closely related in common card-dispute usage. The precise terminology and formal procedure depend on the network framework.

### Does it guarantee that the chargeback will be reversed?

No. The issuer evaluates the response under applicable rules and evidence requirements.

### Can every chargeback proceed to second presentment?

Available response rights depend on the network, dispute category, and procedural conditions.

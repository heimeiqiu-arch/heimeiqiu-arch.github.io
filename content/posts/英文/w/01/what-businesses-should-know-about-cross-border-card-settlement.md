A customer may complete an international card payment successfully, but the merchant does not necessarily receive the money immediately. The transaction can pass through authorization, clearing, settlement, currency conversion, and payout processes. Understanding these stages helps businesses forecast cash flow and reconcile international sales.

## What cross-border settlement means

Cross-border card settlement is the process through which funds associated with card transactions are transferred between the parties involved across countries or currency arrangements. A customer in one country may pay a merchant in another. The transaction can involve the issuer, card network, acquirer or payment processor, and the merchant's receiving account. The exact structure depends on the provider, card product, countries, and merchant agreement.

## Authorization is not settlement

Authorization is the issuer's decision to approve or decline a payment request. An approval may reserve funds or reduce the cardholder's available balance. Clearing is the exchange and processing of transaction information and financial obligations. Settlement is the fulfillment of those obligations between the relevant parties. Payout is the transfer of funds to the merchant's designated account under the provider's schedule.

A successful authorization does not mean that the merchant has already received settled funds. Some transactions may be adjusted, reversed, refunded, or delayed before payout.

## Understand the amounts in a settlement report

A merchant should distinguish the gross transaction amount from refunds, reversals, processing fees, conversion adjustments, and the net amount paid out. For example, a batch of EUR 1,000 in sales with EUR 100 in refunds and EUR 25 in fees would produce EUR 875 before any other adjustments in this simplified example. Actual reports may group fees and adjustments differently.

Keep transaction-level references so that a payout can be traced back to the underlying customer payments. A single bank deposit may represent many transactions rather than one sale.

## What affects timing?

There is no universal settlement time for every cross-border transaction. Timing may depend on the acquiring provider, merchant agreement, country, currency, transaction type, weekends and public holidays, risk review, and banking arrangements. A provider's standard schedule may not cover exceptional cases. Review the merchant agreement and provider documentation rather than relying on a generic claim about how many days international payments take.

## Reconcile the complete payment path

For each transaction, retain the payment reference, original amount and currency, authorization date, settlement reference, converted amount, settlement currency, fees, refunds, payout date, and receiving account. Reconcile provider reports with bank deposits and accounting entries. If a payout is delayed, identify whether the transaction is still pending, included in a later batch, under review, or adjusted.

## Questions to ask a provider

Before processing significant international volume, ask which transaction and settlement currencies are supported, how exchange rates are determined, what fees are deducted, how refunds appear in reports, how missing payouts are investigated, and whether country-specific restrictions apply. Obtain the relevant terms in writing and ensure finance staff know where to find the reports.

## Plan for delays

Do not treat every authorized payment as immediately available cash. Forecast expected payouts separately from completed deposits, maintain an operating reserve appropriate to the business, monitor outstanding batches, and keep provider support details accessible. This is particularly important when supplier payments depend on incoming card revenue.

## Final thoughts

Cross-border settlement involves more than approval at checkout. Businesses need to understand clearing, settlement, payout, conversion, fees, and reconciliation. A clear process makes missing funds easier to investigate and cash flow easier to manage.

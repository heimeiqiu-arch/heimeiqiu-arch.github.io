A merchant may believe that a card transaction was legitimate, but that belief alone is not enough to resolve a chargeback. The merchant needs records that support its position and address the cardholder's allegation. In payment disputes, this is often described as **compelling evidence**.

The phrase is especially relevant in fraud-related disputes, but the broader principle applies to many chargebacks: evidence should be relevant, reliable, and consistent with the applicable network requirements.

## What is compelling evidence?

Compelling evidence is documentation or transaction information that meaningfully supports a merchant's position. It may help establish what happened, who participated in the transaction, whether a product or service was provided, or whether the merchant followed relevant procedures.

The exact evidence considered depends on the dispute category and applicable network rules. No single document automatically wins every chargeback.

## Examples of evidence

### Order and transaction records

Keep the order confirmation, invoice or receipt, transaction amount and date, merchant order reference, product or service description, and relevant checkout information. These records connect the disputed payment to the customer's order.

### Delivery and fulfilment records

For physical goods, relevant records may include shipping information, carrier tracking, delivery confirmation, and fulfilment timestamps. For digital services, evidence may include account activation, download records, service-access logs, or records showing that the service was made available.

### Authentication and security information

Where applicable and permitted, 3-D Secure information, authentication results, and other transaction security data may help explain how a payment was verified. Interpret these records according to the applicable rules; authentication does not automatically resolve every dispute category.

### Customer communications

Emails, support tickets, cancellation requests, and other communications can establish what the customer requested and how the merchant responded. Preserve timestamps and context. A cropped screenshot may be less useful than a complete, readable record.

### Refund and cancellation records

For a refund-related dispute, retain cancellation timestamps, refund submission records, references, status information, and customer notifications. Distinguish between a refund being requested, submitted, and completed.

## Match evidence to the dispute reason

Relevance is the most important principle.

- A non-delivery claim calls for delivery or service-fulfilment evidence.
- A duplicate-processing claim calls for transaction records showing whether more than one charge occurred.
- An unrecognised-transaction claim may require relevant authentication, account activity, and customer-interaction records.
- A refund-related claim calls for records showing the refund process and status.

Submitting the same generic documents for every dispute is unlikely to be effective.

## What makes evidence reliable?

**Authentic:** It comes from a reliable business system or original source.

**Consistent:** Dates, amounts, order identifiers, and customer information do not contradict one another.

**Relevant:** It directly addresses the allegation.

**Readable:** The reviewer can understand it without guessing.

**Complete:** Important context has not been removed in a misleading way.

Merchants should also respect privacy, data-protection requirements, and the evidence rules of their provider.

## Organise evidence before a dispute arrives

Create a transaction-specific record containing the original order, payment details, fulfilment information, customer communications, relevant security information, and refund or cancellation records. Then prepare a short explanation connecting each document to the allegation.

The goal is not to overwhelm the reviewer. It is to make the relevant facts easy to verify.

## Common evidence problems

Screenshots without timestamps or account identifiers can be difficult to verify. Internal notes without supporting source records may carry limited weight. Documents relating to different transactions, or inconsistent amounts and dates, can undermine an otherwise valid response.

Before submission, confirm that all records refer to the same transaction and tell a consistent story.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 3-D Secure evidence always enough to win a fraud dispute?

No. Its effect depends on the transaction, applicable network rules, and dispute category.

### Can customer emails be used as evidence?

They may be relevant when they establish order details, delivery, cancellation, customer acknowledgement, or other facts. Provider evidence rules still apply.

### Is more evidence always better?

No. A focused set of relevant records is often easier to evaluate than a large collection of unrelated files.

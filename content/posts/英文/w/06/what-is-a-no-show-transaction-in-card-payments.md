# What Is a No-Show Transaction in Card Payments?

A customer books a hotel room or another guaranteed reservation but does not arrive.

Depending on the booking terms, the merchant may be entitled to charge a cancellation penalty or no-show fee.

In certain card-payment frameworks, this type of transaction is known as a **no-show transaction**.

However, a merchant cannot simply charge any customer who misses an appointment. The transaction must meet the applicable reservation, customer-agreement, and payment-network requirements.

## What Is a No-Show Transaction?

A no-show transaction is a card payment associated with a guaranteed reservation that the customer does not use, where the merchant is permitted to collect an agreed no-show penalty.

It is particularly relevant to certain lodging, travel, and reservation-based businesses.

The merchant must follow the applicable card-network rules and ensure that the customer was properly informed of the relevant terms.

## How Does It Work?

A simplified process may look like this:

1. The customer makes a reservation.
2. The merchant provides the cancellation and no-show terms.
3. The customer agrees to the reservation conditions.
4. The customer does not arrive or cancel within the permitted period.
5. The merchant verifies that the no-show conditions have been met.
6. The merchant submits the eligible charge using the required transaction information.

The exact process depends on the network, merchant category, and transaction arrangement.

## Why Are Reservation Terms Important?

A merchant should clearly explain:

- The reservation date and time.
- The cancellation deadline.
- Whether a deposit or guarantee is required.
- The amount or calculation of a no-show fee.
- Any exceptions or grace periods.
- How the customer can cancel or modify the booking.

If these terms are unclear, the merchant may face disputes when attempting to collect a penalty.

## No-Show Transaction vs. Ordinary Purchase

An ordinary purchase usually involves the customer receiving goods or services at the time of payment.

A no-show transaction relates to a reservation that was not used, where an agreed penalty may apply.

The transaction should be identified and processed according to the appropriate network requirements rather than submitted as an ordinary completed service purchase without the relevant context.

## No-Show Transaction vs. Delayed Charge

A delayed charge is a supplemental payment processed after an earlier service or transaction under an eligible framework.

A no-show transaction is specifically associated with a missed guaranteed reservation and an applicable penalty.

Both may involve later card processing, but their purposes and requirements differ.

## What Evidence Should Merchants Keep?

A merchant may need to retain:

- Original reservation confirmation.
- Terms accepted by the customer.
- Cancellation policy.
- Relevant customer communications.
- Reservation date and time.
- Records showing that the customer did not arrive.
- Calculation of the applicable penalty.
- Payment processing details.

The merchant should retain records in accordance with privacy, legal, and network requirements.

## Common Mistakes

### Unclear Cancellation Terms

Customers may dispute fees if the cancellation deadline or penalty was not clearly presented.

### Charging the Wrong Amount

The amount should match the agreed and permitted fee calculation.

### Incorrect Transaction Classification

Using an ordinary transaction flow when a specific no-show process applies can create processing and dispute problems.

### Missing Reservation Records

Without a clear booking history, the merchant may struggle to demonstrate why the charge was submitted.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a hotel charge a customer who does not arrive?

It may be permitted where the reservation was guaranteed, the customer agreed to the relevant terms, and applicable network and legal requirements are met.

### Is a no-show fee always the full reservation amount?

Not necessarily. The amount depends on the agreed cancellation policy and applicable rules.

### Can customers dispute a no-show charge?

Yes. Customers may question a charge if they believe the reservation terms were not followed or the amount was incorrect.

No-show transactions are a specialised payment scenario. Clear reservation terms, accurate records, and correct transaction handling are essential for both merchants and customers.

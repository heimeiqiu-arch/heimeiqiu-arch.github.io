A card balance may fall by more than the final purchase price because a merchant placed an authorization hold. This is a temporary reservation of funds or spending capacity that lets a merchant check whether a card can cover an expected payment before the transaction is finalized. Holds are common in hotels, car rentals, fuel purchases, and some online services.

## How authorization works

The merchant or processor sends an authorization request through the payment network and issuer. The issuer checks card status, available funds or credit, spending controls, and risk. If approved, the authorization may reduce available capacity while the merchant completes the payment. The merchant may later submit the final amount, reverse the authorization, or allow it to expire under applicable rules.

## Why the hold differs from the final amount

A hotel may authorize an estimated amount for incidentals, a rental company may place a hold before the vehicle is returned, or a fuel station may request an initial amount before the final total is known. A $500 hotel authorization followed by a $420 final bill does not automatically mean the cardholder paid both amounts.

If a card has $1,000 available and a merchant places a $300 hold, only $700 may remain available for new payments. This can cause another transaction to be declined. Pending authorizations and completed charges are different stages. If both appear, check whether the pending entry is expected to disappear or be replaced by the settled transaction before assuming a duplicate.

## What to do about a lingering hold

A hold may remain because the merchant has not reversed it, the issuer display has not updated, or processing crosses business days or time zones. Release timing depends on merchant, transaction type, network rules, and issuer; there is no universal period. Ask the merchant whether the transaction was completed or reversed, then contact the issuer if the hold remains unresolved. Avoid repeated payment attempts because they can create additional authorizations.

Businesses should explain pre-authorizations before employee travel, allow capacity for expected holds, and review pending items separately from settled expenses. Keep receipts and booking confirmations for reconciliation.

Some card disputes cannot be resolved through earlier response stages. When the issuer and acquiring side continue to disagree, the case may qualify for **chargeback arbitration** under the relevant card-network rules.

Arbitration is a formal dispute-resolution stage in which the card network may review the case and determine how the dispute should be resolved. It is generally more involved than an ordinary chargeback response and can involve additional costs.

## What is chargeback arbitration?

Chargeback arbitration is a formal process used to resolve certain unresolved disputes between the issuing and acquiring sides of a card transaction. The relevant network reviews the case under its rules and procedures.

The network's role is to determine the outcome within its dispute framework. This is not the same as a court proceeding or a general consumer complaint process. Procedures differ across networks.

## Why does arbitration happen?

A dispute may reach arbitration when the issuer and acquirer cannot agree after earlier stages. For example, a merchant may provide evidence supporting delivery, while the issuer maintains that the evidence does not satisfy the applicable requirements.

If the dispute remains eligible and the relevant party chooses to proceed, arbitration may be requested.

## How does arbitration work?

1. **Earlier stages take place.** The initial chargeback and applicable response stages are completed.
2. **The remaining disagreement is reviewed.** The issuer and acquirer examine the unresolved issue and determine whether arbitration is available.
3. **A request is submitted.** The relevant party submits the case through the network's prescribed process.
4. **The network reviews the case.** The network evaluates the submitted information under its rules.
5. **A decision is issued.** The network determines the outcome within the arbitration framework.

Financial consequences and fee allocation depend on applicable rules and agreements.

## What information may be reviewed?

The case may include original transaction information, the chargeback reason and supporting details, merchant representment evidence, subsequent responses, relevant communications, procedural records, and other documentation permitted by the network.

The review is based on applicable rules, not simply on which party submits more pages of evidence.

## What costs may be involved?

Arbitration can involve network fees and additional administrative costs. Amounts, allocation, and consequences vary by network and provider agreement.

A merchant should ask its acquirer for the current fee schedule and understand whether it is responsible for costs associated with escalation.

## Should a merchant proceed?

Not automatically. Evaluate the strength of the evidence, applicable rules, amount in dispute, potential fees, and likelihood of a favourable result. In some cases, accepting the chargeback may be more commercially reasonable than continuing.

## Arbitration vs. pre-arbitration

Pre-arbitration may allow parties to address an unresolved disagreement before a formal arbitration request. Arbitration is the later process in which the network reviews an eligible case and makes a determination under its rules. The sequence depends on the network and dispute category.

## How can merchants prepare?

Maintain complete transaction records from the beginning: the original order, payment authorization and capture information, fulfilment records, customer communications, refund history, and earlier dispute submissions.

Before escalation, confirm that the case is eligible and all procedural requirements have been met. A well-organised case file makes the dispute easier to evaluate and reduces administrative mistakes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is arbitration the same as going to court?

No. It is a card-network dispute-resolution process governed by network rules and procedures.

### Does arbitration always favour the cardholder?

No. The outcome depends on the evidence and applicable rules.

### Can fees exceed the disputed amount?

Depending on the network and provider fee structure, escalation costs can be significant relative to the amount in dispute. Check current terms before proceeding.

Chargeback arbitration is a formal escalation option, not a routine step for every dispute. Businesses should use it selectively and understand the evidence, rules, and potential costs involved. Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) for current card product information.

# What Is a Delayed Charge in Card Payments?

A customer checks out of a hotel and later sees an additional charge for an expense discovered after departure.

A rental customer returns a vehicle and later receives a charge for an eligible additional cost.

These situations may involve a payment process known as a **delayed charge**.

Delayed charges are used in certain merchant categories and business scenarios, but they are not a general permission to charge a stored card whenever a merchant identifies a new amount.

## What Is a Delayed Charge?

A delayed charge is an additional card transaction processed after the original service or transaction has taken place, under an applicable agreement and payment-network framework.

It may be used for certain supplemental charges associated with an earlier transaction.

Examples can include eligible additional expenses in lodging, vehicle rental, cruise, and related merchant environments.

The exact requirements depend on the network, merchant category, transaction type, and stored-credential rules.

## Why Do Delayed Charges Occur?

### Hotel and Accommodation Expenses

A guest may incur an additional eligible expense that is identified after checkout.

The hotel may process a separate charge where the original agreement and applicable rules permit it.

### Vehicle Rental Costs

A rental company may identify an additional contractual charge after the vehicle has been returned.

The merchant must have a valid basis for the charge and follow the applicable payment procedures.

### Cruise and Travel Services

Some travel-related businesses may process eligible supplemental charges after the original service has been provided.

The specific permitted use cases are governed by network and industry rules.

## How Does a Delayed Charge Work?

A simplified process is:

1. The customer makes the original purchase or uses the service.
2. The customer agrees to the relevant terms and payment arrangement.
3. The original transaction is processed.
4. An eligible additional amount is identified later.
5. The merchant checks that the additional charge is permitted.
6. The merchant submits the delayed charge using the appropriate transaction information and indicators.
7. The issuer evaluates the new payment request.

The transaction may be processed as a separate payment event rather than simply changing the original settled transaction.

## Delayed Charge vs. Recurring Payment

These are different transaction types.

A recurring payment is part of an agreed schedule, such as a monthly subscription or periodic service fee.

A delayed charge is an additional charge associated with an earlier service or transaction and is processed later under applicable rules.

For example, a monthly software subscription is recurring billing. An eligible extra expense identified after a hotel stay may be a delayed charge.

## Delayed Charge vs. Incremental Authorization

An incremental authorization adjusts or increases the amount reserved under an existing authorization where supported.

A delayed charge is a separate supplemental transaction processed after the original service under an eligible framework.

The merchant should use the correct transaction type and indicators rather than selecting whichever process is technically easier.

## What Should Merchants Consider?

Merchants should ensure that:

- The customer agreement clearly covers the additional charge.
- The charge is permitted for the merchant category and transaction type.
- The amount can be supported by records.
- The correct stored-credential and transaction indicators are used.
- The transaction is submitted within applicable requirements.
- Customer communication and dispute records are retained.

A merchant should not assume that holding a customer's card details gives it unrestricted authority to make future charges.

## What Can Customers Do if They Do Not Recognise a Delayed Charge?

Customers should first review the original purchase agreement, receipts, and merchant communication.

If the charge remains unclear, they can contact the merchant and, where appropriate, their card issuer.

Merchants should maintain enough documentation to explain the relationship between the original service and the supplemental amount.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a delayed charge the same as a subscription renewal?

No. A delayed charge is a supplemental transaction related to an earlier service, while a recurring payment follows an agreed billing schedule.

### Can any merchant submit delayed charges?

No. Eligibility depends on network rules, merchant category, transaction type, and provider configuration.

### Does a delayed charge require a new authorization?

The required processing method depends on the network and transaction framework. Merchants should follow their acquirer's instructions.

Delayed charges can support legitimate supplemental billing, particularly in certain travel and hospitality environments, but they require a clear customer agreement and correct transaction processing.

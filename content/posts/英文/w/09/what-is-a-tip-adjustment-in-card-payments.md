# What Is a Tip Adjustment in Card Payments?

A customer pays at a restaurant and adds a tip after the initial card authorization.

The final amount processed may therefore be higher than the amount initially approved.

This is commonly handled through a **tip adjustment** or another permitted authorization-adjustment process.

The exact procedure depends on the payment network, merchant category, terminal configuration, and acquiring provider.

## What Is a Tip Adjustment?

A tip adjustment is a change to the payment amount to account for a gratuity added after the initial transaction authorization.

It is commonly associated with restaurant and hospitality payments where the tip is entered after the customer has presented the card or completed the initial payment step.

For example, a restaurant bill may be $60, while the customer adds a $12 tip.

The final amount is $72, subject to the applicable payment rules and processing method.

## How Does It Work?

A simplified process may look like this:

1. The restaurant calculates the original bill.
2. The cardholder presents the card or completes the initial payment step.
3. The merchant requests authorization for the transaction amount.
4. The customer adds a tip.
5. The merchant submits the final amount using the supported adjustment or capture procedure.
6. The payment is processed according to the applicable network and acquirer rules.

Not every transaction type or card-processing environment supports the same adjustment method.

## Why Can the Final Amount Be Different?

The original authorization may cover only the bill before gratuity is added.

When the tip is included, the final amount may change.

This is one reason a pending authorization and the final posted card transaction can show different amounts.

The merchant must ensure that the final amount is consistent with the customer's instructions and the applicable processing rules.

## Tip Adjustment vs. Incremental Authorization

An incremental authorization is a broader payment operation that can increase an existing authorization amount where supported.

A tip adjustment is a specific business use case involving gratuity.

Some payment systems may process a tip through an authorization adjustment, while others use a different supported transaction flow.

The terms should not be treated as universally interchangeable.

## What If the Tip Is Entered Incorrectly?

Merchants should have procedures to correct mistakes before the transaction is finalised.

If the incorrect amount has already been processed, the appropriate correction may involve a reversal, refund, or another permitted adjustment depending on the stage of the payment.

The merchant should retain records showing the original bill, customer-entered tip, and final amount.

## What Should Merchants Consider?

### Confirm the Customer's Tip

The tip should reflect the amount entered or otherwise authorised by the customer.

### Use the Correct Transaction Flow

The merchant's terminal and processor must support the applicable tip-adjustment method.

### Follow Network Limits

Some networks and merchant categories have specific requirements for how the final amount may differ from the initial authorization.

### Keep Accurate Records

The original amount, tip amount, final total, and transaction reference should be traceable.

## Why Might a Cardholder See Two Different Amounts?

A pending transaction may show the initial authorization, while the final posted transaction includes the tip.

The difference does not automatically mean that the merchant charged the card twice.

If the final amount appears incorrect, the customer should review the receipt and contact the merchant for clarification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a tip adjustment the same as a second payment?

Not necessarily. It may be processed as an adjustment to the original transaction under the supported payment flow.

### Can a restaurant increase the final amount without the customer's consent?

The merchant must follow applicable law, network rules, and the customer's instructions. A tip should not be added arbitrarily.

### Does every card terminal support tip adjustment?

No. Support depends on the terminal, processor, acquiring setup, merchant category, and payment method.

Tip adjustments are a common example of why the initial authorization amount and final card transaction amount may differ. Merchants should use the correct processing method and keep clear records of the customer's tip instruction.

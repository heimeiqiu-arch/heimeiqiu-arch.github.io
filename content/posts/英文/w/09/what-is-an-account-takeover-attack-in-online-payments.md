An account takeover attack occurs when an unauthorised person gains control of someone else's online account. In payment environments, this can allow criminals to access saved payment methods, view personal information, change account settings or make transactions without permission.

Account takeover fraud affects more than banks. It can target shopping accounts, subscription platforms, digital wallets, payment services and business software. The consequences depend on what the compromised account can access.

## How account takeover happens

**Credential stuffing:** Criminals use usernames and passwords exposed in previous data breaches and try them on other websites. This works when people reuse passwords.

**Phishing:** A message appears to come from a legitimate company and directs the recipient to a fake login page or asks for sensitive information.

**Malware:** Malicious software may capture keystrokes, steal browser session information or collect saved credentials.

**Social engineering:** An attacker impersonates a customer, employee or support representative to persuade someone to reveal information or approve an action.

**Session theft:** If an attacker obtains a valid session token, they may access an account without entering its password again. Protecting active sessions is therefore important.

## What happens after an account is compromised?

An attacker may act quickly to reduce the chance of detection. Common actions include changing the password, replacing the registered email address or phone number, adding a payment method, changing delivery details, making purchases or accessing invoices and personal information.

For business accounts, the consequences can extend to employees, software subscriptions and shared payment resources.

## Warning signs

Watch for login notifications from unfamiliar devices, password reset emails you did not request, unexpected changes to contact information, new payment methods, purchases you do not recognise, unexpected logout events or alerts about repeated failed login attempts.

One unfamiliar login location does not automatically prove fraud. Travel, mobile networks and VPN use can affect location information. Consider the surrounding activity.

## How individuals can protect accounts

Use a unique password for every important service. A password manager can generate and store strong credentials.

Enable multi-factor authentication wherever available. An authenticator app or hardware security key can provide stronger protection than a password alone.

Avoid signing in through links in unexpected emails or messages. Open the official website or app directly. Review active sessions and remove devices you no longer recognise. Keep your operating system, browser and security software updated.

For accounts that store payment details, enable transaction alerts where available. This makes it easier to detect activity after an account has been compromised.

## What to do if an account is taken over

1. Use the official recovery process to regain access.
2. Change the password from a trusted device.
3. Secure the associated email account.
4. Remove unknown devices and active sessions.
5. Check saved payment methods and recent transactions.
6. Contact the payment provider or card issuer about unauthorised activity.
7. Preserve relevant emails, notifications and transaction records.

If the same password was used elsewhere, change it on those services too.

## How businesses can reduce account takeover fraud

Combine authentication controls with behavioural monitoring. Useful measures include multi-factor authentication, login rate limits, device and session monitoring, suspicious password-reset detection and alerts for sensitive account changes.

A new device, password reset or contact-information change may warrant additional verification before a payment method can be added or used. Customer support procedures matter as well: fraudsters may exploit weak identity verification during account recovery.

## Final thoughts

Account takeover attacks exploit weaknesses in account security rather than necessarily attacking the payment card itself. Protect login credentials, secure recovery channels and monitor unusual activity.

Separating payment activity across appropriate card accounts may provide additional control, depending on the product. [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) offers card-based payment options; review available account security features and terms before using any payment product.

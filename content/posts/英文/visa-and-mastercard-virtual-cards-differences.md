# Visa and Mastercard Virtual Cards: What Are the Differences?

When choosing a payment card for online transactions, many users focus on the card type, payment features, and supported merchants.

Two of the most recognized payment networks worldwide are Visa and Mastercard.

Both networks support millions of merchants and are widely used for online and offline payments. Virtual cards based on these networks provide users with digital payment options for websites, subscriptions, software services, and international platforms.

Understanding the differences between Visa and Mastercard virtual cards can help users better understand how card networks work and choose a suitable payment solution for their needs.

## What Are Visa and Mastercard?

Visa and Mastercard are global payment networks that connect cardholders, merchants, banks, and payment providers.

They do not usually issue cards directly to consumers. Instead, they provide the infrastructure that allows card transactions to be processed.

When a user makes a payment:

1. The merchant sends a payment request.
2. The payment network processes the transaction.
3. The card provider verifies the payment.
4. The transaction is approved or declined.

Both Visa and Mastercard support different types of cards, including:

- Credit cards
- Debit cards
- Prepaid cards
- Virtual cards
- Physical cards

## What Is a Virtual Visa Card?

A virtual Visa card is a digital card that operates through the Visa payment network.

Instead of having a physical plastic card, users receive digital card details that can be used for online transactions.

Common uses include:

- Online shopping
- Subscription services
- Digital products
- International websites
- Software platforms

A virtual Visa card works similarly to a traditional Visa card when making online payments.

## What Is a Virtual Mastercard?

A virtual Mastercard is a digital payment card that operates through the Mastercard network.

Like a virtual Visa card, it provides digital card information that can be used for online transactions.

Common use cases include:

- Online purchases
- Digital subscriptions
- Business services
- International payments
- Online platforms

The main difference is the payment network behind the card.

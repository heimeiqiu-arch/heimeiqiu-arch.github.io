A business that wants to accept card payments needs more than a checkout page. It also needs a way to connect to the payment system, meet relevant requirements, and receive its funds. A **payment facilitator**, often shortened to **PayFac**, is one model that can help eligible businesses do this.

The term can be confusing because payment facilitators work alongside other payment providers. This guide explains what a PayFac does, how the model works, and what businesses should check before choosing a provider.

## What Is a Payment Facilitator?

A payment facilitator is a company that enables eligible businesses—often called sub-merchants—to accept payments through an arrangement with an acquiring bank or another authorised payment partner.

Instead of every small business independently setting up a traditional merchant-acquiring relationship, a PayFac may onboard businesses under its broader payment programme. The facilitator typically coordinates onboarding, payment integration, transaction monitoring, reporting, and settlement-related processes. The exact responsibilities depend on the programme and applicable regulations.

A PayFac is not automatically a bank, and the label does not mean every provider performs every payment function itself. The underlying acquiring and processing arrangements still matter.

## How Does the PayFac Model Work?

A simplified payment facilitator workflow looks like this:

1. **A business applies.** The business submits information about its legal identity, owners, products, expected transaction volume, and operating model.
2. **The provider reviews the application.** The PayFac and its partners assess eligibility, risk, and required verification. Approval is not guaranteed.
3. **The business integrates checkout.** It uses the provider's supported checkout, API, terminal, or other payment tools.
4. **A customer makes a payment.** The transaction travels through the relevant gateway, processing, acquiring, and network arrangements.
5. **The transaction is reconciled.** The provider records transaction activity, fees, refunds, disputes, and other adjustments.
6. **Funds are paid out.** Subject to the programme's rules, reserves, risk reviews, and settlement schedule, the business receives its payout.

This is a high-level overview rather than a universal flow. The parties involved and the timing of funds can differ by provider, country, payment method, and contract.

## Payment Facilitator vs Gateway vs Processor vs Acquirer

These terms describe different roles, although one provider may offer several of them together.

| Term | Typical role |
|---|---|
| Payment facilitator (PayFac) | Enables and supports sub-merchants through an acquiring or authorised partner arrangement |
| Payment gateway | Securely captures and transmits payment information between checkout and payment services |
| Payment processor | Handles transaction-message processing and related technical connections; exact scope varies |
| Acquirer | A bank or authorised acquiring institution that supports merchants' acceptance of card payments |
| Payment service provider (PSP) | A broad term for a company offering one or more payment services; its exact role depends on the product |

A gateway is not necessarily a PayFac. A gateway may provide technology without onboarding sub-merchants under a facilitator model. Likewise, a company described as a PSP may combine gateway, processing, acquiring, or facilitation services, so read its service description and agreement rather than relying on the label alone.

## Why Businesses Use a Payment Facilitator

### A more streamlined onboarding process

A PayFac may offer a standardised application and integration process, which can be easier for smaller businesses than arranging each payment relationship separately. The provider still needs to perform appropriate checks.

### Integrated payment tools

Businesses may get checkout components, payment APIs, transaction reporting, refund workflows, and support through one provider. Available features vary by plan and integration.

### Easier management across multiple sellers

Platforms and marketplaces may use a facilitator model to help eligible sellers accept payments within a broader product experience. This can reduce the amount of separate payment setup each seller needs to do, depending on the programme.

### Consolidated reporting

A provider may present payment activity, fees, refunds, disputes, and payouts in one dashboard. Businesses should confirm whether reports include all the details needed for accounting and reconciliation.

## What Are the Risks and Limitations?

A streamlined setup does not remove payment obligations. Before signing up, consider the following:

- **Underwriting and eligibility:** Some industries, business models, countries, or transaction patterns may be restricted.
- **Fees:** Review processing rates, fixed fees, chargeback fees, cross-border charges, currency conversion, and any monthly or payout fees.
- **Payout timing:** Funds may not be available immediately. Weekends, holidays, risk reviews, reserves, and disputes can affect payouts.
- **Account holds or termination:** Unusual activity, incomplete verification, elevated disputes, or contract violations may lead to review or restrictions.
- **Disputes and refunds:** Understand who receives dispute notifications, what evidence is required, and how deadlines are managed.
- **Data and compliance:** Check the provider's security practices, responsibilities, and applicable data-protection requirements.

The provider's marketing page is only a starting point. The contract and programme terms explain the actual rights, fees, responsibilities, and payout conditions.

## How to Choose a Payment Facilitator

Compare providers against the way your business actually operates.

1. **Confirm availability.** Make sure the provider supports your country, legal entity, business category, and customer locations.
2. **Understand the legal arrangement.** Identify the acquirer or authorised partner, and clarify whether the provider is acting as a PayFac, gateway, processor, or another type of PSP.
3. **Calculate the total cost.** Look beyond the headline transaction rate to include refunds, disputes, international cards, currency conversion, and payouts.
4. **Review payout rules.** Ask about settlement timing, minimum payout amounts, reserves, and circumstances that can delay funds.
5. **Check integration requirements.** Confirm support for your website platform, mobile app, API, subscriptions, and required checkout methods.
6. **Evaluate risk tools.** Look for suitable fraud controls, authentication options, dispute alerts, and reporting.
7. **Test support and documentation.** Clear technical documentation and responsive support can be important when payments fail or reconciliation does not match.

## Is a Payment Facilitator Suitable for Every Business?

No. A PayFac can be useful for eligible businesses that value a streamlined setup, but a larger business with specialised risk, settlement, reporting, or international requirements may prefer a different acquiring or PSP arrangement. A marketplace may also have different needs from a single online shop.

Compare the complete service, not just the onboarding speed. The best option is one that fits your business model, risk profile, transaction locations, customer payment methods, and operational needs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a PayFac the same as a payment gateway?

No. A gateway generally transmits payment information, while a PayFac enables sub-merchants to accept payments through a broader acquiring or authorised partner arrangement. A company can offer both services.

### Does a payment facilitator hold the money?

Fund flows depend on the provider's legal structure, acquiring arrangements, and applicable rules. Do not assume that a PayFac is a bank or that it holds customer funds in the same way as a bank account. Check the contract and payout terms to understand who handles settlement and payouts.

### Can a small business use a PayFac?

Many PayFac programmes are designed to make payment acceptance accessible to smaller businesses, but eligibility depends on the provider, country, industry, verification results, and risk rules.

### How long does onboarding take?

There is no universal timeline. Some applications may be processed quickly, while others need additional documentation or manual review. Ask the provider for its current process and do not assume that submitting an application means the business is approved.

## Final Takeaway

A payment facilitator helps eligible businesses accept payments through a managed arrangement with an acquirer or authorised partner. It may simplify onboarding and payment operations, but fees, risk controls, payout timing, and contractual responsibilities still matter. Before choosing a PayFac, verify its role, read the terms carefully, and make sure its service fits your business needs.

Match supplier credit notes and refunds to original invoices and card transactions to keep expense records accurate.

A consistent payment process reduces duplicate payments and makes it easier to explain business spending. Before approving a charge, check the supplier record, the underlying invoice, and whether the same cost has already been paid or credited.

## Verify the invoice

Confirm the supplier, invoice number, service period, amount, currency, due date, and approval status. Compare the invoice with the purchase request or contract and check for duplicate references or overlapping service periods.

## Keep supporting records

1. Save the invoice and purchase approval.
2. Match the transaction to the correct supplier and cost centre.
3. Record the payment reference and date.
4. Track refunds, credit notes, and unresolved differences.
5. Review access and billing ownership when staff roles change.

## Resolve differences carefully

Ask the supplier to clarify duplicate or inconsistent invoices before paying. When a refund or credit note arrives, match it to the original transaction rather than treating it as unrelated income. Check transaction status before retrying a payment that appeared to fail.

Use official billing portals and protect payment credentials. Virtual card acceptance varies by merchant, so confirm supported methods before relying on one.

## FAQ

### What should happen when an invoice looks duplicated?
Compare invoice numbers, service dates, amounts, and payment history, then contact the supplier before making another payment.

### Why record refunds against the original purchase?
It keeps the audit trail clear and helps explain the net cost of the original expense.

## Final takeaway

Verify the invoice before paying, retain the approval trail, and reconcile credits or refunds against the original transaction.

Translation agencies and localization providers may charge by word, project, language, or ongoing service agreement. A virtual card may help with online invoices when the provider accepts it, but the contract defines scope, deadlines, and revision terms.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Confirm project scope
Before paying, verify languages, word counts, file formats, delivery dates, review rounds, confidentiality requirements, and any rush charges. For recurring localization work, clarify how new requests are priced and approved.

## Maintain project-level records
Save the quote, invoice, statement of work, and delivery confirmation together. A separate card can make vendor charges easier to identify, while purchase orders or project records explain the business purpose. For larger engagements, check whether the provider requires bank transfer or another payment method instead.

## If the transaction fails
Check the card’s available limit, status, billing details, and provider payment policy. If the invoice is marked paid but work is not scheduled, contact the agency through the agreed official channel before paying again.

## FAQ
**Can I pay a translation agency with a virtual card?** It depends on the agency’s payment methods.

**Does payment settle the scope of work?** No. Keep the contract and deliverables clearly documented.

**Where can I review card options?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and check whether the card fits the provider’s billing process.

## Final thoughts
Pair a clear scope of work with traceable payments to reduce misunderstandings on localization projects.

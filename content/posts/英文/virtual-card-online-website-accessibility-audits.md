Website accessibility audit services evaluate pages and workflows for usability barriers and accessibility issues. Providers may charge per audit, page count, domain, or ongoing monitoring. A virtual card can be a convenient way to pay for an eligible audit or subscription if accepted by the provider.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Compare the billing model before checkout

Clarify the scope: pages tested, user journeys, manual review, automated scans, remediation guidance, and retesting. Check whether the report is a one-time deliverable or part of a recurring plan. A scan alone cannot guarantee that every accessibility issue has been found.

## Troubleshoot a declined payment methodically

If payment fails, check the card’s online-payment eligibility, balance, limits, billing details, and authentication requirements. Confirm accepted card types and regional restrictions. For a custom audit, the provider may invoice under a contract rather than use a self-service card checkout.

## Keep recurring costs under control

Keep the statement of work and final report with the invoice. Record which pages were included and schedule remediation follow-up. Before renewing a monitoring plan, compare current coverage and confirm reports remain accessible after cancellation.

## Frequently asked questions

Does an automated accessibility scan prove a site is fully accessible? No; coverage and manual evaluation matter. Can a virtual card pay for an audit? Only if the provider accepts it.

## Final thoughts

A virtual card can be a useful way to organise eligible online software payments, but acceptance, limits, and recurring-payment support vary by provider and card issuer. Check the service’s current terms, keep renewal reminders, and save invoices for your records. To review available options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/).

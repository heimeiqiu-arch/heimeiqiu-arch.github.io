# How Do Foreign Currency Card Payments Work? Exchange Rates and Fees

When you pay a merchant in another currency, the amount charged to your card account may differ from the amount shown at checkout. This can happen because the transaction must be converted into the currency used by your card account, and additional fees may apply.

Understanding the conversion process can help you compare payment options, review unexpected charges, and make better decisions when shopping internationally or paying for overseas subscriptions.

## What Is a Foreign Currency Card Payment?

A foreign currency card payment occurs when a transaction is made in a currency different from the currency in which your card account is billed or settled.

For example, you might use a card account billed in one currency to pay a website that charges in another. The merchant submits the transaction in its transaction currency, and the payment system applies the relevant conversion process where required.

The final amount depends on factors such as the transaction currency, the applicable exchange rate, the processing arrangements, and any fees imposed by the issuer or card provider.

## How Is a Foreign Currency Card Payment Converted?

A typical foreign currency transaction involves several stages.

### 1. The Merchant Charges in a Particular Currency

The merchant's checkout page or payment terminal displays the currency in which it intends to charge you.

Before confirming the purchase, check both the amount and the currency. A price of 100 in one currency is not equivalent to 100 in another.

### 2. The Transaction Enters the Payment System

The merchant submits the card transaction through its payment processing arrangements. Depending on the transaction and card programme, a card network or other conversion arrangement may determine the exchange rate used for conversion.

The exchange rate applicable to a card transaction is not necessarily the same as the rate displayed by a search engine or currency converter at the moment you purchase.

### 3. The Issuer or Provider Applies Its Rules

The issuer or card provider determines how the transaction is reflected in your account. A foreign transaction fee or currency conversion fee may apply under the card's terms.

Some card products advertise no foreign transaction fee, but this does not automatically mean that every transaction will use the mid-market exchange rate or be free from all conversion-related costs.

### 4. The Transaction Appears in Your Account

The initial authorisation amount and the final posted amount may differ. This can occur when the exchange rate changes between authorisation and processing, when the final transaction amount is adjusted, or when other applicable fees are reflected.

Check the transaction details and the terms of your card before assuming that a difference is an error.

## What Is a Foreign Transaction Fee?

A foreign transaction fee is a charge that may apply when you make a payment involving a foreign currency or a transaction processed outside the card programme's domestic scope.

The exact definition varies by issuer and card product. Some providers charge a percentage of the transaction amount, while others offer products without this fee.

A foreign transaction fee is separate from the exchange rate itself. A transaction can involve currency conversion without an explicit foreign transaction fee, and a fee may apply even when the conversion is processed automatically.

Review the card's fee schedule to understand which transactions qualify and how the fee is calculated.

## What Is Dynamic Currency Conversion?

Dynamic currency conversion, often called DCC, is a service that may allow a merchant or ATM to offer a transaction in the currency of your card account rather than the local currency.

For example, while travelling, a terminal might offer to charge your card in your home currency instead of the merchant's local currency.

This can make the amount easier to understand, but the conversion rate and any associated charges may be less favourable than the conversion arrangement used by your card provider.

Before accepting a DCC offer, compare the displayed amount and exchange rate with the available alternative. If the terminal does not clearly explain the rate or fees, ask the merchant for clarification.

DCC is not available in every transaction, and the details depend on the merchant, terminal, currency, and provider.

## Should You Pay in the Merchant's Currency or Your Card's Currency?

There is no universal answer for every card and transaction. The right choice depends on the exchange rate offered by the merchant, the card provider's conversion arrangements, and the fees associated with each option.

Consider the following:

- **Check the displayed rate.** If the merchant offers a conversion, look for the rate and any additional charges.
- **Review your card's terms.** Find out whether a foreign transaction fee applies and how currency conversion is handled.
- **Compare the total cost.** A convenient display of your home currency does not necessarily mean the transaction is cheaper.
- **Avoid guessing.** If the terminal does not provide enough information to compare the options, ask the merchant what each choice means.

When paying in the merchant's local currency, the conversion is generally handled through the card payment arrangements and the applicable issuer terms. However, the exact treatment depends on your card programme.

## Why Was I Charged More Than the Checkout Amount?

A difference between the checkout amount and the final card charge can have several explanations.

### The Exchange Rate Changed

The exchange rate used for the final transaction may differ from the rate you saw when placing the order. The authorisation and final posting may occur at different times.

### A Foreign Transaction Fee Applied

Your card may charge a fee for certain foreign currency or international transactions. Check the fee schedule and the transaction details to see whether a separate fee was applied or incorporated into the displayed amount.

### The Merchant Used Dynamic Currency Conversion

If you accepted a merchant's offer to charge in your card account currency, the merchant's conversion rate may have determined the amount.

### The Final Transaction Amount Changed

Some merchants adjust the final amount after authorisation, such as where a purchase involves an estimate, a deposit, or a final bill. Check whether the transaction amount itself changed in addition to any currency conversion.

### A Temporary Authorisation Was Replaced

A pending authorisation can differ from the final posted amount. Review the status of each entry before treating the difference as an additional charge.

## How to Check a Foreign Currency Card Charge

If the amount looks incorrect, use this checklist:

1. Find the original receipt or checkout confirmation.
2. Confirm the currency in which the merchant charged you.
3. Check the final posted amount and any separate fee entries.
4. Review your card provider's foreign transaction and currency conversion terms.
5. Look for a DCC choice on the receipt if you paid in person.
6. Contact the merchant or card issuer if the calculation remains unclear.

Keep the receipt and transaction details until the payment has been fully resolved. Do not share your card security code or one-time passwords when asking for help.

## Do Virtual Cards Support Foreign Currency Payments?

Some virtual cards can be used for eligible foreign currency transactions, but support depends on the issuer, card programme, currency, merchant, and transaction type.

A virtual card does not automatically eliminate exchange rate differences or foreign transaction fees. Before using one for overseas subscriptions, digital services, or international purchases, review its supported currencies, fees, limits, and transaction controls.

If you frequently pay in more than one currency, compare the total cost and practical features of the available card options rather than looking at one fee in isolation.

## Foreign Currency Card Payment FAQs

### Is the exchange rate the same as the rate on Google?

Not necessarily. The rate used for a card transaction may come from the applicable card network, provider, or conversion service, and the timing may differ from the rate shown by a public currency converter.

### Can a card charge be converted twice?

A transaction can involve more than one currency-related processing step in some payment arrangements, but the exact treatment depends on the merchant, payment route, and card programme. If you suspect an unexpected conversion, compare the receipt, transaction currencies, and provider terms.

### Does a card with no foreign transaction fee guarantee the best exchange rate?

No. The absence of a foreign transaction fee does not guarantee that the conversion rate is the most favourable. Check both the fee schedule and the conversion method.

### Why is my international transaction still pending?

International transactions can remain pending while the merchant and payment system complete processing. The timing varies. If the pending entry lasts beyond the period stated by your issuer, contact the issuer for clarification.

## Final Thoughts

Foreign currency card payments depend on more than the headline exchange rate. The merchant's currency choice, conversion method, issuer terms, and possible foreign transaction fees can all affect the final amount.

Before confirming an international payment, check the currency displayed at checkout and understand how your card provider handles conversion. These small checks can make overseas purchases and subscriptions easier to manage.

Review usage, plan changes, taxes, exchange rates, and provider notices when a subscription charge differs from last month.

Recurring charges do not always remain identical from one period to the next. Plan changes, prorated periods, taxes, usage, and currency conversion can all affect the final amount.

## Compare the invoice with the plan

Check the billing period, plan tier, usage quantity, tax line items, currency, and any notice about price changes. For trials or upgrades, note the date paid billing begins and whether the first period is shorter than a full cycle.

## A practical review

1. Save the original plan details and renewal date.
2. Compare the current invoice with the previous period.
3. Identify taxes, usage charges, prorations, or currency differences.
4. Check whether a plan or billing location changed.
5. Contact the merchant if the difference remains unexplained.

## Avoid duplicate payments

If a payment attempt fails or the checkout page stalls, verify whether the order or transaction exists before retrying. Use official merchant support for invoice questions and your card provider for transaction-status concerns. Keep card credentials and authentication codes private.

## FAQ

### What is a prorated subscription charge?
It is an adjusted amount for a partial billing period, often after a plan change or mid-cycle start. Check the provider's explanation and contract.

### What records should I keep?
Keep invoices, plan terms, renewal notices, transaction references, and records of any upgrades or address changes.

## Final takeaway

Compare the current bill with the plan and billing period, and ask the merchant to explain differences before assuming the card transaction is wrong.

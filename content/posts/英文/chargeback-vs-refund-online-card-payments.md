# Chargeback vs Refund: What Online Cardholders Need to Know

When an online purchase goes wrong, you may hear two terms: **refund** and **chargeback**. Both can involve money returning to a cardholder, but they are different processes with different rules, timelines, and consequences.

Knowing which route to use matters if you pay for online shopping, AI subscriptions, software, or other digital services with a virtual card. This guide explains the differences, the steps to take, and what to check before raising a dispute.

## What Is a Refund?

A **refund** is typically initiated by the merchant after a customer requests money back or the merchant decides to reverse a purchase.

Common situations include:

- An order is cancelled before fulfilment.
- A product is returned under the merchant's return policy.
- A service was unavailable and the merchant agrees to reimburse the customer.
- A customer was charged twice and the merchant corrects the error.
- A subscription provider agrees to refund an eligible renewal.

The merchant processes the refund through its payment provider. The money usually returns through the original payment method, although timing and exceptions depend on the payment system.

A refund is not always immediate. A merchant may approve it today, but the credit may take additional time to appear in the card account.

## What Is a Chargeback?

A **chargeback** is a card dispute process in which a cardholder asks the issuer or relevant card provider to investigate a transaction under applicable card-network rules.

A chargeback is not the same as simply asking the merchant to return the money. It involves a formal process and may require evidence from the cardholder and merchant.

Potential dispute situations can include:

- A transaction the cardholder did not authorise.
- Goods or services not received as promised.
- A duplicate charge.
- A cancelled recurring service that continues to bill.
- A refund promised by the merchant that has not been processed.

Not every complaint qualifies for a chargeback. Eligibility, deadlines, required documents, and outcomes depend on the transaction, card programme, network rules, and local law.

## Chargeback vs Refund: Key Differences

| Question | Refund | Chargeback |
|---|---|---|
| Who usually starts it? | The merchant, often after a customer request | The cardholder through the issuer or relevant provider |
| Who reviews the request? | Primarily the merchant | The issuer and other payment participants under dispute rules |
| Is approval guaranteed? | No; merchant policy and legal rights apply | No; the dispute may be rejected or reversed |
| Is evidence needed? | Sometimes | Often |
| Can the merchant challenge it? | The merchant decides whether to approve the refund, subject to applicable law | The merchant may submit evidence to contest the chargeback |
| Does it take time? | Yes | Often longer and more complex |

A refund is generally the simpler starting point when the merchant is responsive and the issue is a normal return or cancellation. A chargeback may be relevant when a qualifying issue cannot be resolved directly, or when an unauthorised transaction needs urgent attention.

## When Should You Contact the Merchant First?

For ordinary service problems, contacting the merchant is often a useful first step.

For example, imagine you paid for a monthly software plan but cancelled it shortly after renewal. Before filing a card dispute, check the service's refund policy and contact support with your invoice and cancellation confirmation.

Provide clear information:

1. The order or invoice number.
2. The transaction date and amount.
3. The problem with the purchase.
4. What resolution you are requesting.
5. Relevant screenshots or emails, with sensitive card information hidden.

Keep a record of the conversation. If the merchant agrees to issue a refund, ask for confirmation and the expected processing window.

**Important:** If you notice a transaction you did not authorise, contact your card issuer or provider promptly. Do not delay reporting suspected fraud while waiting for a merchant response.

## How to Request a Chargeback

The process varies, but these steps are common.

### 1. Review the transaction

Check the merchant descriptor, amount, currency, and date. Sometimes a business appears on a statement under a different trading name, and a pending authorisation may not yet be a final charge.

### 2. Gather evidence

Depending on the issue, useful evidence may include receipts, order confirmations, cancellation records, delivery information, and correspondence with the merchant.

Do not share your full card number, CVV, password, or authentication codes in ordinary email messages.

### 3. Contact the card provider

Use the official support channel and explain the transaction and dispute reason. Ask whether your card programme supports the relevant dispute process and what deadline applies.

### 4. Follow the instructions

The provider may request a form or supporting documents. Provide accurate information and respond to follow-up questions promptly.

### 5. Track the outcome

Some disputes involve temporary credits or later adjustments. A temporary credit does not necessarily mean the case has been won. The final outcome depends on the review and applicable rules.

## How Long Do Refunds and Chargebacks Take?

There is no universal timeline.

A merchant refund may take several business days to appear after processing, depending on the merchant, payment processor, issuer, and account system.

A chargeback can take substantially longer because multiple parties may exchange evidence. Card-network deadlines and investigation periods vary by dispute reason and programme.

Instead of relying on a single number found online, ask the merchant or card provider for the timeframe applicable to your transaction.

## Can You Dispute a Virtual Card Payment?

Possibly. A virtual card is a digital form of payment card, but its dispute rights and procedures depend on the underlying issuer, network, card programme, and relevant law.

Do not assume that every virtual card provider offers identical protections. Before using a card for expensive purchases, review the provider's dispute policy and support channels.

## What Happens If the Virtual Card Is Frozen or Expired?

An expired, frozen, or replaced card does not automatically mean a refund is impossible. In many card systems, refunds may still be routed to the underlying account even when the original card credentials are no longer active.

However, the handling differs by issuer and programme. If you expect a refund to an old virtual card, contact the provider rather than assuming the money is lost or automatically redirected.

Keep the transaction reference and refund confirmation until the credit is visible.

## Subscription Billing: Refund or Chargeback?

Recurring subscriptions create a common source of confusion.

Suppose you signed up for an AI tool or SaaS service and later discovered a renewal charge. First check whether automatic renewal was disclosed, whether you cancelled before the deadline, and whether the merchant's refund policy applies.

If the merchant made a valid charge under an agreement you accepted, disliking the renewal does not automatically establish chargeback eligibility. If you cancelled properly but continued to be billed, preserve your cancellation confirmation and contact the merchant or provider.

To reduce future problems:

- Record subscription renewal dates.
- Read cancellation terms before subscribing.
- Keep cancellation confirmation emails.
- Review card transactions regularly.
- Use a payment method that supports the intended recurring billing.

## Mistakes to Avoid

### Filing a chargeback for every inconvenience

Disputes are designed for qualifying transaction problems, not as a substitute for ordinary customer support or a way to avoid valid charges.

### Missing a deadline

Dispute time limits can be strict. Contact your provider promptly once you identify a possible issue.

### Submitting incomplete information

Clear evidence helps the provider understand what happened. Avoid vague explanations and keep dates, amounts, and communications consistent.

### Claiming a purchase was unauthorised when it was not

Give an accurate account of the transaction. False dispute claims can have serious consequences.

### Assuming a temporary credit is final

A provisional adjustment may be reversed if the dispute is not upheld.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a chargeback the same as a refund?

No. A refund is normally processed by the merchant. A chargeback is a formal card dispute handled through the relevant issuer and payment system.

### Should I request a refund before a chargeback?

For ordinary purchase issues, contacting the merchant first is often appropriate and may be required for some dispute categories. For suspected unauthorised transactions, report the issue to your card provider promptly.

### Can a merchant reject a chargeback?

A merchant may challenge a dispute by submitting evidence. The final result depends on the applicable dispute process.

### Can I get a chargeback on a prepaid virtual card?

It depends on the underlying card programme, issuer, network, and transaction. Check the provider's official dispute terms.

### What if a merchant says a refund was sent but I have not received it?

Ask for the refund confirmation and any available reference number, then contact your card provider to trace the credit.

### Does cancelling a virtual card cancel my subscription?

Not necessarily. Cancelling a card is not the same as cancelling your agreement with a merchant. Follow the merchant's cancellation procedure and retain confirmation.

## Final Thoughts

A refund and a chargeback can both address payment problems, but they serve different purposes. For a routine return or service complaint, start with the merchant when appropriate. For suspected fraud or an unresolved qualifying dispute, contact your card issuer or provider quickly and follow its official process.

Whether you use a physical card or a virtual card, the best protection is understanding the terms, keeping records, monitoring charges, and acting promptly when something goes wrong.

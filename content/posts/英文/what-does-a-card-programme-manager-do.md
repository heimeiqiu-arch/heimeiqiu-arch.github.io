# What Does a Card Programme Manager Do?

A payment card programme usually involves several organisations working together. A financial institution may provide the issuing relationship, a processor may handle transaction technology, and a card network may provide the payment infrastructure.

Someone also needs to coordinate the programme's day-to-day operation. This is often the role of a **card programme manager**.

The programme manager helps turn the technical and financial arrangements into a usable card product for customers.

## What Is a Card Programme Manager?

A card programme manager is an organisation responsible for coordinating and managing defined aspects of a card programme.

Its work may cover product planning, partner relationships, onboarding processes, operational controls, customer experience, and programme performance.

The precise responsibilities vary. In some programmes, the manager handles extensive operational work. In others, the issuing bank or service providers retain more responsibilities.

The title does not automatically make the programme manager the legal card issuer or the owner of the BIN.

## What Does a Programme Manager Actually Do?

### Coordinates Programme Partners

A card programme may involve an issuing bank or BIN sponsor, an issuer processor, a card network, a technology platform, fraud and identity-verification providers, card manufacturers and fulfilment partners, and customer support providers.

The programme manager helps coordinate these relationships and ensures the services work together according to the programme's requirements.

### Helps Define the Card Product

The programme manager may participate in decisions about physical or virtual card availability, debit or prepaid product structure, supported currencies, spending controls, fees and limits, customer eligibility, and cardholder features.

These decisions must remain consistent with the sponsor's approval, network requirements, and applicable regulations.

### Manages Operational Workflows

Daily operations may include card issuance, activation, suspension, replacement, customer onboarding, transaction support, and operational reporting.

The programme manager may also coordinate changes to card features and customer-facing services.

### Supports Risk and Compliance Processes

Depending on the programme, responsibilities may include coordinating identity verification, fraud monitoring, suspicious-activity escalation, complaints, and dispute workflows.

These responsibilities do not necessarily transfer the sponsor's legal obligations to the programme manager. The contractual and regulatory allocation of responsibility must be clear.

### Monitors Programme Performance

A programme manager may monitor active card numbers, transaction volume, authorization performance, fraud and dispute levels, customer support volume, card activation rates, and operational incidents.

These indicators help identify issues and guide product improvements.

## Programme Manager vs. BIN Sponsor

A BIN sponsor is the eligible financial institution providing the relevant issuing and network relationship.

A programme manager coordinates defined programme operations and partner relationships.

The two roles may work closely together, but they are not interchangeable. The sponsor generally retains the responsibilities assigned to it by applicable network rules and regulatory arrangements.

## Programme Manager vs. Issuer Processor

The issuer processor provides the technical infrastructure for card records, network connectivity, and transaction processing.

The programme manager focuses on the broader programme operation and coordination.

One provider may offer both types of service. Businesses should still identify which entity is responsible for each function.

## Why Does the Role Matter?

Without clear coordination, a card programme can experience problems such as delayed integrations, unclear operational ownership, inconsistent customer support, or gaps in risk controls.

A capable programme manager can help align partners and maintain a consistent customer experience.

However, the quality of the programme also depends on the sponsor, processor, network, technology, and the effectiveness of the overall operating model.

## What Should a Business Evaluate?

Before working with a programme manager, ask about its experience with the relevant card product, network relationships, integration capabilities, operational support, risk processes, reporting, and ability to coordinate the required service providers.

Clarify which activities it performs directly and which are handled by subcontractors or the sponsoring institution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a card programme manager the card issuer?

Not necessarily. The legal issuer may be the sponsoring bank or another eligible institution.

### Does every card programme need a separate programme manager?

No. Some providers offer integrated issuing services that combine programme management with other capabilities.

### Can the programme manager control card limits?

It may manage or configure limits within the authority granted by the sponsor and the programme's approved rules.

A card programme manager is best understood as the coordinator of the programme's operating model, rather than automatically the bank or processor behind every transaction.

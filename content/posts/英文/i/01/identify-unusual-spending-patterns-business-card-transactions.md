Large card statements are difficult to review transaction by transaction. Looking for patterns can reveal duplicate billing, outdated subscriptions, policy exceptions, or potentially unauthorized activity. Yet unusual activity may also reflect a legitimate project or seasonal increase. The aim is to identify items worth reviewing, not assume every variation is fraud.

## Establish a baseline

Use enough historical data to capture normal business cycles. Track average transaction amount, transaction count, spending by category, common merchants, recurring payment amounts, typical transaction days, and department budgets. A software team may have stable hosting costs; several large payments to unfamiliar merchants may deserve attention. A baseline is a guide, not a rigid rule.

## Compare activity with role and department

A salesperson who travels regularly may have hotel and transport expenses; a staff member who rarely travels may need to explain similar charges. Department comparisons can reveal duplicate subscriptions or newly adopted tools, but responsibilities and approved budgets matter.

## Look for repeated or fragmented payments

Review similar amounts close together, repeated merchant descriptors, purchases split across cards, or payments just below an approval threshold. These may indicate duplicate billing or threshold avoidance, but supplier order processing may explain them. Check invoices before drawing conclusions.

## Monitor recurring payments

Maintain a subscription register with service owner, expected amount, billing frequency, renewal date, and cancellation process. Flag unexpected price increases, new recurring charges, duplicate subscriptions, renewals after cancellation, and services without a clear owner. Usage-based billing, taxes, and extra seats may explain changes.

## Review timing and merchant details in context

A weekend or late-night transaction is not automatically suspicious; employees may work across time zones or travel. An unfamiliar descriptor may identify a parent company or payment processor. Review receipts, invoices, category, transaction status, and cardholder explanation together.

## Investigate and improve controls

Verify the data, check pending versus settled status, compare with policy and historical patterns, request evidence, and contact the issuer promptly if unauthorized use is suspected. Record conclusions and corrective action. An anomaly is a signal, not proof of misconduct. Use recurring findings to improve renewal approvals, spending limits, supplier records, and employee training.

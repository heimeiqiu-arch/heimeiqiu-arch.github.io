Accessibility overlay tools add interface controls or automated features to websites, but they do not automatically resolve every accessibility barrier. Plans may depend on domains, traffic, or support. A virtual card may be accepted for the subscription, subject to vendor terms.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Compare the billing model before checkout

Review the exact features, limitations, and compatibility with your site. Consider testing with disabled users and established accessibility evaluation methods; do not treat an overlay as a substitute for fixing underlying code and content. Check renewal terms and domain limits.

## Troubleshoot a declined payment methodically

If payment is declined, check balance, limits, billing details, recurring-payment eligibility, and authentication. Verify accepted card types and regions. For a subscription dispute, use the provider’s official billing channel and preserve the plan description and invoice.

## Keep recurring costs under control

Track domains covered and review actual accessibility outcomes before renewal. Keep invoices with website costs and document any remediation work separately. Before cancelling, remove scripts safely and test core pages after the change.

## Frequently asked questions

Does an overlay guarantee accessibility compliance? No. Can a virtual card pay for the service? Only if the vendor accepts it.

## Final thoughts

A virtual card can be a useful way to organise eligible online software payments, but acceptance, limits, and recurring-payment support vary by provider and card issuer.

# Why Is the Merchant Name on My Card Statement Different?

Have you ever bought something online and later noticed that the name on your card statement does not match the website you visited? This can be confusing, especially when you are trying to identify a subscription payment.

A different merchant name does not automatically mean that your card has been used fraudulently. There are several legitimate reasons why the name displayed by your bank may differ from the brand you recognise.

## 1. The Business Uses a Different Legal Name

Many businesses operate under a public-facing brand but process payments through a registered company with a different legal name. For example, a subscription may be sold as ExampleApp while the statement displays Example Digital Services Ltd.

The statement may identify the legal entity receiving or processing the payment rather than the brand shown on the website. Check the receipt or billing policy for the company's legal name.

## 2. A Payment Processor Appears on the Statement

Some businesses use third-party payment processors or payment platforms. Depending on the arrangement and statement descriptor, the displayed name may identify the processor, the merchant, or both.

This is common with online services, marketplaces, digital products and subscriptions. A processor's name alone is not evidence of fraud, but you should still confirm that the transaction matches a purchase you made.

## 3. You Purchased Through a Marketplace

When you buy from an independent seller through a marketplace, the statement may identify the marketplace, seller or a related billing entity. Similar arrangements can apply to app stores and travel booking platforms.

Review your order history and email receipts, comparing the transaction date and amount with the purchase.

## 4. A Subscription Uses a Different Billing Entity

Recurring payments can be difficult to recognise when the descriptor differs from the service's product name. Software may be billed by its operating company, while purchases made through an app store may appear under the app store's billing entity.

Check active subscriptions, account billing pages and previous invoices. Do not assume a charge is legitimate simply because it repeats monthly. For more information, see [What Is a Merchant-Initiated Transaction?](/posts/what-is-a-merchant-initiated-transaction/).

## 5. The Descriptor Is Abbreviated

Card statement descriptions often have limited space. A merchant's name may be shortened, rearranged or displayed with a location, website address, telephone number or reference number. Different banks may present the same transaction differently.

Compare the full transaction details in your banking app with the information on your receipt before drawing conclusions.

## How to Identify an Unfamiliar Card Charge

1. **Check the amount and date.** Compare them with recent purchases, subscriptions and free trials.
2. **Review receipts and order histories.** Search email and online accounts for matching transactions.
3. **Check billing information.** Look for a legal company name, payment processor or marketplace reference.
4. **Ask your card issuer.** The issuer may be able to provide additional transaction details.
5. **Act if the charge remains unexplained.** Contact your issuer through its official app, website or phone number if you suspect unauthorised use.

Never share your full card number, security code or one-time verification code with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

## Does a Different Merchant Name Mean Fraud?

No. A statement descriptor can differ from the brand name for legitimate reasons. The important question is whether you can connect the transaction to a purchase, subscription or other payment you authorised.

If you cannot establish that connection, treat the transaction seriously. Your card issuer can explain the available investigation and dispute options.

## Does the Type of Card Affect the Description?

The displayed description is generally influenced by the merchant's payment setup, transaction data and the card issuer's presentation of that information. Using a virtual card does not guarantee that the merchant's brand name will appear exactly as expected.

Virtual and physical cards can both be used for online payments, subject to the card provider's terms, supported merchants and security requirements. If you use a dedicated card for a subscription, it may be easier to compare transactions with the service you intended to pay.

## Final Thoughts

An unfamiliar merchant name is not necessarily a warning sign, but it deserves a quick check. Compare the transaction amount and date with receipts, subscriptions and order history. If you still cannot identify the charge, contact your card issuer rather than ignoring it.

The safest approach is to verify unexplained payments and respond promptly when a transaction appears unauthorised.

A product may be listed at $50, yet the checkout page shows $58. Customers may assume the website has made a mistake or added an unexpected charge. Price differences can have legitimate causes, but unexplained changes damage trust and lead to abandoned orders.

## Taxes
Sales tax, VAT, GST, or other charges may depend on customer location and product type. Some sites calculate tax only after an address is entered. Explain whether displayed prices include tax and follow the pricing rules that apply in each market.

## Shipping and delivery
Physical products may have delivery charges based on destination, service level, package size, or order value. Show delivery options and costs as early as practical. If express delivery costs more, make the difference clear before the final payment step.

## Discounts and promotional conditions
A coupon may require a minimum spend, apply only to selected products, exclude delivery, or have expired. Explain why a code was accepted or rejected. Do not silently remove a discount without telling the customer.

## Currency conversion
A site may display an estimated local-currency price while submitting the transaction in another currency. The card issuer may then convert it into the account currency. Exchange-rate changes, rounding, and conversion fees can affect the final statement. Identify the transaction currency and avoid promising that every cardholder will see the same converted amount.

## Service and handling fees
If a business applies a service or handling charge, explain what it covers, how it is calculated, and whether it is mandatory. Confirm that the fee structure complies with local consumer rules and payment-network requirements.

## Subscription pricing
For subscriptions, distinguish the amount charged today from future renewals. State the billing frequency, promotional period, renewal amount, and cancellation terms. Customers should understand what they are authorizing before payment.

## Rounding and calculation differences
Currency calculations can produce fractions that must be rounded. Inconsistent rules between product pages, cart calculations, and payment requests can create discrepancies. Use consistent calculation logic and compare the displayed total with the amount submitted to the provider.

## Audit the full pricing journey
Compare the product page, cart, checkout total, payment request, and transaction record. Review tax configuration, shipping rules, discounts, currency settings, and provider responses. Test important customer locations and currencies.

## Final thoughts
Taxes, delivery, discounts, conversion, and subscription terms can all explain price differences. Show the complete amount and currency before authorization, and explain how the total is calculated.

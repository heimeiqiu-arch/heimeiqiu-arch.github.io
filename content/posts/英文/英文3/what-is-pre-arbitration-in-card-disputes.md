A merchant may submit evidence to challenge a chargeback, but the issuer may disagree with the response. In some card-network frameworks, the dispute can then move into a further stage known as **pre-arbitration**. This stage may provide another opportunity to resolve the disagreement before a formal arbitration decision is requested.

## What is pre-arbitration?

Pre-arbitration is a dispute-escalation stage that may occur after an earlier chargeback response has been submitted and the disagreement remains unresolved. It allows the relevant parties to address remaining issues under applicable network rules.

The exact name, eligibility conditions, deadlines, and procedures differ across networks and dispute categories. It is not available for every chargeback.

## Why does a dispute reach pre-arbitration?

A dispute may continue because the issuer does not accept the merchant's representment evidence or believes the original chargeback remains valid. For example, a merchant may provide proof that a product was delivered, while the issuer considers that the evidence does not satisfy the requirements for the particular dispute category.

## How does the process work?

1. **Initial chargeback:** The issuer raises a dispute through the relevant network process.
2. **Merchant representment:** The acquiring side submits the merchant's evidence and response.
3. **Disagreement continues:** The issuer may reject the response or maintain its position.
4. **Eligibility is assessed:** The parties review whether the dispute qualifies for the next stage.
5. **Further response:** The relevant party submits its position and supporting information under network requirements.
6. **Resolution or escalation:** The parties may resolve the disagreement or proceed to arbitration if permitted.

The actual sequence may differ by network.

## What should merchants evaluate?

Pre-arbitration can involve additional operational work, fees, and potential financial exposure. Before responding, consider whether the original evidence directly addresses the dispute, whether new information is available, whether the rules support the merchant's position, whether the case is eligible, what costs may apply, and what deadline governs the next step.

A merchant should not escalate every case automatically. In some situations, accepting the dispute may be commercially more reasonable.

## Pre-arbitration vs. representment

Representment is the merchant-side response to an initial chargeback. Pre-arbitration is a later stage that may be available when the disagreement continues after that response. They are related but have different procedural purposes.

## Pre-arbitration vs. arbitration

Pre-arbitration is generally an opportunity to address a remaining disagreement before a formal arbitration decision. Arbitration is a later dispute-resolution process in which the network may review the case under its rules. Not every dispute progresses through both stages.

## Reduce unnecessary escalations

Maintain complete transaction records and build evidence around specific allegations. A structured review can identify whether the original response was incomplete, the dispute category was misunderstood, or the merchant has a reasonable basis for further action.

Record why disputes are accepted or escalated. This helps improve future processes and makes operational costs visible.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is pre-arbitration mandatory?

No. Whether it is required or available depends on the relevant network and dispute framework.

### Can additional fees apply?

Potential fees and financial consequences depend on the case, provider agreement, and network rules.

### Does pre-arbitration mean a final decision has been made?

No. It is a stage in the dispute process, not necessarily the final determination.

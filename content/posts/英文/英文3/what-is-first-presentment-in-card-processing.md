A card payment does not end when checkout displays an approval message. After authorization and capture, transaction information moves through the payment system so that financial obligations between participants can be calculated and settled. One term used in this process is **first presentment**.

## What is first presentment?

First presentment is the initial submission of a card transaction's financial and related data for clearing under the applicable network process. It generally follows the merchant's capture process and the acquiring side's preparation of transaction records.

The information is submitted through the relevant network and processing arrangements so participants can account for the payment. The precise technical definition and timing depend on the network and transaction type.

## Where it fits in the payment lifecycle

### 1. Authorization

The merchant requests approval. The issuer or designated authorization system evaluates the request and returns a response.

### 2. Capture

The merchant confirms that an approved transaction should proceed for processing. Capture timing depends on the merchant's business model and payment setup.

### 3. First presentment

The transaction data is submitted into the clearing process so the relevant financial obligations can be calculated.

### 4. Clearing

Payment participants exchange and process transaction information according to network procedures.

### 5. Settlement

Financial obligations between participants are settled through the applicable arrangements.

### 6. Merchant payout

The acquirer or payment provider pays the merchant according to the merchant agreement and payout schedule.

These stages are related but not interchangeable. Systems may combine or describe parts of the process differently.

## What information is included?

Transaction data may include the amount, currency, date, merchant information, transaction identifiers, card-related data, and other fields required by network specifications. Exact data elements vary by network and transaction type.

Accurate information matters because errors can affect clearing, reconciliation, reporting, and dispute handling.

## Why first presentment matters

First presentment moves a transaction beyond initial authorization and into the financial processing workflow. Accurate presentment supports transaction accounting, consistent settlement records, reconciliation between sales and payment reports, and identification of processing discrepancies.

If the amount, currency, or transaction information is incorrect, the merchant may need to investigate the resulting discrepancy.

## First presentment vs. authorization

Authorization is a request for approval to proceed with a transaction. First presentment is the submission of transaction information into clearing.

An approved authorization does not automatically mean that first presentment has occurred. A merchant may need to capture an authorized transaction before it enters subsequent financial processing stages.

## First presentment vs. merchant payout

First presentment is part of network clearing. Merchant payout is the payment made to the merchant under its acquiring or payment-service agreement. The events are connected, but payout timing and calculation depend on the provider's settlement and payout arrangements.

## What can cause presentment problems?

Potential issues include incorrect amounts, missing or inconsistent data, duplicate submissions, late processing, and mismatches between authorization and captured transaction information. Treatment depends on network rules and provider procedures.

When investigating a discrepancy, compare checkout records, capture logs, processor reports, and settlement statements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does first presentment happen immediately after authorization?

Not necessarily. Capture and presentment timing depend on the merchant's workflow, transaction type, and processing arrangement.

### Is first presentment the same as settlement?

No. Presentment submits transaction data for clearing; settlement concerns financial obligations between participants.

### Does it mean the merchant has received the money?

No. Merchant payout is a separate event governed by the acquiring or payment-provider agreement.

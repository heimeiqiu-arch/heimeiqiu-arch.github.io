Friendly fraud occurs when a cardholder disputes a legitimate transaction or reports it as unauthorised, even though the purchase was made by them or someone they authorised.

The term can be misleading. It does not necessarily mean the cardholder deliberately intends to commit fraud. Some disputes result from confusion, forgotten purchases, unclear billing descriptors or family members using a shared card. Other cases involve intentional abuse of the chargeback process.

## Common examples

**A forgotten purchase.** A customer sees an unfamiliar merchant name on a statement and assumes the transaction is fraudulent.

**A disputed subscription renewal.** A customer forgets that a recurring subscription was active and disputes the renewal instead of contacting the merchant.

**A family purchase.** A child or another household member uses a saved payment method. The cardholder does not recognise the transaction.

**A received product is disputed.** In deliberate cases, a customer receives goods or services but claims they were never delivered or authorised.

**A misunderstood digital purchase.** A customer buys an online service or download but later disputes the transaction because the billing name or purchase terms were unclear.

## Friendly fraud vs. genuine fraud

Genuine card fraud involves someone using card details without the cardholder's permission. Friendly fraud involves a dispute over a transaction made by the cardholder or an authorised user. Merchant error is different again: the merchant may charge the wrong amount or process a duplicate transaction.

Subscription confusion can overlap with these categories. A merchant should not assume every disputed transaction is friendly fraud. Genuine unauthorised transactions must be taken seriously, and the issuer or payment provider determines how the dispute proceeds under applicable rules.

## Why it happens

Unclear billing descriptors are a common cause. A customer may recognise the product but not the company name on the statement.

Subscription renewals create confusion when customers forget the renewal date or do not understand cancellation terms. Digital goods can be harder to document than physical deliveries: access to a software service or download requires different evidence from a shipping record.

Some customers also deliberately misuse chargebacks because they believe it is easier than requesting a refund.

## How merchants can prevent avoidable disputes

**Use recognisable billing descriptors.** The name on the statement should be consistent with the business and recognisable to customers.

**Explain recurring charges.** Show renewal frequency, price and cancellation terms before the customer confirms a subscription.

**Send clear confirmations.** Receipts should identify the product, merchant, amount and transaction date.

**Make cancellation straightforward.** A confusing cancellation process can increase disputes.

**Provide accessible support.** Customers should be able to resolve billing questions without immediately contacting their bank.

**Keep relevant records.** Preserve order confirmations, delivery records, account activity, authentication results and customer communications where permitted.

## Evidence that may help with a chargeback

The appropriate evidence depends on the dispute reason and network requirements. For physical products, relevant records may include delivery confirmation, shipping information and customer communications.

For digital services, useful records may include account registration, login activity, download records, service usage and acceptance of terms.

For recurring payments, retain evidence of the original agreement, renewal notices, cancellation records and relevant billing communications. Submit accurate, relevant information that addresses the actual dispute reason. More evidence is not automatically better.

## How consumers can avoid accidental disputes

Check the merchant descriptor before reporting an unfamiliar transaction. Search email receipts and subscription records for the amount and date. If a family member may have made the purchase, confirm that first.

If a transaction is genuinely unauthorised, contact the issuer promptly.

## Final thoughts

Friendly fraud ranges from innocent billing confusion to deliberate chargeback abuse. Merchants can reduce unnecessary disputes by making payment information transparent, providing clear receipts and maintaining reliable transaction records. Consumers benefit from reviewing statements and subscription renewals regularly.

# Payment Aggregator vs Payment Facilitator: Key Differences

Payment aggregators and payment facilitators can both help businesses accept payments through a shared provider relationship. The terms are sometimes used interchangeably in marketing, but they do not always describe the same legal or operational model.

The most reliable way to compare them is to examine the merchant relationship, acquiring structure, onboarding responsibilities, and how funds are handled. This guide focuses on those distinctions rather than repeating a general introduction to PayFac services.

## What Is a Payment Aggregator?

A payment aggregator generally brings multiple merchants onto a shared payment service or acceptance arrangement. Depending on the market, this may involve a master merchant structure, a platform arrangement, or a broader payment service that combines several functions.

The term is not applied identically in every jurisdiction. A provider described as an aggregator may perform different roles depending on its acquiring partners and contractual model.

## What Is a Payment Facilitator?

A payment facilitator, often called a PayFac, commonly refers to a defined submerchant model supported by an acquiring institution and relevant card-network arrangements. Eligible businesses are onboarded as submerchants within the facilitator's programme, rather than each necessarily establishing a conventional direct acquiring relationship.

A PayFac may have specific onboarding, monitoring, risk, and reporting responsibilities. The precise obligations depend on the applicable rules and agreements.

## Key Differences

| Area | Payment Aggregator | Payment Facilitator |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Broad term for a shared or aggregated payment acceptance service | More specific submerchant acquiring model in many card-payment contexts |
| Merchant relationship | Depends on the provider's structure; may use a master merchant or platform arrangement | Eligible businesses are commonly onboarded as submerchants under the facilitator programme |
| Acquiring relationship | May rely on one or more acquiring partners | Typically operates through an acquiring sponsor or authorised acquiring relationship |
| Responsibilities | Vary significantly by market and contract | Often includes defined submerchant onboarding and ongoing programme responsibilities |
| Terminology | Used broadly and inconsistently | More formal meaning in relevant network and regulatory contexts |

This comparison is a guide, not a universal legal definition. In some markets, a provider may describe its service as aggregation while also operating a PayFac model.

## How Do Onboarding Responsibilities Differ?

Under an aggregated service, the provider may collect merchant information and coordinate access to its payment platform. Who performs underwriting, verification, and ongoing monitoring depends on the underlying arrangement.

In a PayFac programme, the facilitator typically has a defined role in onboarding submerchants and managing the programme under its agreements with the acquirer and other parties. This does not remove verification requirements or guarantee approval.

Merchants should ask who makes the final eligibility decision, which entity is responsible for monitoring, and what happens if the merchant's business model changes.

## How Do Funds and Payouts Work?

Do not assume that every aggregator or PayFac handles funds in the same way. Depending on the model, transactions may be settled through an acquirer and then paid out to merchants by the provider or another designated entity.

Ask which entity receives settlement proceeds, which entity pays your business, how fees and reserves are applied, and what happens during a dispute or account review. The agreement should explain payout timing, currencies, deductions, and any conditions that can delay funds.

## Which Model Is Better for a Merchant?

There is no universal winner. A small online seller may prefer a provider that offers a straightforward integrated service. A software platform onboarding many submerchants may need the specific capabilities and responsibilities of a formal PayFac arrangement.

Compare the service based on your business model, geography, transaction volume, risk profile, reporting needs, and the level of control you require. Review the contract and identify the actual acquiring institution and service provider rather than relying on labels alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are payment aggregators and PayFacs the same?
Not necessarily. The terms can overlap, but PayFac often describes a more specific submerchant acquiring model.

### Does a PayFac eliminate merchant verification?
No. Submerchants are still subject to applicable onboarding, verification, risk, and monitoring requirements.

### Can a company be both an aggregator and a PayFac?
Depending on the market and structure, a provider may use aggregation language while operating a formal PayFac programme. Verify the actual legal and acquiring arrangement.

The distinction is ultimately about structure and responsibility—not simply which provider has the easiest signup process.

Website security scanners may charge for scheduled scans, malware monitoring, vulnerability reports, or multiple domains. A virtual card can make the subscription easier to identify, but it is only one part of managing a security tool.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Check coverage and limits
Review the domains covered, scan frequency, alert features, remediation support, and exclusions. Confirm whether the tool scans the application areas you need and whether add-ons cost extra. A subscription should not be treated as proof that a site is secure.

## Organize access and billing
Assign an owner, record renewal dates, and keep invoices with security review records. Use a reusable card for recurring fees where accepted. Limit who can change billing and administrator settings, and remove access for former team members.

## Payment troubleshooting
Confirm card status, available balance, billing information, and merchant acceptance. If a payment is declined, use the provider’s official account page to check whether the subscription is still active.

## FAQ
**Can a virtual card pay for security scanning tools?** Some providers accept virtual cards; check checkout rules.

**Does a scanner subscription guarantee protection?** No. Results need review and remediation.

**Where can I learn about virtual cards?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and verify the intended vendor accepts the card.

## Final thoughts
Keep security testing, remediation, and payment administration as separate checklist items.

# Virtual Card Fees Explained: Issuance, Funding, Currency Conversion, and More

A virtual card can make online payments more convenient, but the total cost is not always limited to the amount shown at checkout. Depending on the card provider and programme, fees may apply when a card is issued, funds are added, a transaction uses another currency, or a payment is refunded.

If you are comparing virtual cards for software, AI subscriptions, online shopping, or business expenses, review the full fee structure before choosing a provider. ## Which Fees Might Apply to a Virtual Card?

Not every provider charges every fee listed below. Some may offer a service without a particular fee, while others may use a fixed charge, percentage, exchange-rate markup, or a combination. Always check the provider's current official fee schedule.

### 1. Card issuance fee

A provider may charge a fee to create a virtual card. The fee can vary by card type, account level, or programme. Some services may advertise free issuance but charge for other activities, so look at the whole fee schedule rather than one headline price.

### 2. Funding or top-up fee

If your card balance must be funded before spending, adding money may involve a fee. The cost can depend on the funding method, currency, or payment channel. Check whether the fee is charged by the card provider, the funding service, or both.

### 3. Currency conversion fee

When the card's billing currency differs from the merchant's transaction currency, currency conversion may occur. The final cost can depend on the card network's exchange rate, the issuer's rules, and any provider markup or foreign-transaction fee.

For example, a purchase displayed in euros may be converted to another currency before it is deducted from your balance. The amount you ultimately pay can differ from a rough estimate based on a public exchange rate.

### 4. Transaction fee

Some programmes charge per transaction or apply a fee to particular transaction types. Others do not. Check whether the rules differ for online purchases, recurring payments, cash-like transactions, or specific merchant categories.

### 5. Declined transaction fee

Some card programmes may charge for certain declined transactions, while others do not. Before repeatedly retrying a payment, check your balance, card status, spending limits, and merchant requirements. Repeated attempts can also trigger a merchant's or issuer's risk controls.

### 6. Refund or dispute-related costs

A merchant refund is not always processed in the same way across providers. Ask whether the original transaction fee, conversion cost, or other charges are returned when a purchase is refunded. Dispute-related fees, if any, depend on the programme's terms and applicable rules.

### 7. Inactivity or account maintenance fee

Some financial products may have maintenance or inactivity charges. Do not assume that a virtual card has such a fee—or that it does not. Look for these terms in the account agreement and fee schedule.

## How to Calculate the Real Cost of a Virtual Card

To compare options fairly, consider the total cost of your expected use rather than focusing on a single fee.

A simple comparison can include:

- Card creation or issuance cost.
- Cost of adding funds.
- Expected monthly transaction count.
- Currency conversion costs for overseas merchants.
- Recurring subscription payments.
- Possible refund handling or account maintenance costs.

For a business, estimate the costs across all employees and cards. For an individual, consider your most common subscription currencies and how frequently you make purchases.

## Why the Exchange Rate Matters

A provider may not use the same exchange rate that appears in a search-engine currency converter. Rates can vary by timing, card network, issuer, and provider markup.

Before making a payment in another currency:

1. Check which currency your card balance uses.
2. Review the provider's foreign-currency and conversion rules.
3. Confirm whether the merchant offers its own currency conversion.
4. Compare the final checkout amount with the amount shown by your card provider after processing.

If a merchant offers to charge you in your home currency, compare that conversion with the card's conversion method where possible. The option that looks more familiar is not automatically the cheaper one.

## Are Free Virtual Cards Really Free?

“Free” can refer to one specific part of the service, such as card creation. It does not necessarily mean that funding, currency conversion, transactions, or account maintenance are also free.

Read the full fee schedule and the account terms. If an important fee is unclear, ask the provider for an explanation before depositing money or relying on the card for a recurring payment.

Also distinguish between a fee and a minimum funding requirement. A minimum balance is not automatically a service charge, although it can affect how much money you need to keep available.

## Questions to Ask Before Choosing a Provider

Use this checklist when comparing virtual card services:

- Is there a card issuance fee?
- Is there a fee to add or withdraw funds, where supported?
- Which exchange rate is used for foreign-currency transactions?
- Is a foreign-transaction or conversion markup charged?
- Are declined payments charged?
- How are refunds handled?
- Are there monthly, maintenance, or inactivity fees?
- Do fees differ by card type or account tier?
- Where can I find the official, current fee schedule?

Do not rely solely on promotional pages or old reviews. Fee schedules can change, and terms may differ by region, card programme, or account status.

## Virtual Card Fees for Business Use

Businesses should also consider administrative costs. Multiple cards may help teams separate software, advertising, travel, and other expenses, but the value depends on the controls and reporting actually provided by the chosen service.

Before issuing cards to a team, compare per-card costs, transaction charges, currency conversion, available reporting, and the time needed to reconcile expenses. Confirm which controls are included rather than assuming every provider offers the same features.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do all virtual cards have fees?

No. Fee structures vary by provider and card programme. Some services charge for certain actions, while others may not. Review the applicable fee schedule.

### Is a virtual card cheaper than a physical card?

Not automatically. The answer depends on issuance costs, funding, conversion, transaction fees, and how you use the card. Compare the total expected cost of both options.

### Can I avoid currency conversion fees?

It depends on the card and the currency of the transaction. Paying in the card's supported currency may avoid one conversion in some cases, but merchant conversion options and provider terms vary. Check the final amount and applicable rules.

### Are refund fees always returned?

No universal rule applies. Whether transaction fees or conversion costs are returned depends on the provider, issuer, merchant, and transaction.

### Where can I find the exact fees for a particular virtual card?

Use the provider's current official fee schedule and account terms. If a fee is not clearly explained, ask customer support before using the service.

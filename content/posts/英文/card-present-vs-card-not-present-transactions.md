# Card-Present vs Card-Not-Present Transactions: Key Differences

A card payment at a supermarket checkout is different from paying for a streaming subscription online, even when both transactions use the same Visa or Mastercard.

Payment professionals commonly describe these as **card-present (CP)** and **card-not-present (CNP)** transactions. The distinction affects how payment information is collected, how the transaction is authenticated, and how merchants manage fraud risk.

For customers using physical or virtual cards, understanding these categories can also explain why a card accepted in one situation may be rejected in another.

## What Is a Card-Present Transaction?

A card-present transaction generally occurs when a payment card is presented to a merchant through an in-person acceptance environment.

Common examples include:

- Inserting a chip card into a payment terminal.
- Tapping a contactless card at a supported terminal.
- Using a physical card with an appropriate point-of-sale terminal.

The terminal and payment infrastructure use the supported card-reading method to generate the transaction data required for authorisation.

Depending on the transaction, the customer may need to enter a PIN, provide another form of verification, or complete a contactless payment without additional interaction.

Card-present transactions are not automatically risk-free. Lost or stolen cards, terminal fraud, and other security issues can still occur. The relevant protections depend on the card technology, terminal, issuer, merchant, and applicable rules.

## What Is a Card-Not-Present Transaction?

A card-not-present transaction generally occurs when the physical card is not presented to the merchant's payment terminal.

Examples include:

- Buying products from an online store.
- Paying for a software subscription on a website.
- Entering card details into a supported mobile app.
- Making a telephone order where the merchant manually enters the payment details.

In these cases, the merchant may collect card details through an online checkout or another supported payment channel. Depending on the payment setup, additional verification may be required.

Many virtual card transactions are card-not-present transactions because the card is used for online purchases rather than presented as a physical card at a terminal.

However, a virtual card's ability to work with a particular merchant depends on the card product, merchant acceptance rules, issuer controls, and any required authentication.

## The Main Differences

| Feature | Card-present | Card-not-present |
|---|---|---|
| Typical environment | In-person checkout | Online, app, or remote order |
| Card interaction | Often chip or contactless terminal | Usually digital entry or stored credentials |
| Common examples | Shops, restaurants, physical retail | E-commerce, software subscriptions |
| Verification | Terminal-based methods and applicable checks | Card details, risk checks, and possible online authentication |
| Main fraud concerns | Lost or stolen cards, terminal-related risks | Stolen card details, account misuse, and remote fraud |
| Virtual card suitability | Depends on product and supported acceptance method | Often relevant for online card purchases |

These are general patterns, not universal rules. Payment systems can support different transaction types and verification methods.

## Why Are Card-Not-Present Payments Treated Differently?

In an online transaction, the merchant generally cannot inspect a physical card or rely on a card being inserted into a terminal. The merchant therefore uses other information and controls to evaluate the transaction.

These may include:

**Card verification information.** The merchant may request the card's security code where permitted and appropriate.

**Billing information checks.** Some merchants use address verification or other data to help assess risk.

**3D Secure authentication.** Depending on the card, merchant, region, and transaction, an online payment may require an additional authentication step. See our explanation of [3D Secure authentication](https://heimeiqiu-arch.github.io/posts/what-is-3d-secure-authentication/).

**Risk assessment.** Merchants and payment providers may evaluate transaction patterns, device signals, account history, and other available information.

**Issuer authorisation.** The issuer evaluates the payment request under its applicable rules and controls.

These mechanisms can help reduce risk, but none guarantees that every transaction will be approved or that fraud will be eliminated.

## Why Might a Card Work In-Store but Fail Online?

A card may work at a physical checkout but fail on a website for several reasons.

### 1. The merchant uses a different acceptance setup

A physical terminal and an online checkout do not necessarily support the same card types, transaction categories, or verification methods.

### 2. The website requires additional verification

An online merchant may require a billing address, security code, or authentication step that was not needed for the in-person purchase.

### 3. The issuer applies transaction-specific controls

Issuers may evaluate online and in-person payments differently. A transaction can be declined because of risk controls, account settings, available funds, or other restrictions.

### 4. The merchant restricts certain cards

Some merchants limit accepted cards by issuing region, product type, or other eligibility criteria. A card that works at a local shop may not meet the online merchant's requirements.

### 5. The card is not designed for that use

Certain card products support only specific transaction types. For example, a card designed for online purchases may not support in-person terminal transactions or digital wallet provisioning.

When troubleshooting, check the merchant's supported payment methods, confirm your card details, review any authentication request, and contact the issuer if the reason for the decline is unclear.

## Can a Virtual Card Be Used for Card-Present Payments?

Not every virtual card supports in-person payments.

A virtual card may be intended primarily for online transactions. Some products can also be added to a supported digital wallet and used at compatible contactless terminals, but that depends on the issuer, card network, wallet, device, and region.

Do not assume that having a virtual Visa or Mastercard automatically means it can be added to every wallet or used at every contactless terminal. Confirm the product's current features and eligibility requirements.

## Does Card-Not-Present Mean Less Secure?

Not necessarily. The transaction environment changes the types of risks and the methods used to manage them.

Online payments can use tokenisation, encryption, authentication, fraud monitoring, and issuer controls. In-person payments can use chip technology, contactless security mechanisms, PIN verification, and other protections.

Security depends on how these controls are implemented and whether the customer, merchant, and payment providers use them correctly.

Customers should use trusted checkout pages, avoid sharing card credentials unnecessarily, monitor transaction notifications where available, and report suspicious activity promptly.

## What This Means for Overseas Online Payments

When paying for international software, AI services, digital products, or recurring subscriptions, the transaction is usually processed through a card-not-present channel.

Before paying, check:

- Whether the merchant accepts your card network and card type.
- Whether the card supports international and online transactions.
- Whether the billing information matches the issuer's records.
- Whether the transaction currency and fees are acceptable.
- Whether the merchant requires 3D Secure or another verification method.

For eligible online payments, you can review the card options available through [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/). Confirm the current product terms, supported transaction types, fees, and merchant restrictions before use.

## Final Thoughts

Card-present and card-not-present transactions differ mainly in how the card is presented and how the payment is accepted. In-person transactions often use chip or contactless terminals, while online transactions rely on digital payment information and remote verification.

Knowing the difference helps explain why a card may work at one checkout but fail at another. It also helps customers choose a suitable payment method and troubleshoot declines more effectively.

A card payment can be declined even when the card appears to have funds. Business cards may have several independent controls: a per-transaction cap, daily limit, monthly budget, or merchant restriction. A payment can be below the available balance but still violate one of these rules.

## Per-transaction, daily, and monthly limits
A per-transaction limit sets the maximum amount for one payment. An employee may have a $2,000 monthly budget but a $500 single-payment cap; a $650 equipment purchase can fail even if little has been spent. A daily limit controls cumulative spending during a defined period. A monthly budget can be exhausted by subscriptions, advertising, or seasonal purchases. Reset times and treatment of pending authorizations depend on the provider.

## Pending authorizations
Hotels, rental companies, and some online services may place estimated authorization holds that reduce available capacity before the final amount is known. Ask the issuer whether pending authorizations count toward the relevant limit.

## Merchant restrictions
A small payment can fail despite sufficient funds if the merchant or category is not permitted. Increasing the spending limit may not help because the restriction is not amount-based.

## Troubleshoot systematically
Confirm amount and currency, check available funds, review the transaction cap, inspect daily and monthly totals, look for pending authorizations, and check merchant restrictions. Then review the decline message or contact the issuer. Avoid repeated retries without identifying the cause.

Finance should verify that the purchase is legitimate and within the employee's authority before changing settings. If approved, consider a temporary adjustment and record the reason, approver, amount, and period. Businesses evaluating virtual or physical cards, including CBPayments, should review available controls, funding arrangements, merchant acceptance, and transaction policies.

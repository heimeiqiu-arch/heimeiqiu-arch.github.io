# What Is an Estimated Authorization in Card Payments?

When a customer checks into a hotel or collects a rental vehicle, the merchant may not know the final amount that will be charged.

The customer may incur additional expenses during the stay or rental period.

Instead of waiting until the final bill is known, the merchant may request an authorization based on an estimated amount.

This is known as an **estimated authorization** in certain card-payment contexts.

## What Is an Estimated Authorization?

An estimated authorization is an authorization request based on an estimated transaction amount rather than a fully determined final amount.

It may be used in certain industries where the final cost is not known at the beginning of the service.

Examples include:

- Hotel stays.
- Vehicle rentals.
- Cruise services.
- Other eligible travel or hospitality transactions.

The availability and required indicators depend on the card network, merchant category, transaction type, and processor configuration.

## Why Do Merchants Use Estimated Authorizations?

### The Final Amount Is Not Yet Known

A hotel may not know the guest's total incidental expenses at check-in.

A rental company may not know the final rental cost until the vehicle is returned and the contract is completed.

### The Merchant Needs Payment Assurance

The merchant may want to establish that the card account can support an expected amount before providing the service.

### The Transaction May Change During Fulfilment

The final amount may increase or decrease depending on the actual service provided.

An estimated authorization can support the initial payment arrangement, subject to the applicable rules.

## How Does It Work?

A simplified process is:

1. The customer begins a service or rental.
2. The merchant calculates an estimated amount using its pricing and payment policy.
3. The merchant submits an authorization request using the appropriate transaction information.
4. The issuer evaluates the request.
5. If approved, the amount may be reserved against the cardholder's available balance or credit.
6. The final amount is determined when the service is completed.
7. The merchant processes the final transaction according to the applicable authorization and capture rules.

The estimated amount is not necessarily the final amount collected.

## Estimated Authorization vs. Final Charge

An authorization is a payment approval and may reserve funds.

The final charge is the amount that the merchant ultimately processes for payment.

For example, a hotel may request an estimated authorization of $500. At checkout, the final eligible bill may be $430.

The merchant should process the final amount according to the applicable network and provider rules. Any unused authorization amount should be handled through the appropriate adjustment, reversal, or release process.

## Estimated Authorization vs. Incremental Authorization

An estimated authorization establishes an initial amount based on an estimate.

An incremental authorization is an adjustment or increase to an existing authorization where supported.

A merchant may use both concepts during an extended service, but they are not the same operation.

## What Should Customers Know?

Customers should understand that an authorization hold is not necessarily a completed charge.

The amount reserved may reduce the available balance temporarily, even though the final charge has not yet been processed.

The final amount can differ from the estimated amount if the merchant's agreement and applicable rules permit the additional or reduced charge.

## What Should Merchants Consider?

Merchants should calculate estimates reasonably and explain their payment policies before the transaction.

They should also:

- Use the correct transaction indicators.
- Monitor authorization validity.
- Keep records of the estimate and final bill.
- Process the final amount accurately.
- Release unused authorization amounts where required.
- Explain changes to customers clearly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an estimated authorization a guaranteed final charge?

No. It is an authorization based on an estimated amount. The final amount depends on the actual service and applicable payment rules.

### Can the final charge be higher than the estimate?

It may be possible in certain permitted scenarios, but the merchant must follow the applicable authorization rules and customer agreement.

### Why is the estimated amount still pending after checkout?

The authorization and final transaction may update at different times. The merchant's processing and the issuer's display can affect when the pending amount changes.

Estimated authorizations help merchants handle services where the final amount is not immediately known. Clear communication and accurate final processing are essential to avoid confusion.

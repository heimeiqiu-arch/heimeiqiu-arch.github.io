# What Is a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR)?

When a business accepts a card payment, the amount it receives may be lower than the amount the customer paid. The difference can include fees charged for accepting and processing the transaction. One term used to describe this overall cost is the **merchant discount rate**, commonly shortened to MDR.

Understanding MDR helps a business interpret its payment agreement, compare providers, and estimate the real cost of accepting cards. ## What Does Merchant Discount Rate Mean?

The merchant discount rate is the fee or overall rate charged to a merchant for processing eligible card transactions. It is commonly expressed as a percentage of the transaction value, although the exact pricing structure and included charges depend on the provider, market, card network, and merchant agreement.

For example, if a provider applies a 2% MDR to a $100 transaction, the percentage-based fee would be $2. This is a simplified illustration, not a quote or a universal rate. Some agreements also include fixed per-transaction charges or separate fees that are not included in the headline rate.

## What Can Be Included in MDR?

A merchant's card acceptance cost may reflect several components:

- **Interchange:** A fee associated with the card transaction and generally paid within the acquiring ecosystem to the card issuer.
- **Card network fees:** Fees assessed by a card network for services and network participation. Their structure varies.
- **Acquirer or processor markup:** The amount charged by the acquiring institution or payment provider for its services.
- **Other pricing elements:** Depending on the contract, fixed transaction charges or other service fees may be billed separately rather than included in MDR.

These components should not be treated as identical. In particular, interchange and network assessment fees are distinct categories, even when a provider groups them into a single blended price.

## How Is MDR Calculated?

A simple percentage-based example is:

**MDR = merchant processing fees ÷ processed card sales × 100**

Suppose a merchant processes $20,000 in card sales and incurs $500 in fees that are included in its MDR calculation. Its effective rate for that period is 2.5%.

The calculation is only meaningful when the numerator and denominator are defined consistently. Check whether refunds, chargebacks, fixed fees, taxes, currency conversion, and non-card payment methods are included in the provider's reported figure.

## MDR vs. Interchange Fee

Interchange is one component of card acceptance economics. MDR is a merchant-facing measure of the overall rate charged under a particular pricing arrangement. A merchant should not assume that its MDR equals the interchange rate, or that every fee on its statement is an interchange fee.

## How Should a Business Evaluate Its MDR?

Look beyond the advertised percentage. Review the card mix, average transaction value, domestic and international sales, refund levels, fixed charges, monthly fees, currency conversion, and any minimum commitments. Ask the provider for a worked example based on your actual transaction profile.

A low headline rate may not produce the lowest total cost if other charges are significant. Conversely, a higher blended rate may include services that would otherwise be billed separately.

For a clearer comparison, examine several months of statements and calculate the effective cost using the same method for each provider. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is MDR the same as a processing fee?
Not always. MDR commonly refers to a merchant's overall card acceptance rate, while a processing fee may refer to one component or a broader collection of charges.

### Is MDR fixed for every card?
No. Pricing may vary by card type, transaction location, channel, merchant category, risk profile, and contract.

### Can a merchant negotiate MDR?
Some providers review pricing based on transaction volume, business profile, and processing history. Any revised offer should be compared using the full fee schedule, not only the headline rate.

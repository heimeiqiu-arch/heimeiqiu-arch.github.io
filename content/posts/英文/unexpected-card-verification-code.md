# What to Do If You Receive an Unexpected Card Verification Code

Receiving a card verification code when you are not making a payment can be unsettling. The message might be caused by a mistyped phone number, an attempted transaction, or someone trying to use your card or account details.

The code alone does not prove that fraud has occurred. However, it is a reason to pause and check whether the activity is legitimate.

## 1. Do Not Share the Code

The most important rule is simple: never share an unexpected verification code with another person.

A fraudster may contact you by phone, email, or messaging app and claim to be a merchant, bank employee, or security representative. They may say that they need the code to cancel a transaction or protect your account.

Do not provide it. An authentication code can authorize a particular action, depending on the service that issued it. Giving it to someone else may allow them to complete an action you did not intend.

Use your card provider's official contact details if you need help.

## 2. Check Whether You Recently Started a Transaction

Before assuming that someone is using your card, consider whether you recently:

- Started an online checkout.
- Added a card to a merchant account.
- Changed a payment method.
- Signed in to a service that uses verification codes.
- Requested a password reset or account change.

Some verification messages arrive later than expected. If you did not initiate the relevant action, do not approve it simply because the message appears genuine.

## 3. Avoid Links in Suspicious Messages

An unexpected code may be followed by a message asking you to click a link, confirm a payment, or call a number.

Instead of following the instructions in an unsolicited message, open the official website or application independently. Check your account activity there.

Be cautious if the message creates urgency, threatens immediate account closure, or asks you to install software. These are common warning signs of social engineering.

A genuine-looking sender name is not sufficient proof that a message is authentic.

## 4. Review Your Card and Account Activity

If you receive a code you did not request, check your recent card transactions and relevant account activity.

Look for unfamiliar purchases, new payment methods, unexpected account changes, or login notifications. If you see a transaction you do not recognize, contact your card provider through an official channel.

If your provider offers a card lock or freeze feature, ask whether using it is appropriate while the issue is investigated. Follow the provider's instructions about replacing the card or securing the account.

Do not assume that the absence of a completed charge proves that no attempt occurred. Equally, do not assume that every unexpected code means your card has been compromised.

## 5. Secure the Account Connected to the Card

If you suspect someone has accessed your shopping or payment account, change its password from the official website or application.

Use a unique password that you do not use for other services. Enable multi-factor authentication when available, and review account recovery details to make sure they still belong to you.

If you reused the same password elsewhere, update those accounts as well. Secure your email account, because it may be used to reset other passwords or receive security alerts.

## 6. Understand the Difference Between a Code and a Payment

A verification code is not necessarily proof that a payment has been completed.

Depending on the system, a code may be used to authenticate a transaction, verify account access, or confirm another action. A transaction may still be declined, remain pending, or require further processing after authentication.

Check your transaction history and merchant order status rather than relying only on the message. If you see an unfamiliar charge, report it even if the verification process appeared to fail.

## 7. When Should You Contact Your Card Provider?

Contact your card provider promptly if:

- You see an unfamiliar transaction.
- You receive repeated codes you did not request.
- Someone asks you to disclose a code.
- Your card or account settings appear to have changed unexpectedly.
- You entered card details on a suspicious website.

Use the phone number printed on your card or the provider's official website or application. Keep relevant messages and transaction references in case they are needed for an investigation.

If you use virtual cards for online purchases, review the available card controls and reporting process in advance. Providers differ in how they handle card locking, replacement, and disputed transactions. You can compare relevant virtual and physical card features at [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/), while checking the current terms for the specific card product.

## Final Thoughts

An unexpected verification code should be treated as a warning to check, not as automatic proof of fraud. Never share it, avoid suspicious links, and review your account activity through official channels.

If anything looks unfamiliar, contact your card provider promptly. Acting carefully can help protect your account without giving a potential fraudster more information.

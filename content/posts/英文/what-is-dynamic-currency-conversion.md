When you pay by card in another country, a terminal may ask whether you want to pay in the merchant's local currency or your home currency. The home-currency option can feel easier because the amount is familiar, but it may use an exchange rate that costs more. This service is called Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC).

## What DCC means

DCC allows an international cardholder to see and accept a transaction converted into the cardholder's home currency at the point of sale. For example, a visitor whose card account is in US dollars may be buying something for JPY 10,000 in Japan. The terminal may offer the choice of paying JPY 10,000 or accepting a displayed USD amount.

If the customer chooses local currency, the transaction is submitted in that currency and the card's normal conversion arrangements generally determine the eventual home-currency amount. If the customer accepts DCC, the DCC provider calculates the converted amount before the transaction is submitted.

## Why the DCC amount can be higher

A DCC provider may apply a markup to a reference exchange rate. That markup can make the offered home-currency amount less favorable than the conversion available through the card issuer or payment network. The difference is not necessarily a separately itemized fee; it may be built into the exchange rate.

For illustration, suppose a purchase has a reference converted value of USD 100, while the terminal offers USD 105. The USD 5 difference represents a 5% increase in this hypothetical example. It is not a universal DCC rate. Actual pricing varies by provider, merchant, currency pair, and transaction conditions.

A separate foreign transaction fee may also apply under the card agreement. Do not assume that choosing local currency removes every fee, or that accepting DCC avoids all issuer charges in every situation.

## How to choose at checkout

Before confirming, check the original local-currency amount, the offered home-currency amount, the exchange rate, and any disclosed markup. Compare the offer with your card's foreign transaction terms if you know them. If the terminal does not provide enough information to make a meaningful comparison, paying in local currency is often a sensible default because it avoids automatically accepting the merchant-provided conversion.

Keep the receipt. It should show the currency selected and the amount submitted. If the receipt shows a currency you did not knowingly choose, raise the issue with the merchant promptly and contact your issuer if the transaction appears inconsistent with your authorization.

## DCC on websites

Online merchants may also let customers select a display currency. However, a website showing a price in your preferred currency is not necessarily using traditional DCC. It may simply price products in multiple currencies or use a payment provider to convert the amount. Check the currency that will actually be charged before submitting payment.

## What merchants should disclose

Businesses offering DCC should make the choice clear and voluntary. Show the original amount, converted amount, exchange rate, and applicable markup before the customer confirms. Receipts should identify the transaction currency. Clear disclosure helps customers make an informed choice and reduces confusion and payment disputes.

## Final thoughts

DCC offers convenience, not necessarily the best exchange rate. Compare the displayed rate and your card's terms rather than choosing a familiar currency automatically.

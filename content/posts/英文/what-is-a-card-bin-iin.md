# What Is a Card BIN? Understanding the First Digits of a Card Number

When you enter a payment card online, the merchant may use information from the beginning of the card number to help identify the payment card and route the transaction.

This information is commonly known as the Bank Identification Number, or BIN. The term Issuer Identification Number, or IIN, is also used.

Understanding BINs can help explain why some websites identify a card's network or issuing region before a payment is completed, and why a card may not be accepted by every merchant.

## What Is a Card BIN?

A card BIN is the initial portion of a payment card number used to identify information associated with the card's issuer and payment program.

Historically, BIN commonly referred to the first six digits of a card number. Modern payment-card numbering systems can use an eight-digit IIN, so it is not always accurate to assume that every BIN consists of exactly six digits.

The BIN or IIN is part of the card number structure. It does not reveal the cardholder's full identity, and it should not be treated as a complete description of the card's current capabilities.

## What Can a BIN Tell You?

Depending on the available database and the card program, BIN information may help identify:

- **Card network:** The payment network associated with the card, such as Visa or Mastercard.
- **Issuer or issuing institution:** The institution or program associated with the card range.
- **Card type:** Some databases classify cards as debit, credit, prepaid, or another product type.
- **Issuing country or region:** Reference data may indicate where the card range was issued.
- **Product classification:** Some databases include additional information about the card product.

Not every database contains all these fields, and the information may be incomplete or outdated. A BIN lookup should therefore be treated as an indication rather than a guarantee.

## How Is BIN Information Used in Online Payments?

Merchants and payment providers may use BIN information as one input in their payment workflows.

### 1. Identifying the card network

The initial card digits can help a checkout system identify the relevant payment network and apply the corresponding processing rules.

### 2. Supporting payment routing

Payment providers may use card-related information to determine how a transaction should be routed through supported processing arrangements.

### 3. Applying risk checks

A merchant may compare card information with transaction details as part of its fraud-prevention process. BIN data is only one possible signal; it cannot independently prove that a transaction is legitimate or fraudulent.

### 4. Checking payment compatibility

Some merchants accept only certain card types, issuing regions, or payment products. BIN information may help the merchant determine whether a card appears compatible before submitting the payment.

The final acceptance decision may still depend on issuer authorization, authentication, merchant policy, and other transaction details.

## BIN vs. the Full Card Number

A BIN is only one part of a card number. The complete number contains additional digits used within the payment-card numbering system, including a check digit in common card-number formats.

The BIN alone is not sufficient to make a payment. A transaction normally requires the relevant card credentials and other information required by the merchant and payment processor.

Do not share your complete card number, security code, passwords, or one-time authentication codes with an untrusted BIN lookup service.

## Why Might a BIN Lookup Show Incorrect Information?

BIN databases are not always perfectly current or complete. A lookup tool may show a different issuer name, card type, or country from what you expect.

Possible reasons include:

- The database has not been updated.
- A card program is operated through a partner or issuing institution.
- The card range is classified differently by different providers.
- The card product has characteristics that a basic lookup tool does not capture.
- The database uses an outdated six-digit classification for a range that requires more detailed identification.

For these reasons, do not rely on a single lookup result to determine whether a card is genuine or whether a merchant must accept it.

## Does a BIN Determine Whether a Card Will Work?

No. BIN information does not guarantee acceptance.

A card can have a recognizable BIN and still be declined because of insufficient available funds, issuer restrictions, incorrect payment information, unsupported merchant categories, authentication requirements, or suspected fraud.

Likewise, a card may be legitimate even if a free BIN database does not recognize its range.

If a payment fails, review the merchant's requirements and the issuer's response rather than assuming the BIN is the only cause.

## What Should Virtual Card Users Know About BINs?

Virtual cards also have card numbers and associated issuing information. Their BIN data may help identify the card program or network, but it does not reveal every restriction attached to the card.

Before using a virtual card, check whether the merchant accepts the card type, issuing region, transaction currency, and required authentication method. A BIN lookup cannot confirm all these details.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a BIN always six digits?

No. Six digits were historically common, but modern card-number systems also use eight-digit issuer identification numbers. The exact interpretation depends on the numbering standard and card range.

### Can a BIN lookup reveal the cardholder's name?

A standard BIN lookup identifies information about a card range, not the individual cardholder's identity. Be cautious of services claiming to reveal private cardholder information.

### Does a BIN tell me whether a card has money available?

No. BIN data does not provide the card's available balance or guarantee that a transaction will be approved.

### Can two cards have similar BIN information?

Yes. Cards within the same range may share issuer or product characteristics while belonging to different cardholders or accounts.

## Final Thoughts

A card BIN or IIN helps identify the issuing information associated with a payment-card range. It can support payment routing, card classification, and some compatibility checks, but it cannot determine every feature of an individual card.

Use BIN information as a reference, not as proof of card validity or payment approval. For a declined transaction, the merchant's requirements and issuer response are usually more useful than the BIN alone.

Check service periods, transaction fees, taxes, adjustments, and credits when reconciling provider invoices.

A reliable software spending process connects each service to a clear owner, budget, and business purpose. Reviewing the records regularly helps teams spot unused tools, duplicate plans, and unexplained invoice changes.

## Build a useful inventory

Record the provider, service name, business purpose, owner, cost centre, billing frequency, renewal date, and cancellation deadline. For usage-based services, also record the relevant quota or metering unit. Store the inventory in a location with appropriate access controls.

## Review costs systematically

1. Compare the current invoice with the previous period.
2. Identify changes in seats, usage, plan tier, fees, or currency.
3. Check whether duplicate services cover the same need.
4. Confirm each subscription still has an active owner.
5. Save supporting invoices, receipts, and approval notes.

## Handle discrepancies

Ask the provider to explain unexplained line items, credits, or adjustments. Compare invoices with account usage and transaction history. If a card payment appears to have failed, verify whether it succeeded before trying again.

Use official billing portals and protect payment credentials. Acceptance of virtual cards varies by merchant, so check supported methods before relying on one.

## FAQ

### How often should software subscriptions be reviewed?
A monthly invoice review plus a deeper review before renewal is a practical starting point; adapt the frequency to usage and risk.

### What should be retained?
Keep invoices, receipts, approval notes, usage reports, renewal dates, and records explaining changes.

## Final takeaway

Give every subscription an owner, compare expected and actual costs, and keep evidence for each material change.

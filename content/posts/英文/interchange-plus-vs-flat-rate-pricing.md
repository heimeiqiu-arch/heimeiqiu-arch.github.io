# Interchange-Plus vs Flat-Rate Pricing: Which Is Better?

When choosing a payment processor, the advertised rate is only one part of the decision. The pricing model determines how processing costs are presented, which fees are visible, and how much the final bill may vary with your transactions.

Two common approaches are **interchange-plus pricing** and **flat-rate pricing**. Neither is automatically cheaper for every business. The right choice depends on transaction volume, card mix, average ticket size, sales channels, and how much pricing detail your team wants.

## What Is Interchange-Plus Pricing?

Interchange-plus pricing separates the underlying interchange and applicable network costs from the processor's markup. A quote may show interchange plus a fixed percentage and/or per-transaction fee, with other charges listed separately.

This structure can make the provider's markup easier to identify. However, the total cost can vary because interchange and network fees depend on applicable card, transaction, and market factors. The statement may also contain additional service fees.

## What Is Flat-Rate Pricing?

Flat-rate pricing charges a stated rate or set of rates for eligible transactions. The provider combines several underlying cost components into a simpler price.

This can make budgeting and forecasting easier, particularly for smaller merchants with relatively straightforward payment needs. The trade-off is that the merchant may have less visibility into the underlying cost components, and the same flat rate may not be equally economical for every card type or transaction.

## Key Differences

| Factor | Interchange-Plus | Flat-Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing visibility | Separates underlying costs and provider markup | Combines costs into a simpler rate |
| Month-to-month variation | Can vary with card and transaction mix | Often more predictable for eligible transactions |
| Statement complexity | Usually more detailed | Often simpler |
| Best fit | Businesses seeking cost transparency and able to review statements | Businesses prioritising simplicity and predictable pricing |
| Comparison challenge | Requires understanding the fee components | Requires checking what the headline rate includes |

These are general tendencies. Provider contracts differ, and some pricing plans combine features of both models.

## Which Model May Cost Less?

The answer depends on your transaction profile. A business with a high proportion of lower-cost domestic transactions may find a particular interchange-plus offer attractive, but the processor markup and other charges still matter. A merchant with a mixed card profile may value the predictability of a flat rate, even if some transactions would have a lower underlying cost under a different structure.

Do not compare only the percentage. Include fixed transaction fees, monthly charges, minimums, gateway fees, chargeback costs, refunds, cross-border charges, currency conversion, and any additional service costs.

## How to Compare Offers Fairly

Use the same recent transaction data for each provider. Include transaction count, average ticket, total volume, card mix, domestic versus international sales, and refunds. Ask each provider to estimate the total monthly cost using that profile.

Then calculate the effective rate:

**Effective rate = total relevant processing fees ÷ processed card sales × 100**

Make sure the fees and sales included in the calculation are consistent. If one provider excludes monthly fees and another includes them, the comparison will be misleading.

## Questions to Ask Before Signing

Ask whether the quoted price includes network fees, whether rates differ by card type or channel, how refunds are treated, what happens when the card mix changes, and whether the provider can change pricing during the contract. Request a sample statement and a full schedule of additional fees.

For merchants accepting payments in multiple countries, check how currency conversion and cross-border transactions are priced. These can materially change the total cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is interchange-plus always cheaper?
No. It offers more cost detail, but the total depends on the underlying fees, markup, and transaction profile.

### Is flat-rate pricing easier to budget?
It can be, particularly when the rate applies consistently to the merchant's transactions. Check exclusions and additional fees.

### Can a business switch pricing models?
Some providers offer different plans or may review pricing as volume changes. Check contract terms and any termination or migration costs.

Choose the model that gives your business the best combination of total cost, transparency, predictability, and support—not simply the lowest advertised rate.

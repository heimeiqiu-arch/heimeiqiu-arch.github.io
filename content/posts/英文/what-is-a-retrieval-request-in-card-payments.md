A merchant may receive a request for information about a card transaction even when no chargeback has been formally raised. This is commonly called a **retrieval request** or a request for transaction documentation.

The request is generally used to obtain information that helps an issuer or cardholder understand a transaction. It is not automatically the same as a chargeback.

## What is a retrieval request?

A retrieval request is a request for transaction information or supporting documentation relating to a card payment. Depending on the network and processing arrangement, it may be initiated through the issuer and passed to the merchant through the acquirer or payment provider.

The merchant may be asked for a sales receipt, order confirmation, transaction record, product or service description, delivery information, or other permitted documents.

## Why might an issuer request information?

A cardholder may not recognise a merchant name on a statement, may have forgotten a purchase, or may ask the issuer to investigate a transaction. The issuer may request supporting information to clarify what happened.

A retrieval request therefore does not necessarily mean that the issuer has concluded that the transaction is fraudulent or invalid.

## How does the process work?

1. **A transaction is queried.** A cardholder asks about a transaction or the issuer identifies a need for additional information.
2. **The issuer requests documentation.** The request is submitted through the relevant network or payment arrangement.
3. **The merchant is notified.** The acquirer or processor passes the request to the merchant.
4. **The merchant locates records.** The merchant searches payment, order-management, fulfilment, and customer-support systems.
5. **The documentation is submitted.** The merchant provides the requested information through the required channel and within the applicable timeframe.
6. **The information is reviewed.** The issuer or relevant parties use it to assess the transaction or continue the investigation.

Exact steps and deadlines depend on network and provider procedures.

## Retrieval request vs. chargeback

A retrieval request seeks information about a transaction. A chargeback is a formal dispute mechanism that can result in a transaction amount being reversed or debited from the merchant under applicable rules.

A retrieval request may occur before a chargeback, but the relationship is not identical across every network and dispute type. Merchants should read the actual notification rather than assume that every information request will become a chargeback.

## How should a merchant respond?

Identify the transaction using the available reference details. Retrieve the original order, receipt, payment record, and relevant delivery or service information. Make sure the response is complete, readable, and consistent with the transaction data.

If the merchant cannot locate the requested information, it should contact its acquirer or provider promptly to understand the next steps.

## What if the merchant does not respond?

Consequences depend on network and provider rules. An unanswered request may make it harder to resolve a customer enquiry and could affect a subsequent dispute process where the applicable framework allows it. Merchants should not assume that ignoring a retrieval request has no consequences.

## Prepare in advance

Maintain searchable transaction records and link payment references to order-management systems. Keep receipts, invoices, delivery confirmations, customer communications, and refund records in line with applicable retention and privacy requirements.

A consistent recordkeeping process makes it easier to respond when a transaction is questioned.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a retrieval request a chargeback?

No. It is generally a request for transaction information, although it may be part of a wider dispute investigation.

### Does it mean the customer has accused the merchant of fraud?

Not necessarily. The request may simply be intended to identify or clarify a transaction.

### Should a merchant respond?

Follow the applicable notification and provider instructions. Ignoring a request may create avoidable problems during a later investigation.

Treat a retrieval request as an opportunity to clarify a transaction with reliable records before assuming that a formal chargeback has occurred. Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) for information about current card products and services.

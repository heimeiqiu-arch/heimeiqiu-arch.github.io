The advertised price of an online purchase is not always the total cost of paying with a virtual card. Issuance fees, funding costs, foreign-currency conversion, merchant surcharges, and inactivity fees may apply depending on the provider and card product.

If you are comparing virtual card options for supported online purchases, review [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and check the current card features, fees, and eligibility for your location before applying.

## List the fees that can affect your transaction

Look for card creation or maintenance fees, top-up charges, transaction fees, currency conversion rates, international usage charges, and any minimum or fixed fee. The merchant may separately charge a processing fee, so distinguish provider fees from checkout fees.

## Compare the total cost, not one headline rate

For a fair comparison, estimate the complete cost for the amount and currency you actually plan to spend. Consider how often you pay, whether the card is reusable, how refunds are handled, and whether a minimum balance is required. A low headline fee may not be the lowest-cost choice for every usage pattern.

## Check the provider’s current terms

Read the provider’s current fee schedule and terms before funding a card. Fees can vary by region, card type, and funding method, so avoid relying on old screenshots or third-party summaries. Keep a copy of the fee information relevant to your account.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will every website accept a virtual card?

No. Acceptance depends on the merchant, card network, issuing country, card type, and provider rules. Check the merchant’s payment policy before relying on a card.

### What should I do if a payment is declined?

Check available balance, card status, expiry date, billing details, transaction limits, and any authentication request. If the cause is unclear, contact the merchant or issuer through official support.

### Is a virtual card a guarantee against fraud?

No payment method eliminates all risk. Use trusted websites, protect verification codes, monitor transactions, and contact the issuer promptly if you suspect unauthorised activity.

## Final Thoughts

A virtual card is most useful when its acceptance, limits, fees, and billing rules match the purchase you need to make. Check merchant requirements first, keep your receipts, and use official support channels if a payment or refund does not behave as expected.

An employee card audit checks whether transactions are legitimate, documented, and consistent with company policy. A payment can be authorized successfully and still be an inappropriate business expense. The audit should create reliable financial control, not begin with an assumption of wrongdoing.

## Define the scope and collect records

Decide whether the review covers a month, department, selected cardholders, or higher-value transactions. Gather card history, merchant names, dates, amounts, currencies, receipts, invoices, expense claims, approvals, budgets, and card assignments. Online purchases may need order confirmations because a statement descriptor can identify a processor rather than the service.

## Match purchases to business purpose

For each payment, ask what was purchased, who made or authorized it, and why it was necessary. Check whether the category was permitted, the amount was within authority, approval was obtained, and supporting documents were submitted. An over-limit purchase may be an approved exception; a purchase within the limit may still violate policy. Spending limits do not replace expense approval.

## Investigate exceptions fairly

Review unfamiliar merchants, repeated near-threshold payments, duplicate charges, unusual increases, missing receipts, vague descriptions, and payments after a role change. Verify transaction status before treating two entries as duplicates: one may be pending and the other settled. Ask the cardholder or manager for context. If unauthorized use is suspected, follow the issuer's reporting process promptly.

## Record outcomes and improve controls

Classify each item as approved, documentation supplied, accounting correction, duplicate referred for resolution, personal expense requiring repayment, or unauthorized transaction reported. Keep reviewer, date, evidence, decision, and follow-up. Use recurring findings to improve receipt submission, card limits, training, and subscription reviews. Routine monthly checks may suit a small business; larger organizations may combine automated monitoring with deeper exception reviews.

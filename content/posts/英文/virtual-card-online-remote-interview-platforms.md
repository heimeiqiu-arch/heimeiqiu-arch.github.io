Remote hiring platforms can charge for interview scheduling, coding assessments, video interviews, candidate management, and recruiter seats. These services may have monthly plans or usage-based fees per candidate. A virtual card may suit the platform subscription where accepted.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Compare the billing model before checkout

Check whether the plan is priced by recruiter, active job, candidate, or completed assessment. Confirm candidate-volume allowances and fees for extra interviews. Review data retention and export options as well as the payment terms, particularly before committing to an annual contract.

## Troubleshoot a declined payment methodically

If a payment fails, check available funds, card limits, billing details, and recurring-payment support. Some services require a specific payment method or account verification. Resolve declined charges through the vendor’s official billing process and avoid entering payment details into links received from unverified messages.

## Keep recurring costs under control

Assign a budget owner and reconcile the invoice against actual hiring activity. Remove unused recruiter seats and review auto-renewal dates before a hiring slowdown. Keep records of approved purchases and make sure access is removed when a recruiter leaves the organisation.

## Frequently asked questions

Can a virtual card pay for hiring software? It may, depending on the provider’s acceptance rules. What should be checked before renewal? Usage volume, extra-candidate fees, data export, and contract cancellation terms.

## Final thoughts

A virtual card can be a useful way to organise eligible online software payments, but acceptance, limits, and recurring-payment support vary by provider and card issuer.

Accessibility consultants may bill for audits, remediation reviews, training, or ongoing monitoring. A virtual card may work for online invoices if the consultant accepts it, but the contract should define the deliverables clearly.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Agree on the scope
Clarify the website or app covered, testing methods, standards referenced, report format, remediation guidance, and follow-up review. Check whether the fee includes manual testing with assistive technologies or only automated scans. Payment does not guarantee that every accessibility issue will be found or resolved.

## Keep project and payment records together
Save the proposal, statement of work, invoice, and final report. For recurring consulting, record the renewal or milestone schedule. A dedicated card can make vendor charges easier to identify, while the contract and deliverables explain what was purchased.

## If a payment fails
Check card status, available limit, billing details, and accepted payment methods. If the consultant shows the invoice as paid but work has not started, confirm the status through the agreed official contact channel before submitting another payment.

## FAQ
**Can a virtual card pay an accessibility consultant?** It depends on the consultant’s payment options.

**Does an audit automatically make a site compliant?** No. Findings usually require remediation and follow-up testing.

**Where can I learn about virtual-card services?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and check whether the card suits the vendor’s billing method.

## Final thoughts
Define deliverables clearly and retain reports alongside payment records.

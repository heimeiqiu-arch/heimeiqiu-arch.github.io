Heatmap and session replay tools help teams understand how visitors interact with a website. Paid plans may be priced by sessions, page views, websites, or team seats. A virtual card can help track the subscription if the vendor accepts it, but teams should review privacy settings as well as cost.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Compare the billing model before checkout

Check session limits, data-retention periods, sampling rules, and the price for additional traffic. Make sure sensitive fields are masked and that collection settings follow applicable privacy requirements. Understand which features are included in the plan and whether multiple websites require separate subscriptions.

## Troubleshoot a declined payment methodically

If a payment is declined, confirm balance, limits, billing address, online and recurring-payment eligibility, and any verification prompt. Check the vendor’s accepted card methods and billing region. If an account is paused, use the official dashboard to identify whether payment, quota, or verification caused the interruption.

## Keep recurring costs under control

Review recorded-session volume and retention needs before renewal. Keep invoices with the analytics budget and limit access to recordings. Before cancelling, export reports that matter and confirm how stored recordings and collected data are handled after the subscription ends.

## Frequently asked questions

Will every session replay service accept a virtual card? No; policies differ. What should be checked besides price? Session quotas, retention, masking controls, and privacy settings.

## Final thoughts

A virtual card can be a useful way to organise eligible online software payments, but acceptance, limits, and recurring-payment support vary by provider and card issuer.

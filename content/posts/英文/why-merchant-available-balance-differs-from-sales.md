A merchant may see strong sales in its ecommerce dashboard but a much smaller available balance in its payment provider account. This can be confusing when the sales total is used to plan supplier payments or operating expenses.

The key is that sales, processed payments, settled funds, available balance, and payouts are different measures. They do not necessarily update at the same time or include the same adjustments.

## What Is a Merchant's Available Balance?

A merchant's available balance is the amount the provider currently makes available for permitted use, withdrawal, or payout. The provider may exclude pending transactions, reserved funds, disputed amounts, or other restricted balances.

The exact definition varies. Check the dashboard's balance descriptions instead of assuming that the figure equals revenue or total sales.

## Why Can Available Balance Be Lower Than Sales?

**Orders have not been captured.** An ecommerce system may count an order before its card payment is captured. An authorization approval does not always mean the merchant has completed the payment or become eligible for payout.

**Transactions are still processing.** Payments may be pending, under review, or waiting for the next processing stage.

**Fees have been deducted.** Percentage-based fees, fixed charges, conversion costs, and other contractual costs reduce net proceeds.

**Refunds and chargebacks reduce funds.** Adjustments may be recorded after the original sale and in a different reporting period.

**Reserve funds are unavailable.** Rolling or fixed reserves may appear separately and may not be eligible for immediate payout.

**Payouts have already been initiated.** A payout may leave the provider's available balance before appearing in the merchant's bank account.

## Sales, Captures, Settlement, and Balance

Treat these measures separately:

- **Sales:** Orders or commercial activity recorded by the merchant.
- **Authorized payments:** Transactions approved for the requested amount, subject to authorization conditions.
- **Captured payments:** Transactions submitted for completion and subsequent processing.
- **Settled funds:** Amounts accounted for through the applicable settlement arrangement.
- **Available balance:** Funds the provider currently makes available under its rules.
- **Payout:** Funds transferred to the merchant's bank account or nominated destination.

Different totals at each stage do not automatically indicate an error.

## How to Investigate a Difference

Identify the figures and periods being compared. A sales dashboard may use order dates, while a provider report may use capture, settlement, or payout dates.

Compare sales with captured transactions, identify pending or failed payments, review refunds and disputes, check fees and currency conversion, inspect reserve and hold balances, and match completed payouts to bank deposits.

Use transaction IDs and payout references wherever possible. Matching only dates and amounts can produce misleading results. For a broader process, see [What Is Payment Reconciliation?](https://heimeiqiu-arch.github.io/posts/what-is-payment-reconciliation/).

## Avoiding Cash-Flow Surprises

Forecast cash flow using expected net payouts rather than gross sales. Maintain a buffer for refunds, disputes, operating expenses, and payout delays. Review provider statements regularly and understand how reserve and hold terms affect availability.

If sales volume changes sharply, check whether the provider requires advance notice or additional verification.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a lower available balance mean money is missing?** Not necessarily. It may reflect pending transactions, fees, refunds, reserves, or payouts already initiated. Reconcile the records first.

**Can available balance be higher than today's sales?** Yes. It may include funds from earlier processing periods that have become available.

**Why does the ecommerce dashboard show more revenue?** Systems may count different events. Orders, authorized payments, captured transactions, and net payouts are not interchangeable.

**Should available balance be used as accounting revenue?** Not automatically. Payment-provider balances and revenue recognition serve different purposes. Follow the business's accounting policies and applicable standards.

A merchant's available balance is different from a cardholder's available card balance. [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) provides information about its card products and applicable cardholder terms.

# What Are Card Network Assessment Fees?

A merchant's card-processing statement may contain several charges that look similar but serve different purposes. One category is **card network assessment fees**—fees associated with the card network and its payment services or programmes.

Understanding these charges helps merchants avoid confusing network fees with interchange, processor markup, or other service costs. The exact fee names and calculation methods vary by network, region, transaction type, and agreement.

## What Are Assessment Fees?

Assessment fees are charges imposed by a card network under its applicable fee schedule or rules. They may be calculated using transaction volume, transaction count, region, product, or another defined basis.

They are part of the wider cost of accepting network-branded cards, but they are not the same as interchange. A merchant may see them as a separate line item in an interchange-plus statement, or they may be included within a bundled or blended price.

## Assessment Fees vs. Interchange

Interchange is generally associated with the issuer side of a card transaction and is determined under applicable network and market rules. Assessment fees are network charges. Both can contribute to the cost of card acceptance, but they are different fee categories.

A provider statement may group multiple charges together, so merchants should ask for a breakdown if the labels are unclear.

## Assessment Fees vs. Processor Markup

Processor or acquirer markup is the amount charged by a payment provider or acquiring institution for its services. It is separate from network-assessed fees, although the provider may present all charges together in a simplified pricing plan.

This distinction matters when comparing providers. A processor may have limited control over network fee schedules, while its own markup and service fees may be more directly negotiable.

## How Are Assessment Fees Calculated?

There is no single universal calculation. Depending on the network and fee category, a charge may be percentage-based, fixed, or calculated using a combination of factors. Domestic and cross-border transactions may be treated differently, and certain card products or transaction types may have separate schedules.

Avoid relying on a generic percentage found online as a universal rate. The correct amount should be checked against the current official network fee documentation and the merchant's statement.

## How Can Merchants Find These Fees?

Review the detailed fee section of your monthly processing statement. Look for network-related labels, assessment descriptions, and any supporting transaction-volume calculations. If the statement uses abbreviations, ask the provider to map each label to the underlying fee category.

Compare the statement period with the provider's processing report. Check whether the fee is based on gross volume, settled volume, transaction count, or a particular subset of transactions. Confirm how refunds and reversals are treated.

## How Can a Business Control the Overall Cost?

A merchant generally cannot simply opt out of applicable network fees while continuing to accept the relevant cards. However, it can improve visibility into the overall cost by:

- Requesting an itemised fee schedule.
- Separating network charges from interchange and provider markup.
- Reviewing the impact of international and premium card mix.
- Checking for duplicate, unexpected, or incorrectly classified charges.
- Comparing total effective cost across providers using the same transaction period.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are assessment fees paid to the card issuer?
They are network fees, not the same as interchange paid within the acquiring ecosystem to the issuer.

### Are assessment fees included in every processing quote?
Not necessarily as a separate line. Some pricing models bundle them into a blended rate.

### Can assessment fees change?
Yes. Network fee schedules and applicable rules can change. Merchants should use current official documentation and their provider's disclosures.

When reviewing card-processing costs, keep network assessments, interchange, and processor markup separate. That makes statements easier to understand and provider comparisons more reliable.

# What Is a Card Preauthorisation Hold? Why Your Available Balance Changes

Have you ever checked your banking app after booking a hotel, renting a car or paying for a service and noticed that your available balance had decreased, even though the merchant had not completed the final charge?

This may be a card preauthorisation hold. It can look like a payment has gone through, but the transaction may only be reserving funds while the merchant confirms the final amount.

Understanding how these holds work can help you avoid confusion and decide when to contact your card issuer.

## What Is a Card Preauthorisation Hold?

A card preauthorisation is a request to check whether a card can support a proposed payment amount. Depending on the card issuer and transaction arrangement, an approved authorisation can reserve part of the available funds or credit.

The reserved amount is commonly called an authorisation hold. It reduces the balance available for other transactions, even though the merchant may not yet have collected the money as a completed payment.

For example, suppose your account has 500 units of available funds and a merchant places a hold of 100 units. Your available balance may fall to 400 units while the hold remains active. The exact display depends on your issuer and account type.

A hold is not necessarily a final charge, and the amount may change before the transaction is completed.

## Why Do Merchants Use Preauthorisation?

Preauthorisation can help a merchant confirm that a payment method is usable and reserve funds before the final cost is known.

Common examples include:

- **Hotels:** A property may authorise an amount for the room, incidentals or a security deposit.
- **Car rentals:** A rental company may reserve funds against expected charges or potential additional costs under the rental agreement.
- **Fuel stations:** Some unattended terminals may request an initial authorisation before the final fuel amount is known.
- **Other services:** A merchant may use a hold where the final amount depends on usage, additional items or completion of the service.

The process and amount vary by merchant, card product, issuer and payment arrangement. Not every merchant uses preauthorisation, and a hold should be explained by the applicable terms.

## Preauthorisation Hold vs Completed Charge

These statuses are different, although both may appear in a banking app.

| Detail | Preauthorisation hold | Completed charge |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Reserves funds or checks payment availability | Collects payment for a transaction |
| Effect on available balance | May reduce available funds or credit | Usually reflects a posted transaction |
| Final amount | May be adjusted before completion | Represents the amount posted, subject to later adjustments |
| What happens next | May be completed, changed or released | May later be refunded or disputed where appropriate |

A banking app may show a hold as pending, but labels differ between issuers. If you are unsure whether a transaction is a hold or a completed payment, check the transaction details or ask your issuer.

## Why Is the Hold Amount Different from the Final Charge?

The initial authorisation may be based on an estimate rather than the exact final cost.

For example, a hotel may authorise a room amount plus a deposit, while the final payment covers only the completed stay and applicable extras. A rental company may use an initial estimate that differs from the amount ultimately charged.

A merchant may be permitted to complete a transaction for a different amount under the applicable payment rules and agreement. This does not mean every increase is automatically valid. Review the merchant's terms and final receipt if the amount looks unexpected.

## How Long Does a Preauthorisation Hold Last?

There is no single timeframe that applies to every card transaction.

The duration can depend on the merchant's processing practices, the transaction type, the card network, the issuer's policies and whether the merchant completes or reverses the authorisation.

Some holds are released relatively quickly. Others can remain visible for several days or longer, depending on the circumstances.

A merchant saying that it has released a hold does not always mean the available balance will update immediately. The issuer may still need to process the release and refresh the account display.

If the hold remains longer than the timeframe indicated by your merchant or issuer, contact them with the transaction date, amount and any reference details available.

## What Should You Do If a Hold Remains?

Follow these steps:

1. **Check the transaction status.** Determine whether the item is pending, authorised or posted.
2. **Contact the merchant.** Ask whether the authorisation is still needed or has been released.
3. **Keep supporting records.** Save receipts, cancellation confirmations and any message about the release.
4. **Contact your card issuer.** If the hold remains unexpectedly, ask whether it is still active and when your available balance may update.
5. **Report anything you do not recognise.** If you did not make the transaction, use your issuer's official support channel and follow its security instructions.

Avoid making repeated payments just because a hold is still visible. First confirm whether the original transaction is pending or completed, so you do not accidentally pay twice.

## Can a Virtual Card Have a Preauthorisation Hold?

A virtual card may receive authorisation holds if the card product and merchant support the relevant transaction type. However, support varies by provider and card configuration.

Before using a virtual card for a hotel, rental service or another merchant that may reserve funds, check whether the card supports preauthorisations and whether the available balance is sufficient for both the hold and other planned payments.

## Final Thoughts

A card preauthorisation hold temporarily reserves funds or credit while a merchant prepares to complete a transaction. It can reduce your available balance without being a final charge.

Check the transaction status, understand the merchant's terms and contact your issuer if the hold remains unexpectedly. Most importantly, distinguish a pending authorisation from a completed payment before attempting to pay again.

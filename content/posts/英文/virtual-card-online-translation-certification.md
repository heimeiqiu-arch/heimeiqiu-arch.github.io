Certified translation services may charge by page, word count, language pair, urgency, or notarization requirements. A virtual card may be accepted for online payment, but the receiving organization determines which certifications are acceptable.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Confirm acceptance requirements first
Before ordering, ask the school, court, agency, or other recipient what certification, signature, notarization, or translator credentials are required. Check the delivery format, turnaround time, spelling of names, and whether revisions are included. A payment receipt does not establish that a translation meets the recipient’s rules.

## Keep order documentation
Save the quote, source-document reference, invoice, and final delivery confirmation. For sensitive documents, review the provider’s confidentiality and retention policies. If the service bills recurring business work, use a reusable card where supported and track invoices by project.

## If payment is pending
Check the provider’s order status and card transaction history before trying again. Contact the provider through official channels if the payment appears but no order confirmation arrives.

## FAQ
**Can a virtual card pay for certified translation?** It depends on the service’s accepted methods.

**Will every institution accept the same certification?** No. Confirm requirements with the recipient before ordering.

**Where can I review card options?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and verify compatibility with the provider.

## Final thoughts
Confirm the required certification before paying to avoid costly reorders.

Using one card for personal and business purchases may seem convenient, but mixed transactions create extra work when preparing accounts or explaining payments. A statement identifies merchant and amount; it may not show whether a purchase served a business purpose.

## Use dedicated payment methods

Where practical, use one card or account for business purchases and another for personal spending. A company card should be used for approved business expenses, while private purchases should use a personal method. Dedicated payment methods make month-end review easier. Dedicated virtual cards may also help separate expense purposes, depending on provider features and terms.

## Record the business purpose

Record why each business purchase was necessary and which project or department it supports. A useful record includes date, merchant, amount, currency, purpose, project, receipt or invoice, and approval details. A merchant name alone does not prove that a transaction is a business expense.

## Correct mistakes transparently

If an employee accidentally uses a company card for a personal purchase, require prompt reporting, transaction details, and repayment or another accounting correction where appropriate. Keep the original transaction visible and record the correction rather than deleting it. The correct accounting treatment depends on the company's structure and local rules; consult a qualified accountant when needed.

## Separate subscriptions and reimbursements

Maintain a register showing service, account owner, business or personal classification, billing frequency, price, renewal date, payment method, and cancellation process. Occasional work use does not automatically make a personal service a business expense. Keep employee reimbursements distinct from company-card transactions: reimbursement settles an employee's outlay and is not a second purchase.

Review transactions monthly or weekly when volume is high. Investigate unfamiliar merchants, mixed-purpose purchases, missing receipts, duplicate charges, and unused services while details are fresh. Apply the policy consistently to owners, managers, and employees. Separating payment methods, documenting purpose, and reviewing records regularly improves reporting and reduces accounting confusion.

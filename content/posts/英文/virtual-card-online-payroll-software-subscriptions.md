Payroll software may charge by employee count, payroll run, contractor payment volume, or add-on features. A virtual card can help track subscription fees when the vendor accepts it, but it should not be confused with a method for paying employee wages.

For a general overview of virtual-card options, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and review the available product terms before choosing a payment method.

## Separate software fees from payroll funds
First determine whether the charge is for the software subscription, payroll processing, tax filing, or actual wage disbursement. These may use different payment rails and rules. A virtual card that can pay a SaaS invoice may not be suitable or accepted for funding payroll or transferring salaries.

## Set controls around plan changes
Record the account owner, employee count, billing cycle, and approved add-ons. Review charges when the company hires or offboards staff, and keep the card limit aligned with the expected invoice. Use the payroll provider’s own controls to restrict access to salary data and payment approvals.

## Resolve payment issues safely
Check card status, available balance, billing details, and provider acceptance. If a subscription fee is overdue, contact the vendor through official support. Never send employee payroll data or card credentials through unofficial channels to resolve a billing issue.

## FAQ
**Can a virtual card pay payroll software fees?** Possibly, if the provider accepts it.

**Can it necessarily fund employee salaries?** No. Wage funding follows the provider’s supported methods and applicable rules.

**Where can I review card services?** Visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) and check the card’s permitted uses.

## Final thoughts
Keep software subscriptions, payroll funding, and employee disbursements clearly separated in both permissions and accounting.

# How to Respond to an Unauthorized Online Card Transaction

Discovering a card transaction you do not recognize can be stressful. The first step is to establish whether the charge is genuinely unauthorized, then act quickly if you believe someone else used your card.

## Check the transaction details

Review the merchant descriptor, date, amount, currency, and whether the payment is pending or completed. Some merchants bill under a registered company name that differs from their store name. A payment processor may also appear instead of the retailer.

Check email receipts, shopping accounts, subscription history, and authorized family or employee card use. Do not ignore a small unfamiliar charge: criminals sometimes test card credentials with a low-value transaction.

## Lock or freeze the card if needed

If you believe the card has been used without permission, use the issuer's official application or contact channel to ask about temporarily freezing it. If the card details are compromised, ask whether it should be permanently blocked and replaced.

A temporary lock may not stop every type of pending or previously authorized transaction. Ask the issuer what the control covers, and do not assume it cancels recurring subscriptions.

## Contact the issuer promptly

Report the activity using the number printed on your card or the provider's official website or application. Give the date, amount, currency, merchant description, and reason you believe the transaction is unauthorized.

Ask whether the transaction can be disputed, whether the card should be replaced, whether related payments are pending, what evidence is needed, and whether additional account protections are recommended.

Avoid unsolicited messages claiming to recover your money. Fraudsters sometimes target people who have already experienced payment fraud.

## Secure connected accounts

If the transaction may have resulted from a compromised shopping or payment account, changing the card may not be enough. Review accounts where the card was saved, change passwords if access may have been compromised, and enable MFA where available.

Protect the associated email account too. It may be used for password resets and payment notifications.

## Preserve evidence

Keep screenshots of the transaction, confirmation messages, relevant emails, the time you noticed the payment, merchant communications, and issuer reference numbers. Preserve original messages and avoid editing evidence.

## Understand refunds and disputes

A merchant refund is normally initiated by the merchant. A card dispute is raised through the issuer or payment provider when a transaction needs investigation under the applicable rules.

If you recognize the merchant but disagree with the amount or service, check the merchant's cancellation and refund process. If the payment was genuinely unauthorized, contact your issuer without unnecessary delay. Dispute rights, deadlines, and evidence requirements depend on the card agreement and applicable law.

## Monitor after reporting

Continue reviewing pending and completed transactions. If a replacement card is issued, update legitimate recurring payment arrangements through official merchant accounts when necessary. Do not update payment details through unsolicited email links.

## Final thoughts

When a card transaction appears unauthorized, verify the details, secure the card, contact the issuer, and preserve evidence. Acting quickly can reduce further exposure and help the provider investigate. Once the immediate issue is handled, consider how the card details may have been exposed and strengthen connected accounts.

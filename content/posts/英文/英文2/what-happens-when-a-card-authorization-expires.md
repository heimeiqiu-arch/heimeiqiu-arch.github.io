# What Happens When a Card Authorization Expires?

A card payment can be approved even though the merchant has not yet collected the money.

This happens when a merchant obtains an authorization and captures the payment later. The authorization remains valid for a limited period.

If the merchant does not capture the transaction within that period, the authorization may expire.

Understanding authorization expiry helps explain why a pending card transaction can disappear and why merchants sometimes need to request a new authorization.

## What Is Authorization Expiry?

An authorization expiry occurs when the period during which an approved card authorization can be used ends before the transaction is captured or otherwise completed.

When an issuer approves a card authorization, the approved amount may be reserved against the cardholder's available balance or credit.

The reservation is not necessarily a completed charge.

If the authorization expires, the reserved amount is generally released according to the issuer and network processes.

## Why Do Authorizations Have Expiry Periods?

An authorization is not intended to remain valid indefinitely.

The validity period helps ensure that the transaction amount and the cardholder's account status remain relevant when the merchant completes the payment.

A long delay between authorization and capture can create uncertainty because the cardholder's available funds, account status, or transaction circumstances may change.

## What Happens When an Authorization Expires?

A typical sequence is:

1. The merchant requests authorization.
2. The issuer approves the transaction.
3. The approved amount is reserved.
4. The merchant does not capture the transaction before the applicable validity period ends.
5. The authorization expires.
6. The reserved amount is released according to the issuer's processing.

The exact timing of the available-balance update can vary. A cardholder may not see the release immediately in every banking app.

## How Long Does a Card Authorization Last?

There is no single expiry period that applies to every card payment.

The validity period can depend on:

- Card network.
- Transaction type.
- Merchant category.
- Merchant location.
- Card-present or card-not-present status.
- Issuer and acquirer processing rules.
- Provider-specific configuration.

Some ordinary card authorizations may have validity periods measured in days, while certain travel and hospitality transactions can follow different arrangements.

Merchants should use the validity information provided by their acquirer or processor rather than assume a universal period.

## What Should Merchants Do When an Authorization Expires?

If the merchant still needs to collect payment, it may need to obtain a new authorization.

However, reauthorization should not be treated as a simple technical retry.

The merchant should first confirm that the original transaction remains valid, that the customer is still entitled to be charged, and that the applicable rules permit a new authorization.

If the customer sees a new authorization, clear communication can help avoid confusion.

A new authorization can still be declined, and it may create a new pending transaction on the customer's account.

## Authorization Expiry vs. Authorization Reversal

These are different events.

An authorization reversal is a request to cancel an existing authorization and release the associated hold.

Authorization expiry occurs when the authorization reaches the end of its validity period under the applicable rules or system configuration.

A reversal is an active cancellation process. Expiry is generally a time-based event.

## Why Might a Pending Transaction Disappear?

A pending transaction may disappear because the merchant did not complete the capture, the authorization was reversed, or the authorization expired.

This does not always mean that the purchase was fully cancelled.

The merchant may still contact the customer or obtain a new authorization if a valid payment remains due.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does an expired authorization mean the merchant cannot charge the customer again?

Not necessarily. A new authorization may be possible where the transaction remains valid and applicable rules permit it.

### Does authorization expiry automatically mean a refund?

No. An authorization hold is not necessarily a completed charge, so releasing it is different from refunding a settled payment.

### Why does the hold sometimes remain visible after expiry?

Issuer and banking-app updates may take additional time. The display of a pending transaction does not always update at the exact moment the authorization expires.

Authorization expiry is an important part of the card-payment lifecycle. Merchants should monitor outstanding authorizations and avoid attempting captures after the applicable validity period.

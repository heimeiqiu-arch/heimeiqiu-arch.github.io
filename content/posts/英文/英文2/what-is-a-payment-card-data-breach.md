A payment card data breach occurs when sensitive payment information is accessed, disclosed, copied or stolen without authorisation.

The incident may involve a merchant, payment service provider, software supplier, online platform or another organisation that handles payment-related information. The type of information exposed varies. A breach may involve card numbers, cardholder names, expiration dates, account details or other personal information.

Some incidents expose limited information, while others involve large volumes of payment and customer records. A breach does not automatically mean every exposed card will be used for fraud, but it can create risks that require investigation and protective action.

## How breaches happen

**Compromised business systems.** Attackers may exploit unpatched software, weak passwords or poorly secured servers.

**Phishing and stolen credentials.** Employees may be tricked into revealing login information, allowing criminals to access systems that contain payment data.

**Malware.** Malicious software can capture information from compromised devices or payment environments.

**Third-party vulnerabilities.** A merchant may use external software providers, plugins or payment integrations. A weakness in a third party can expose information beyond the merchant's direct systems.

**Misconfigured storage.** Payment-related information may be exposed through incorrectly configured databases, cloud storage or access permissions.

## What information may be exposed?

Depending on the system, potentially exposed information includes cardholder names, payment card numbers, expiration dates, billing information, customer email addresses, account credentials and transaction history.

Sensitive authentication data, such as card verification codes, is subject to strict payment security requirements and should not be stored after authorisation.

The actual data involved should be established through an investigation rather than assumed.

## What cardholders should do

If a company confirms that payment information may have been exposed, follow its official guidance.

Review recent card transactions and enable transaction notifications if available. Contact the issuer if you notice unfamiliar activity. The issuer can advise whether the card should be blocked, replaced or monitored.

If account credentials were also exposed, change the affected passwords. Do not reuse the same password on other websites.

Be alert to phishing messages that refer to the breach. Criminals sometimes use real incidents to make fraudulent emails or calls appear credible.

## What businesses should do

Businesses should activate incident response procedures and determine which systems and information were affected. Actions may include:

1. Containing the incident.
2. Preserving relevant logs and evidence.
3. Investigating the scope of exposure.
4. Engaging qualified security and forensic specialists.
5. Notifying payment partners where required.
6. Assessing legal and regulatory notification obligations.
7. Communicating accurately with affected customers.
8. Correcting the vulnerabilities that caused the incident.

Reporting requirements depend on jurisdiction, industry and the type of information involved.

## How businesses can reduce risk

Minimise the amount of payment data handled directly. Use secure payment integrations and tokenisation where appropriate. Apply access controls, multi-factor authentication and regular software updates.

Conduct security assessments of payment environments and third-party providers. Employees should receive training on phishing, credential protection and secure handling of customer information.

Payment security is an ongoing operational responsibility, not a one-time technical project.

## Final thoughts

A payment card data breach can expose sensitive financial and personal information, but the actual risk depends on what was accessed and how the information can be used.

Cardholders should monitor their accounts and follow issuer guidance. Businesses should investigate quickly, communicate responsibly and strengthen the systems involved.

When a cardholder disputes a transaction, the merchant usually receives a notification identifying the category of the dispute. This classification is known as a **chargeback reason code**. It helps issuers, acquirers, merchants, and card networks organise disputes, but it is not a complete explanation of what happened.

## What is a chargeback reason code?

A chargeback reason code is a classification assigned to a disputed card transaction under the relevant card-network or payment-system framework. It indicates the general reason reported for challenging a transaction. Depending on the network, categories may concern an unrecognised transaction, goods or services not received, a product not as described, a missing refund, duplicate processing, an incorrect amount, or a processing issue.

Different networks use different code structures, descriptions, and evidence requirements. A code list from one provider should not be assumed to apply to every network.

## Why reason codes matter

The reason code helps determine which rules and evidence categories may apply. A dispute involving an unrecognised transaction may require authentication information, account activity, and customer-interaction records. A non-delivery claim may call for shipping confirmation, delivery records, or proof that a digital service was made available.

Responding with unrelated documents can weaken a merchant's case. The code should therefore be the starting point for an investigation, not a substitute for one.

## Common categories

### Fraud-related disputes

A cardholder may say they did not authorise or participate in a transaction. The merchant may need to review authentication results, device or account information, order history, and other permitted records. A successful authentication result does not automatically resolve every fraud-related dispute; its effect depends on the transaction and applicable rules.

### Goods or services not received

The cardholder claims that the purchased item or service was not delivered. Relevant evidence may include carrier tracking, delivery confirmation, fulfilment timestamps, service-access logs, or customer communications.

### Product or service not as described

The customer claims that what was received materially differed from the offer. The original product description, order confirmation, terms, and complaint correspondence may help establish what was promised.

### Processing errors

These disputes may involve duplicate processing, incorrect amounts, or other transaction-handling problems. Compare checkout records, payment logs, invoices, and settlement information.

### Refund-related disputes

A customer may claim that an agreed refund was not received. Check whether the refund was requested, submitted, and completed, and retain the original transaction reference and any refund confirmation.

## How to interpret a reason code

First, identify the network and exact code shown in the dispute notification. Read the applicable documentation supplied by the network or acquirer. Next, identify the event the customer is challenging and gather records that directly address that allegation. Finally, confirm the response deadline and submission requirements before deciding whether to accept the dispute or challenge it.

A customer's description may not match the formal category. For example, an unfamiliar merchant descriptor can lead a cardholder to report fraud even when the purchase was made through a differently named billing entity. Investigate the underlying transaction rather than assuming the code proves what happened.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a reason code prove the merchant did something wrong?

No. It identifies the reported dispute category. The facts and applicable rules determine the outcome.

### Are Visa and Mastercard reason codes identical?

No. Networks have their own dispute frameworks and code structures. Similar concepts may exist, but identifiers and procedures can differ.

### Can a merchant challenge a chargeback?

In eligible cases, a merchant may submit a response through its acquirer or payment provider. The available process depends on the dispute category and network rules.

Understanding reason codes helps merchants investigate disputes systematically instead of responding with generic explanations. For information about available card products and payment services, visit [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/).

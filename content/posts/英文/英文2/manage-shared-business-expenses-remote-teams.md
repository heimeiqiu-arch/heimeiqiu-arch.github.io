# How Remote Teams Can Manage Shared Business Expenses

Remote teams often work across different cities, countries, and time zones. Employees may use the same software platforms, purchase online services, pay for project tools, and arrange business-related travel without sharing a physical office.

This flexibility can make daily operations easier, but it also creates a financial management challenge.

When several people can purchase services for the same team, businesses may lose track of who approved an expense, which project it belongs to, and whether the company is paying for duplicate subscriptions.

A clear expense management process helps remote teams maintain control without slowing down everyday work.

## 1. Identify Which Expenses Should Be Shared

Start by listing the expenses that support the whole team rather than an individual employee.

Common examples include project management software, cloud storage, team communication platforms, video conferencing services, website hosting, domain registration, shared development tools, design and collaboration software, online advertising accounts, and business travel.

Not every expense should automatically become a shared cost.

A tool used by one department may belong to that department's budget, while a company-wide platform may be classified as a central operating expense.

Define the expense categories before introducing payment controls.

This makes it easier to assign costs to the right team and understand how much each department spends.

## 2. Assign an Owner to Every Recurring Subscription

One of the most common problems in remote businesses is paying for subscriptions that nobody actively manages.

A service may have been purchased by an employee who later changes roles or leaves the company. The subscription continues renewing, but nobody remembers why it was created.

Every recurring service should have an assigned owner.

Record the service name, account owner, department, business purpose, monthly or annual cost, renewal date, payment method, number of active users, and cancellation procedure.

The owner should periodically confirm that the service is still required.

For example, a development team may need a cloud platform throughout a project, but the requirement may disappear after the project ends.

A renewal review can prevent unnecessary spending.

## 3. Establish Expense Approval Rules

Remote teams cannot always rely on a manager being physically available to approve purchases.

Instead, create simple rules that employees can follow independently.

For example, small routine expenses may be approved by a team lead, new subscriptions may require department approval, and large purchases may require finance approval.

Unplanned expenses may need a written explanation. Certain categories may require management approval regardless of amount.

Set approval limits based on the business's size and financial responsibilities.

Avoid creating so many approval steps that employees cannot complete ordinary work.

A good policy should explain which expenses are pre-approved and which require additional authorization.

## 4. Use Separate Payment Methods for Different Purposes

Using one payment method for every business purchase can make expense tracking difficult.

A company may consider separating payment methods by department, project, or expense category.

For example, one payment method could be used for software subscriptions, another for advertising expenses, and a separate method for approved travel purchases.

Where supported, dedicated business cards or virtual cards can help separate eligible online spending.

However, payment controls vary between providers. A business should check whether its card system supports spending limits, transaction notifications, card freezing, and user permissions before relying on these features.

A payment card should not replace the company's approval process. Employees still need clear rules about what they are allowed to purchase.

## 5. Require Receipts and Business Purposes

A transaction record shows that money was spent, but it may not explain why.

For every business expense, collect enough information to establish its purpose.

A useful expense record includes transaction date, merchant, amount and currency, employee or responsible team, business purpose, project or department, receipt or invoice, and approval reference.

For recurring subscriptions, the original invoice and renewal records may be sufficient, provided the business can identify the service and account owner.

For unusual purchases, ask the employee to provide a short explanation.

Do not rely only on merchant names. Some payment descriptions are unclear or use the name of a payment processor rather than the actual service.

## 6. Create a Reimbursement Procedure

Not every business expense will be paid directly through a company account.

An employee may occasionally use a personal payment method for an approved business purchase.

If the company permits this, establish a reimbursement procedure.

The employee should submit the expense date, amount and currency, business purpose, receipt, relevant project or department, and any required approval.

Finance staff should verify the expense before reimbursement.

Set a submission deadline so that employees do not accumulate months of unreported purchases.

The reimbursement process should also explain how foreign-currency expenses are converted and recorded.

## 7. Monitor Spending Without Creating Excessive Restrictions

Expense controls should protect the business without making ordinary work unnecessarily difficult.

A useful approach is to combine reasonable spending limits with transaction visibility.

For example, a team member may be allowed to pay for approved software subscriptions up to a defined monthly amount, while purchases outside that category require approval.

Review spending patterns to identify unexpected increases, duplicate subscriptions, unused services, repeated failed transactions, purchases outside approved categories, and unusual transaction times or locations.

Transaction alerts can help teams identify issues quickly, but alerts should be reviewed in context. A legitimate employee may make purchases while travelling or working in a different time zone.

## 8. Review Shared Expenses Every Month

A monthly expense review should answer several questions: How much did each department spend? Which subscriptions renewed? Which services are no longer needed? Are there duplicate tools? Were all expenses supported by receipts? Did any employee exceed an approval limit? Are any reimbursements outstanding?

Compare the payment records with invoices and accounting records.

If a transaction cannot be explained, investigate it before closing the period.

The review should also identify subscriptions approaching annual renewal dates. Annual contracts can create large expenses that are easy to overlook when the team focuses only on monthly spending.

## 9. Update Access When Team Responsibilities Change

Remote teams change frequently. Employees join, leave, or move between departments.

Whenever responsibilities change, review payment permissions, card access, subscription ownership, approval authority, shared account access, and expense reporting responsibilities.

Remove access that is no longer required and transfer important subscriptions to an appropriate company-controlled owner.

Do not leave critical business services dependent on an employee's personal email account.

## Final Thoughts

Managing shared expenses in a remote team requires clear ownership, practical approval rules, organized records, and regular reviews.

The objective is not to prevent employees from making necessary purchases. It is to make sure that every expense has a clear business purpose, an accountable owner, and a record that finance staff can verify.

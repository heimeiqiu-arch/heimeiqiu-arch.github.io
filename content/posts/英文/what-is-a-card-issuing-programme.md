# What Is a Card Issuing Programme?

A payment card is the visible part of a much larger system. Behind a debit, prepaid, credit, or virtual card is an arrangement involving financial institutions, card networks, technology providers, and operational teams.

This arrangement is commonly called a **card issuing programme**.

Understanding how a card programme works helps explain why different cards can have different features, restrictions, supported countries, and transaction controls—even when they carry the same network brand.

## What Is a Card Issuing Programme?

A card issuing programme is the overall structure used to design, issue, operate, and manage a particular card product.

It defines how customers receive cards, how transactions are processed, which institution provides the issuing relationship, and which organisations manage the technology and daily operations.

A programme may support physical cards, virtual cards, or both.

It may also offer debit, prepaid, credit, or other card products depending on the issuing arrangement and applicable regulations.

## Who Is Involved in a Card Programme?

### Issuing Bank or BIN Sponsor

The issuing institution provides the eligible issuing relationship and the relevant network access.

In sponsored programmes, it may provide BIN sponsorship and oversee responsibilities required by the applicable rules and agreements.

### Card Network

The network provides the technical infrastructure, operating rules, and standards used by participating institutions.

### Issuer Processor

The issuer processor provides the technology for card records, transaction authorization, network connectivity, and related processing functions.

### Programme Manager

The programme manager coordinates defined parts of the programme's operations, partners, and customer-facing product.

### Technology and Service Providers

Other companies may provide identity verification, fraud monitoring, customer support, card manufacturing, fulfilment, or account infrastructure.

Not every programme uses a separate provider for every function. Some companies combine several services.

## How Does a Card Issuing Programme Work?

A typical programme may follow this structure:

1. A business defines its target customers and card product.
2. An eligible issuing institution reviews the programme.
3. The required network and BIN arrangements are established.
4. The issuer processor and other technology providers are integrated.
5. The programme configures card features, controls, and operating procedures.
6. Eligible customers complete onboarding and receive cards.
7. The programme manages card activity, support, and ongoing operations.

The exact process depends on the jurisdiction, product type, network, and programme structure.

## What Determines a Card's Features?

A card's features are not determined by its network logo alone.

They may depend on the issuing institution and programme structure, debit, credit, or prepaid product type, supported countries and currencies, merchant and transaction restrictions, spending limits, cardholder verification requirements, fees and account terms, and available physical and digital features.

Two cards carrying the same network brand may therefore have very different conditions.

## Physical Cards vs. Virtual Cards in a Programme

A card programme may issue physical cards, virtual cards, or both.

A physical card provides a tangible payment instrument that may be used at supported terminals and online.

A virtual card provides card credentials in digital form. Depending on the product, it may be created, managed, frozen, or replaced through an online interface.

The underlying issuing and authorization arrangements still matter for both formats.

## What Should a Business Evaluate Before Launching a Programme?

Businesses considering card issuance should examine the target customer and use case, supported card product and network, issuing institution and sponsorship arrangement, processor and technology capabilities, compliance and onboarding requirements, funding and settlement structure, fraud and dispute procedures, customer support responsibilities, and operating costs and commercial terms.

A card programme is not simply a software feature. It is a coordinated financial and operational arrangement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a card issuing programme the same as a card network?

No. A programme is the arrangement used to issue and manage a card product. A card network provides the infrastructure and rules for eligible transactions.

### Can one programme issue both virtual and physical cards?

Yes, if its issuing arrangement and technology support both formats.

### Does the programme manager own the cards?

The legal issuing relationship and ownership of programme responsibilities depend on the applicable structure. The programme manager is not automatically the issuing bank.

Understanding the programme behind a card helps customers and businesses evaluate its actual capabilities rather than relying only on branding.

Online purchases include software, cloud services, advertising, supplier orders, and travel. Without a defined workflow, employees may pay before approval and finance must later reconstruct the reason, approver, and accounting treatment.

## Set approval rules

Routine low-value purchases may fit an existing budget. New suppliers, higher-value purchases, annual subscriptions, and long-term commitments may need additional review. Consider amount, category, department budget, supplier status, recurring billing, contract length, data access, and renewal obligations. Make thresholds easy to understand.

## Standardize requests

Require supplier, product or service, business purpose, estimated amount and currency, one-time or recurring status, billing frequency, department or project, required payment date, and a supporting quote or invoice. Subscription requests should include renewal terms and cancellation details.

## Define who can approve

Department managers may approve routine purchases within budget, finance may review available funds and payment details, and senior management may approve high-value commitments. Smaller companies may need fewer steps; larger organizations may require procurement, IT, legal, or security review for selected categories. Avoid allowing a requester to approve their own purchase when independent review is required.

## Link approval to payment

The approver confirms that the expense is justified; the authorized cardholder or payment operator completes the transaction. Preserve the approval reference so the completed payment can be matched to the request. Card limits can reinforce the workflow where supported, but a card limit is not purchase approval.

## Keep records and review performance

After payment, attach the final receipt or invoice. Compare the approved amount with the actual charge and document differences caused by tax, currency conversion, shipping, or price changes. For recurring services, record the owner and renewal date.

Create an expedited route for urgent purchases with named approvers, amount thresholds, and follow-up documentation. Monitor approval times, rejection rates, over-budget payments, and bypasses. Simplify steps that delay routine purchases, but preserve the evidence linking request, approval, and payment.

# Does Miro Accept Virtual Cards?

A virtual card may work for a Miro subscription, but acceptance is not guaranteed for every card or account. The outcome can depend on the card issuer, card network, billing country, currency, available balance, online-payment settings, and the checks performed by Miro or its payment processor.

The safest approach is to check the payment options shown in your own Miro billing flow and confirm that your card supports the type of transaction being attempted. A virtual card is a payment-card credential issued digitally rather than as a physical plastic card; it can still have an expiration date, security code, spending limit, and other controls. For a basic explanation, see [what a virtual card is](/posts/what-is-a-virtual-card/).

## Quick answer

**A virtual card may be accepted when it is a supported card type and passes the merchant's payment checks.** Miro's available payment methods and requirements can vary by plan, location, and checkout flow. Do not assume that every virtual Visa or Mastercard will work simply because the logo matches a card network accepted at checkout.

Before trying to pay, check these points:

- The card is enabled for online and recurring payments, if the subscription renews automatically.
- The billing address and country details are accurate and consistent with the issuer's requirements.
- The card has enough available balance for the charge and any temporary authorization.
- The issuer permits the merchant category, currency, and cross-border transaction, where relevant.
- You can complete any required verification, such as a one-time passcode or 3-D Secure challenge.

## Why virtual-card acceptance can vary

### 1. The card must support the transaction

Virtual cards are not all configured alike. Some are intended for one-time purchases, while others can be reused for recurring subscriptions. A card that works for a single online purchase may still fail when a merchant attempts a renewal or account verification.

Review the card's controls before entering it into Miro. Look for recurring-payment support, online transaction permissions, geographic restrictions, and per-transaction or monthly spending limits.

### 2. Billing country and address can matter

Payment systems may compare the billing country, postal code, and address details supplied at checkout with information held by the issuer. Enter accurate information and follow the card provider's instructions. Do not invent an address or use unrelated billing details to bypass verification; mismatches can lead to a decline or later review.

### 3. The plan and checkout route may affect available methods

The payment options displayed in Miro are the most relevant guide for your account. A plan, organization, invoice arrangement, or third-party purchase route may use a different billing process. If you subscribed through an app store or another provider, you may need to manage payment details there instead of in Miro.

## How to try a virtual card for Miro

The exact labels in the interface can change, but this is a practical sequence:

1. Sign in to the Miro account or workspace that owns the subscription.
2. Open the billing, plan, or subscription settings available to your account. If you are on a team or organization plan, confirm that you have permission to manage billing.
3. Review the payment methods currently offered. If card payment is available, choose the card option.
4. Enter the virtual card number, expiration date, security code, and billing information exactly as provided by the issuer.
5. Check the amount, currency, and renewal terms before confirming.
6. Complete any issuer verification prompt and wait for the final result.
7. Return to the billing page to confirm whether the payment method was saved and whether the subscription status changed.

If you cannot see billing settings, you may lack the required administrator or billing role. Ask the workspace owner or billing administrator to review the payment method rather than repeatedly attempting to change account settings.

## What to check before paying

Use this checklist before submitting the transaction:

- **Balance:** Keep enough available funds for the displayed charge. A temporary authorization can reduce the available balance until it is released.
- **Limits:** Check single-transaction, daily, monthly, and merchant-specific limits.
- **Recurring use:** Confirm that the card can support subscription renewals, not just one-time purchases.
- **Network and region:** Verify the card's network and permitted transaction countries against the options Miro displays.
- **Verification:** Make sure you can receive or approve any authentication request.
- **Expiry:** Confirm the card will remain valid through the next expected renewal, or know how you will replace it.
- **Issuer status:** Check that the card is active and has not been frozen or restricted.

## Why a Miro payment might fail

A decline does not necessarily mean that Miro rejects all virtual cards. It may indicate that this particular transaction did not meet one of the required checks.

### Insufficient balance or a limit

The balance may be lower than the final amount, or a transaction limit may be too restrictive. Check the available balance rather than only the amount originally added to the card. If the issuer supports it, review the decline reason before making another attempt.

### Recurring payments are blocked

Some cards permit online purchases but block recurring or merchant-initiated transactions. If the initial charge succeeds but a later renewal fails, ask the issuer whether recurring transactions are supported and enabled.

### Incorrect billing information

Review the billing address, postal code, expiration date, and security code. Correct any typo before trying again. Repeatedly submitting different or inaccurate details can trigger additional fraud checks.

### Authentication was not completed

A bank or issuer may require an additional verification step. Keep the checkout window open, approve the request through the legitimate issuer channel, and return to the merchant page. If no prompt arrives, contact the issuer to check whether authentication was attempted or blocked.

### The issuer declined the merchant or cross-border transaction

The issuer may apply country, currency, merchant-category, or risk controls. The issuer is often best placed to explain a card decline. Ask for the transaction's decline reason and whether the card is permitted to pay this merchant.

### A temporary processing issue

Network interruptions or a payment processor issue can prevent a transaction from completing. Check the billing page and card activity before trying again. Avoid repeated rapid attempts, which can create multiple pending authorizations or trigger security controls.

## How to troubleshoot a declined Miro payment

Work through the steps in this order:

1. **Read the message.** Note whether Miro says the card is invalid, the payment was declined, authentication failed, or another action is required.
2. **Check the account.** Confirm the subscription owner and billing permissions are correct.
3. **Review the issuer app.** Look for a decline reason, authorization request, frozen-card status, or limit warning.
4. **Verify card details.** Re-enter only accurate card and billing information.
5. **Check transaction settings.** Confirm online purchases, recurring payments, and relevant international transactions are enabled.
6. **Check the balance and limits.** Include any pending authorizations that may temporarily reduce available funds.
7. **Retry once after fixing the cause.** Avoid repeatedly submitting the same failed payment without a change.
8. **Contact the right support team.** Ask the card issuer about issuer-side declines; ask Miro support about a billing-page or account-side issue.

## What if the charge is pending?

A pending card transaction is not always a completed payment. A merchant or processor may request an authorization while a payment is being verified. Depending on the issuer and transaction, that authorization may later be completed, reversed, or released.

If Miro shows an error but your card app shows a pending amount:

- Do not immediately submit the payment several more times.
- Check whether the subscription or invoice is marked paid in Miro.
- Save the date, amount, currency, and transaction reference.
- Ask the issuer whether the item is pending, reversed, or settled.
- Contact Miro support if the account status and card activity disagree.

A pending authorization may temporarily reduce available funds. The time required for it to disappear depends on the issuer and payment process, so check with the issuer rather than assuming a fixed release time.

## Managing renewals and replacing a card

If the virtual card is used for an ongoing Miro subscription, check its status before the next renewal. A card can expire, reach its spending limit, be frozen, or be replaced by the issuer. If you need to update the payment method, use the billing settings for the account or the third-party provider through which you purchased the plan.

After updating the details, confirm that the new method is saved. If an invoice is already overdue or a renewal has failed, check the subscription status separately; saving a new card does not always mean an outstanding charge has been paid automatically.

## Virtual cards and team billing

Miro is a collaboration product, so the person using the workspace may not be the person who controls billing. If a card option is unavailable or you cannot edit payment details, check who owns the subscription and who has billing permissions. An individual member usually should not change a company payment method without authorization.

For business use, consider assigning a dedicated card to a subscription, keeping spending limits appropriate for expected charges, and documenting who is responsible for renewal alerts. These controls can make subscription management clearer, but they do not guarantee that a merchant will approve every payment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Miro accept every virtual Visa or Mastercard?

No universal guarantee applies. Acceptance depends on the payment options available to the account and whether the specific card passes issuer and merchant checks. Confirm the options shown at checkout.

### Can I use a virtual card for a recurring Miro subscription?

Possibly, if the card supports recurring transactions and the payment is accepted. Check the issuer's rules and the subscription's billing requirements before relying on the card for future renewals.

### Why was my virtual card declined even though it has money?

A sufficient balance is only one requirement. Limits, billing-address checks, recurring-payment restrictions, authentication, issuer risk controls, or temporary processing problems can also cause a decline.

### Can I use a virtual card issued in another country?

It depends on the merchant's current payment rules and the issuer's cross-border settings. Use accurate billing details and check the options and currency displayed in your own checkout flow.

### What should I do if I see a pending charge after a failed payment?

Check Miro's subscription or invoice status and your issuer's transaction details. Avoid repeated attempts until you know whether the authorization is still pending or has settled.

### Can a team member update the subscription card?

Only if the account's permissions allow it. If billing controls are unavailable, contact the workspace owner or billing administrator.

## Final checklist

Before relying on a virtual card for Miro, confirm that card payments are offered for your account, the card supports recurring online transactions if needed, the billing details are accurate, and the balance and limits cover the expected amount. If payment fails, use the displayed error and issuer's decline information to identify the cause before retrying. Merchant and issuer rules can change, so the payment options shown in your current Miro billing flow remain the best source for your transaction.

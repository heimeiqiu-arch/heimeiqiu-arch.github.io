A business card speeds up purchasing, but access to company funds needs clear rules. A written policy explains what employees may buy, who can approve purchases, how receipts are submitted, and how mistakes are corrected.

## Define purpose and eligible users
Explain why cards are issued: approved software, supplier invoices, travel, office supplies, or other operating costs. Identify who qualifies and require a business reason for each assignment. Every card should have an owner and documented purpose.

## List permitted and restricted expenses
Give examples of eligible purchases, then identify prohibited or controlled expenses such as personal spending, unapproved gifts, cash-like transactions, or purchases creating conflicts of interest. Some categories may be normal for one team but inappropriate for another. Explain how staff request exceptions.

## Set limits and approval thresholds
Define transaction limits, daily or monthly budgets, department allocations, and approval requirements for higher-value purchases or long-term commitments. Base thresholds on purchasing history and risk. Employees should not approve their own exceptions.

## Require supporting documents
Receipts or invoices should show date, amount, merchant, description, business purpose, project, and approval reference where applicable. Set a submission deadline and explain what to do if a merchant does not provide a receipt. For subscriptions, record the owner, purpose, billing frequency, expected amount, renewal date, and cancellation process.

## Explain incident reporting
Tell employees how to report lost cards, suspected compromise, incorrect charges, and accidental personal purchases. They should contact the issuer or administrator promptly and follow official freeze or dispute procedures. Keep the policy accessible, provide a quick-reference summary, train managers, and review it annually.

When evaluating providers such as CBPayments, verify product terms, supported transaction types, funding arrangements, controls, and dispute procedures. A useful policy answers four questions: what can I buy, how much can I spend, when is approval required, and how do I document it?

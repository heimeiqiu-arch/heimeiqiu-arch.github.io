International customers often shop on websites that price products in a currency different from the one they use every day. A customer may see an estimated amount in local currency but have the transaction processed in another currency. Understanding the distinction helps prevent surprises and supports accurate records.

## Display, transaction, and account currency
Display currency is the currency shown on the website. Transaction currency is the currency in which the merchant or payment provider processes the payment. Card account currency is the currency in which the cardholder's account is maintained. These may be the same, but they do not have to be.

## Exchange rates change
A website may calculate a local-currency estimate using a rate at a particular time. The rate may change before payment is completed. Explain when converted prices are estimates and identify the currency that will actually be charged. If a merchant promises a fixed local-currency price, its pricing and payment systems must support that promise.

## Conversion fees
Depending on the arrangement, costs may include a conversion margin in the exchange rate, a foreign transaction fee from the issuer, or a fee from a payment service. Not every transaction includes all of these. Merchants should disclose charges they apply and avoid making blanket claims about fees charged by a customer's bank.

## Dynamic currency conversion
Some payment environments offer dynamic currency conversion, allowing a customer to choose between the merchant's currency and a currency associated with their card account. The rate and any markup should be disclosed. Paying in the home currency is not automatically cheaper; compare the offered rate with the issuer's conversion terms.

## Improve currency presentation
Use currency codes such as USD, EUR, JPY, or GBP rather than relying only on symbols. Identify the transaction currency, explain whether local prices are estimates, disclose merchant-applied conversion costs, and avoid switching currencies unexpectedly. Show the final amount and currency before authorization.

## Manage international pricing
Choose a primary pricing currency, define display rules, set sensible exchange-rate update intervals, and test checkout behavior in important markets. Keep original transaction amount, transaction currency, settlement amount, settlement currency, exchange rate, and conversion fees in financial records.

## Help customers review charges
Before payment, customers should check the currency shown beside the final amount and review their issuer's foreign transaction terms. After payment, compare the merchant receipt with card transaction details. If a difference remains unexplained, contact the merchant or issuer with the order reference.

## Final thoughts
Currency conversion affects what customers see, authorize, and eventually find on their statements. Clear currency codes, honest estimates, and disclosed fees make international checkout easier to understand.

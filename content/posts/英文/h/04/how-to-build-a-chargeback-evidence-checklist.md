When a merchant receives a chargeback, the response deadline may be limited. Searching different systems for receipts, delivery records, and customer messages can consume valuable time.

A **chargeback evidence checklist** helps businesses organise transaction information before a dispute occurs. It also makes it easier to identify missing records and prepare a focused response.

## Why use an evidence checklist?

Evidence is most useful when it is relevant to the specific allegation. A merchant that stores only payment receipts may struggle to respond to a non-delivery claim. A business that keeps delivery records but cannot connect them to the original order may also have difficulty demonstrating what happened.

A checklist creates a consistent recordkeeping process across the customer and payment lifecycle.

## 1. Order information

Keep the original order number, purchase date and time, product or service description, quantity and price, customer-provided order information, and relevant terms accepted during checkout. These records help establish what the customer ordered.

## 2. Payment information

Retain permitted transaction records such as the amount and currency, payment date and time, authorization and capture status, processor transaction reference, relevant response information, and refund or reversal records where applicable.

Do not store sensitive card data beyond what is permitted by applicable security standards and legal requirements.

## 3. Authentication and verification

Where applicable, maintain relevant records showing how the payment was authenticated. This may include 3-D Secure results or other permitted verification information.

Keep original statuses and associated transaction references where available. Authentication should be interpreted under the relevant rules; it is not automatic proof against every type of dispute.

## 4. Delivery and fulfilment

For physical goods, retain shipping and fulfilment records, including carrier, tracking reference, dispatch date, and delivery confirmation.

For digital products and services, keep records showing account activation, access availability, download activity, or service delivery. Evidence should correspond to the exact product or service being disputed.

## 5. Customer communication

Maintain relevant customer communications in their original context: order confirmations, support tickets, delivery enquiries, cancellation requests, refund discussions, complaints, and responses.

Preserve timestamps and avoid presenting partial conversations in a way that removes important context.

## 6. Subscription and cancellation records

For recurring services, keep subscription terms and relevant customer actions. Records may include billing frequency, renewal terms, cancellation policy, subscription start date, cancellation request, and effective cancellation date.

The merchant should be able to establish what the customer agreed to and what happened after a cancellation request.

## 7. Refund and adjustment records

If a refund or adjustment is relevant, record the original transaction reference, refund amount, request date, submission date, status, provider confirmation or reference, and customer notification.

Distinguish between a refund being requested, submitted, and completed.

## 8. Review evidence quality

Before submitting a response, check that documents refer to the same transaction, show consistent amounts and dates, are readable and complete, directly address the allegation, meet provider file requirements, and do not expose unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

A smaller set of relevant evidence is often easier to review than a large collection of unrelated files.

## 9. Create a transaction-specific folder

A practical folder can contain the original order, payment and authorization records, authentication information, delivery or service fulfilment, customer communications, cancellation and refund history, and final response and submission confirmation.

Use a consistent naming convention based on an internal order reference or dispute identifier. Limit access to staff who need the records, and follow applicable privacy, security, and retention requirements.

## 10. Track deadlines and decisions

Record the dispute notification date, response deadline, responsible staff member, reason code, evidence status, decision to accept or challenge, submission date, and final outcome.

This helps prevent missed deadlines and makes later performance analysis easier.

## Common checklist mistakes

Businesses sometimes collect payment information but forget fulfilment records. Others store screenshots without original timestamps, fail to link documents to a transaction reference, or keep refund records in a separate system that is difficult to search.

Another mistake is using one generic evidence package for every dispute. The checklist should be flexible enough to match the specific reason for each chargeback.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should every merchant use the same checklist?

No. A digital subscription business, physical retailer, travel merchant, and professional-services provider may need different fulfilment records.

### How long should records be retained?

Retention requirements depend on applicable laws, network rules, contractual obligations, and the nature of the business. Establish a documented retention policy rather than assuming one universal period.

### Does a complete checklist guarantee a win?

No. It improves recordkeeping and response preparation, but the outcome depends on the facts and applicable dispute rules.

A practical evidence checklist helps merchants respond faster and more consistently. The most important step is to maintain accurate records from the original purchase onward, rather than trying to reconstruct the transaction after a dispute arrives.

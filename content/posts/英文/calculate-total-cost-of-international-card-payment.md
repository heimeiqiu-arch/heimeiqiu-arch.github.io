# How to Calculate the Total Cost of an International Card Payment

The price shown on an overseas website may not equal the final amount deducted from your card. Currency conversion, provider fees, merchant currency choices, and temporary authorization amounts can all affect the total. A consistent calculation helps you compare payment options more accurately.

## The main cost components

### Purchase amount
Start with the merchant's final order total, including applicable taxes, shipping, and other charges. Do not calculate from a pre-tax product price if checkout shows a different amount.

### Currency conversion
If the merchant charges in a currency different from your card account's billing currency, the transaction may be converted. The rate and timing depend on the payment route and provider rules.

### Foreign transaction or provider fee
Some cards or programmes charge a percentage or fixed fee for transactions involving another currency or region. Others may not. Check the current fee schedule instead of assuming a standard rate.

### Dynamic currency conversion
Some merchants offer to charge in your home currency rather than the merchant's currency. This is called dynamic currency conversion. The exchange rate and markup offered at checkout may differ from the rate applied by your card provider. Compare the disclosed total and fee information before choosing.

### Authorization holds
Hotels, rental services, and some digital merchants may authorize an amount before final settlement. A hold can temporarily reduce available balance without representing the final purchase cost.

## A simple calculation

For an illustrative estimate, suppose a purchase costs 100 units of a foreign currency. If the exchange rate used for the transaction is 1.10 units of your billing currency per foreign unit, the converted amount is 110 units of your billing currency before any additional fees.

If a disclosed provider fee of 2% applies to that converted amount, the fee would be 2.20 units, giving an illustrative total of 112.20 units. This example is only arithmetic, not a quote or a claim about any particular card. Actual rates, fee bases, and settlement amounts vary.

## How to compare payment options

1. Confirm the merchant's final price and currency.
2. Check the card provider's current exchange-rate and fee rules.
3. Review whether the merchant offers a different currency at checkout and what rate it discloses.
4. Consider whether a temporary hold may affect available balance.
5. Compare the expected total, not only the headline exchange rate.
6. Check the posted transaction after settlement to understand the actual amount.

## Keep a record of recurring international costs

For business software and AI subscriptions, record the service, billing currency, transaction date, converted amount, and any visible fee. Reviewing these records over several months can reveal price increases or recurring costs that are easy to miss.

You can review [CBPayments](https://www.cbpayments.cc/) when researching virtual card options, but verify current exchange-rate practices, fees, supported currencies, and account terms before making a decision.

## FAQ

**Is the public exchange rate always the rate used for my card?** No. The final rate may depend on the network, provider, processing date, and transaction route.

**Should I always choose my home currency at checkout?** Not automatically. Compare the disclosed conversion rate and total with the alternative, because the merchant's conversion may include a markup.

**Does a pending amount show the final cost?** Not necessarily. Check the completed transaction after settlement.

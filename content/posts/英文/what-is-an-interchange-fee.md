# What Is an Interchange Fee? How Card Payment Costs Work

When a business accepts a card payment, the amount paid by the customer is not necessarily the amount the merchant ultimately receives. Payment processing involves several participants, and different fees may apply along the way.

One of these fees is the interchange fee. Although customers do not usually see it as a separate checkout charge, interchange is an important part of how card payment costs are structured.

Understanding interchange fees helps merchants compare payment providers and gives cardholders a clearer picture of the infrastructure behind everyday card transactions.

## What Is an Interchange Fee?

An interchange fee is a fee associated with a card transaction that is generally paid by the merchant's acquiring side to the card issuer, according to the applicable card-network rules and arrangements.

The issuer is the institution or program responsible for issuing the payment card. The acquirer provides the merchant with access to card acceptance and submits transactions into the relevant payment system.

Interchange is only one component of the overall cost of accepting a card payment. The merchant may also pay acquiring, processing, gateway, and other service fees.

The exact structure depends on the card network, transaction type, market, and payment agreement.

## How Does Interchange Work?

A simplified card payment involves several stages:

1. A customer presents a card or supported digital payment credential.
2. The merchant submits the transaction through its payment provider or acquirer.
3. The issuer evaluates the authorization request.
4. If the transaction is approved and subsequently processed, the relevant payment systems record and settle the transaction.
5. Interchange and other applicable fees are accounted for according to the relevant arrangements.

The customer generally pays the advertised purchase price, while the merchant receives the transaction proceeds after applicable fees and adjustments.

Interchange is not necessarily calculated or transferred as a separate visible charge for each customer at checkout.

## Who Pays the Interchange Fee?

In the typical merchant-acquiring model, interchange is charged to the acquiring side and forms part of the cost that the merchant bears for accepting the card.

The way the merchant sees this cost depends on its pricing agreement.

For example, a payment provider may offer a blended rate that combines multiple cost components into one percentage and fixed fee. Another provider may use interchange-plus pricing, showing interchange separately from the provider's markup and other charges.

The merchant's agreement determines how those costs are presented and billed.

## What Determines an Interchange Fee?

Interchange rates vary. Factors may include:

- **Card type:** Different card products may have different applicable rates.
- **Transaction environment:** Card-present and card-not-present transactions can be treated differently.
- **Merchant category:** The merchant's business classification may affect the applicable rate.
- **Transaction region:** Domestic and cross-border transactions may follow different arrangements.
- **Card and transaction data:** The transaction's characteristics and compliance with applicable requirements can influence classification.
- **Card-network rules:** Each network maintains its own fee schedules and eligibility conditions.

There is no single interchange rate that applies to every card payment. Published schedules and regulations also vary across countries and can change over time.

## Interchange vs. Payment Processing Fees

Interchange is often confused with the full fee charged by a payment provider. These costs are related, but they are not identical.

| Fee or cost | What it generally covers |
|---|---|
| Interchange | A transaction-related fee associated with the issuer and acquiring side |
| Card-network assessment | Network-related charges under the applicable rules |
| Acquirer markup | The acquirer's pricing for providing merchant card acceptance |
| Payment processor fee | Charges for transaction processing services, depending on the agreement |
| Gateway fee | Charges for services that transmit payment information between systems |
| Other service fees | Possible monthly, cross-border, dispute, or additional service charges |

The precise labels and billing structure differ by provider. Some providers combine several costs, so merchants should read the pricing schedule rather than assuming each line item has the same meaning everywhere.

## Blended Pricing vs. Interchange-Plus Pricing

Two common pricing approaches illustrate how interchange can appear on a merchant statement.

### Blended pricing

The provider charges a combined rate, such as a percentage of the transaction plus a fixed amount. This can make the statement easier to understand, but the merchant may have less visibility into each underlying cost component.

### Interchange-plus pricing

The provider charges the applicable interchange and other relevant costs, then adds a defined markup or service fee. This can provide more detail, although the statement may be more complex.

Neither pricing model is automatically best for every business. The better choice depends on transaction volume, average transaction value, card mix, provider terms, and how much cost transparency the business needs.

## Do Interchange Fees Affect Customers?

Interchange is generally a merchant-side payment cost rather than a separate fee directly displayed to the cardholder.

However, payment acceptance costs can form part of a business's overall operating expenses. Businesses decide how to price their products and services within the applicable legal and contractual requirements.

Customers should not assume that a specific checkout fee is an interchange fee. A fee displayed to the customer may have a different basis and should be explained by the merchant.

## Can Interchange Fees Change?

Yes. Applicable interchange schedules and requirements can change, and the rate for a particular transaction may differ depending on its characteristics.

Merchants should review updates from their payment providers and card networks when evaluating acceptance costs. They should also compare the complete pricing agreement, including other transaction and service charges, rather than focusing on one fee in isolation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is interchange the same as a card processing fee?

No. Interchange is one component of card acceptance costs. A merchant's total processing bill may include network assessments, provider markups, gateway charges, and other fees.

### Does every card transaction have the same interchange fee?

No. Rates depend on the applicable card network, market, transaction type, card product, and other eligibility criteria.

### Do cardholders pay interchange directly?

Typically, interchange is charged on the merchant-acquiring side rather than appearing as a separate line item paid directly by the cardholder at checkout.

### How can a merchant compare interchange costs?

Review the pricing model, statement details, transaction mix, and applicable fee schedules. Compare total effective costs across providers and make sure additional service charges are included in the comparison.

## Final Thoughts

Interchange fees are part of the economics of card acceptance. They connect the issuing and acquiring sides of a payment transaction and form one element of the cost a merchant pays to accept cards.

For businesses, understanding interchange helps make provider pricing easier to evaluate. The most useful comparison considers the full payment agreement rather than treating interchange as the only cost involved.

How to Review Virtual Card Expenses Before the End of Each Month

Monthly expense reviews are an important part of managing online payments. Businesses that use multiple virtual cards may have transactions from software providers, cloud platforms, advertising services, online tools, and other digital merchants throughout the month.

Without a regular review, small recurring charges can go unnoticed, unexpected transactions may be missed, and unused subscriptions can continue generating costs.

A structured month-end review can help businesses understand where money was spent, identify unusual transactions, and prepare more accurate records for the next billing period.

This guide explains how to review virtual card expenses at the end of each month.

Why a Monthly Virtual Card Review Matters

A month-end review provides an opportunity to look at the complete picture rather than individual transactions.

Businesses can use the review to determine:

- How much was spent

- Which categories consumed the most money

- Which subscriptions renewed

- Whether any payments failed

- Whether unexpected charges occurred

- Which services are no longer needed

- Whether spending changed significantly from the previous month

Regular reviews can also make annual financial analysis easier because monthly records are already organized.

Start With a Complete Transaction List

The first step is to collect all transactions associated with the business's virtual cards during the month.

Depending on the payment system, transactions may include:

- Completed payments

- Pending transactions

- Reversed transactions

- Refunds

- Recurring charges

- Failed payments

Do not immediately treat every transaction as a final expense.

Transaction status matters when calculating the actual amount spent.

Separate Completed and Pending Transactions

Pending transactions may temporarily reduce available funds without representing a completed purchase.

For example, a merchant may place an authorization hold before finalizing a transaction.

During a month-end review, separate:

Completed transactions

Payments that have been finalized.

Pending transactions

Payments that have not yet been completed.

Reversed transactions

Authorizations or payments that have been reversed.

Refunds

Amounts returned by merchants.

This creates a more accurate view of actual spending.

Group Expenses by Category

Once transactions have been reviewed, group them into useful categories.

For example:

- Software

- Cloud services

- Advertising

- Online shopping

- Business services

- Subscriptions

- Travel

- Education

- Project expenses

The categories should reflect the company's actual spending structure.

Avoid creating too many categories if they do not provide meaningful information.

Compare Spending With the Previous Month

Month-to-month comparisons can reveal important changes.

For example:

Previous month: $2,000

Current month: $2,700

A 35% increase may deserve further investigation.

Possible reasons include:

- New software

- Increased advertising

- Higher cloud usage

- New employees

- Additional project costs

- Price increases

- Currency changes

The purpose of the comparison is not simply to identify increases, but to understand why they occurred.

Review Recurring Payments

Recurring payments deserve particular attention during a month-end review.

Check for:

- Monthly software

- Cloud subscriptions

- Hosting services

- Communication tools

- AI services

- Marketing platforms

- Online memberships

Ask whether each service is still being used.

A recurring charge that has become unnecessary can continue indefinitely if nobody reviews it.

Check Upcoming Renewals

The end of the month is also a good time to look ahead.

Identify subscriptions that will renew during the next month.

For each one, check:

- Renewal date

- Expected amount

- Payment method

- Number of users

- Business purpose

- Responsible person

This gives the business time to make changes before the charge occurs.

Review Annual Subscriptions

Annual subscriptions deserve additional attention because they may generate larger charges.

If a yearly renewal is approaching, review:

- Current usage

- Number of users

- Current pricing

- Alternative services

- Business value

- Cancellation requirements

An annual renewal should ideally be a deliberate decision rather than an automatic event that nobody notices.

Identify Unused Services

A month-end review can reveal subscriptions that are rarely used.

Possible warning signs include:

- No recent activity

- Few active users

- Duplicate functionality

- Completed projects

- Teams moving to another platform

Not every low-usage service should automatically be cancelled.

Some tools may be needed only occasionally.

However, low usage is a good reason to review the subscription.

Check for Duplicate Expenses

Businesses often use several services with overlapping functions.

For example, two departments may pay for different project management tools.

During the review, compare software by:

- Purpose

- Features

- Number of users

- Cost

- Actual usage

If two services perform similar functions, management can decide whether both are necessary.

Review Virtual Card Usage by Department

If virtual cards are organized by department, compare spending across teams.

For example:

Marketing

Software and advertising expenses.

Development

Cloud and technical tools.

Sales

CRM and sales platforms.

Operations

Administrative and workflow software.

Large changes between departments may reveal new business requirements or expenses that need additional review.

Review Spending by Project

Project-based businesses can also analyze virtual card expenses by project.

For each project, review:

- Software costs

- Cloud expenses

- Advertising

- Online services

- Other project-related payments

This can help businesses understand the actual cost of completing a project.

Check for Unexpected Charges

Unexpected transactions should be investigated before the month is closed.

Possible explanations include:

- Price changes

- Additional users

- Usage-based billing

- Automatic upgrades

- Foreign currency conversion

- Duplicate transactions

- Unrecognized merchants

Do not automatically assume that an unfamiliar transaction is fraudulent. First confirm the merchant, service, amount, and transaction details.

If the charge still cannot be explained, follow the appropriate dispute or security process provided by the card issuer.

Review Failed Payments

Failed transactions can be just as important as successful payments.

A declined subscription payment may eventually cause a service interruption.

Common causes include:

- Insufficient available balance

- Incorrect card details

- Expired card

- Spending limits

- Billing address mismatch

- Merchant restrictions

- Security verification

- Regional requirements

Record important failed payments and investigate them before they become operational problems.

Check International Transactions

Businesses that make international online payments should review currency and conversion details.

Important information may include:

- Original transaction currency

- Final account currency

- Exchange rate

- Conversion amount

- Additional fees, if applicable

International transaction costs can vary depending on the card provider, merchant, currency, and transaction structure.

Review Spending Limits

If virtual cards have spending limits, compare the limits with actual monthly usage.

A limit that is consistently reached may indicate that it needs to be reviewed.

On the other hand, a limit that is much higher than actual spending may provide less control than necessary.

The appropriate limit depends on the business and the card provider's available controls.

Check Card Expiration Information

Businesses using multiple virtual cards should also review card status.

If a card is approaching expiration, check which subscriptions or merchants depend on it.

Updating payment information before an important renewal can reduce the chance of a failed recurring payment.

Review Refunds and Reversals

Refunds and reversed transactions should be matched against the original payments when possible.

For example:

Original payment: $200

Refund: $200

The business should confirm that the refund has actually been received.

This is particularly important when a service has been cancelled or a transaction was disputed.

Keep Monthly Records

After completing the review, save a summary of the month's activity.

A basic monthly record might include:

- Total spending

- Software expenses

- Subscription expenses

- Advertising expenses

- Cloud expenses

- Refunds

- Failed payments

- Unusual transactions

- Upcoming renewals

Keeping these records makes future comparisons easier.

Use a Simple Month-End Checklist

A practical checklist can include:

Transaction Review

Check completed, pending, reversed, and refunded transactions.

Expense Categorization

Assign transactions to appropriate categories.

Subscription Review

Identify recurring services and upcoming renewals.

Department Review

Compare spending across teams.

Project Review

Allocate relevant expenses to projects.

Security Review

Investigate unexplained transactions.

Payment Review

Check failed payments and card status.

Budget Review

Compare actual spending with expectations.

This checklist can be adapted to the size of the business.

Avoid Making the Process Too Complicated

A month-end review should provide useful information without creating excessive administrative work.

Small businesses may only need:

- Transaction list

- Expense categories

- Subscription list

- Renewal dates

- Monthly totals

Larger companies may require more detailed department, project, and accounting information.

The process should match the organization's needs.

Common Month-End Review Mistakes

Looking only at successful payments

Pending and reversed transactions can affect the actual spending picture.

Ignoring small recurring charges

Several small subscriptions can add up.

Reviewing only the current month

Comparing previous periods helps identify spending trends.

Forgetting upcoming renewals

A future annual renewal can create a significant expense.

Not investigating unexpected changes

Large increases should have an identifiable reason.

Not updating records

A review is less useful if the underlying records remain outdated.

A Practical Monthly Workflow

A business can complete its review using seven steps.

Step 1: Collect transaction data

Gather activity from all relevant virtual cards.

Step 2: Check transaction status

Separate completed, pending, reversed, and refunded transactions.

Step 3: Categorize expenses

Group transactions by business purpose.

Step 4: Review recurring payments

Check subscriptions and upcoming renewals.

Step 5: Compare spending

Compare the current month with previous periods and expectations.

Step 6: Investigate unusual activity

Review unexpected charges, failed payments, and significant changes.

Step 7: Record the results

Save a simple summary for future reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should virtual card expenses be reviewed?

A monthly review is a practical approach for many businesses. Companies with high transaction volumes may need more frequent checks.

What should be checked at the end of the month?

Review transaction status, expense categories, recurring payments, refunds, failed payments, unusual charges, upcoming renewals, and spending trends.

Should pending transactions be counted as expenses?

Not necessarily. Pending transactions may not become final charges, so they should generally be reviewed separately until completed.

How can businesses identify unnecessary subscriptions?

Compare each subscription's cost with actual usage, number of users, business purpose, and available alternatives.

Can virtual cards make monthly expense reviews easier?

They can provide additional separation between categories, departments, or projects, which may make transaction classification easier.

Conclusion

A regular month-end review can make virtual card expenses much easier to understand.

By separating transaction statuses, categorizing expenses, reviewing recurring payments, checking upcoming renewals, comparing monthly spending, and investigating unusual activity, businesses can maintain better visibility over their online payments.

Virtual cards can support this process by creating clearer separation between different types of expenses, but the most important factor is consistency.

A simple monthly review performed every month is often more valuable than a complicated system that is rarely maintained.
